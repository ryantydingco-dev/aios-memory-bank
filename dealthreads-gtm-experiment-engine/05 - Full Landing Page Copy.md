## SECTION 3 — The Mechanism (the experiment loop)

### Section heading

**A GTM Experiment Engine — not a freelancer, not a course, not a blast tool.**

### Sub-line

The thing you actually buy is a loop. It runs every week, and every week it gets a little sharper. Here's the whole machine:

**1. Context**
We build deep understanding of your offer, your best clients, your objections, and the signals that mark a good-fit account. This is the part most outbound skips — and the reason most outbound is generic.

**2. Account universe**
We build and enrich your account universe (your real TAM), then score it by fit signals. Every account has a *reason* it's on the list — not "we scraped a job title."

**3. Experiment design**
Each week we design 3–5 experiments: distinct combinations of angle × segment × message, each with a hypothesis we state *before* it ships.

**4. Human-approved execution**
You approve the list and the messages. Then we run LinkedIn connections, DMs, personalized Looms, and structured follow-up on cadence. Nothing un-approved ever reaches a prospect.

**5. Measure**
We track reply rate **and conversation quality** — every reply tagged as real interest, soft no, objection, not-a-fit, or ghost. So you know which segment, which angle, and which opener earned *actual conversations*, not just opens.

**6. Improve**
We kill what flopped, double down on what worked, and write next week's plan from the evidence. Week 5 beats week 1 by design.

> **The durable edge is the loop plus conversation-quality measurement — not how many messages we can fire.** Volume is a commodity, and post-2024 it's a liability. Compounding learning is the moat.

## SECTION 5 — The LinkedIn + Loom + Follow-up Workflow

### Section heading

**The wedge: LinkedIn + a 60–90s Loom + structured follow-up.**

### Sub-line

This is the exact motion Ryan runs live on his own company. It's warm, peer-to-peer, and it's the opposite of a cold-email blast. Here's what a single account actually goes through:

**Touch 1 — Connection request (LinkedIn)**
A personalized connection request from a real profile, referencing the specific signal that flagged this account. No "I help companies like yours scale." A reason you're reaching out *to them*.

**Touch 2 — The DM + Loom (the differentiator)**
Once connected, a short message plus a personalized 60–90 second Loom. The Loom names their situation, the pain hypothesis, and one concrete idea. Most people send a wall of text; a real human on video saying something specific is what earns the reply.

**Touches 3–5 — Structured follow-up (2–3 touches)**
Value-add follow-up, not "just bumping this." A relevant resource, a sharper angle, a second proof point. **The follow-up is where most replies actually live** — and it's the part founders skip because they get busy.

**Reply triage → conversation-quality scoring**
Every reply gets tagged. Real interest routes to a clean CRM task so you can take the call. Soft no's and objections feed back into next week's experiments. Nothing warm goes cold.

**Tracked end to end:**
Invites sent · accept rate · DM reply rate · Loom sends and responses · follow-up reply rate · and the conversation-quality tag on every single reply.

> Email is available as a low-volume, reputation-safe secondary channel if you want it. But LinkedIn + Loom is the wedge — because it's where a peer-to-peer first touch actually belongs.

## SECTION 9 — FAQ

**How is this different from an "AI automation agency"?**
Most of those optimize for *sends* — more emails, more volume, more automation. We optimize for *qualified conversations and weekly learning*. The product is a human-approved experiment loop with conversation-quality measurement, run LinkedIn + Loom-first. Volume isn't the goal; sharper targeting every week is.

**Do you guarantee meetings or revenue?**
No — and be skeptical of anyone who does. Cold outbound runs roughly 1–5% reply rates and hundreds of touches per meeting. We commit to the machine, the weekly activity, and the learning. The pilot's soft activity floor means if we don't run the agreed experiments and touches in a month, that month is credited. We sell better economics and faster learning, not a number we can't control.

**Isn't cold email basically dead now?**
Mass cold email is — which is the point. Since February 2024, Google and Yahoo cap spam complaints at 0.3% and flag bulk senders above ~5,000/day, so spray-and-pray now damages your own domain. Our wedge is the opposite: low-volume, high-relevance, human-QA'd outreach, LinkedIn + Loom-first. Email is an optional, low-volume secondary channel only.

**How much of my time does this take?**
Roughly 30–60 minutes a week. You approve the ICP and account list, green-light messages in a fast approval lane, and record batched Looms (about 30 minutes, and Ryan can run the Looms during the pilot). Then you take the good calls. The engine does the rest.

**Why LinkedIn and Loom instead of just email?**
Because for high-ticket B2B services, a peer-to-peer first touch belongs where your buyer already operates — and a real human on a 60–90 second video saying something specific about *their* situation earns replies that a templated email never will. It's warmer, it's harder to ignore, and it doesn't risk your sender reputation.

**Do you close the deals for me?**
No. We create and warm qualified conversations, triage replies, score quality, and hand you clean follow-up tasks. You run the sales calls and close — because nobody sells your high-ticket offer better than you do.

**What do I need to have ready to start?**
A real offer with happy clients, a starting idea of your ICP (we'll sharpen it), your LinkedIn profile to send from (Sales Navigator ideally), about 30 minutes to record Looms, a fast approval lane, and either your CRM or a willingness to use a shared tracking sheet. One 60-minute kickoff and we're live in week one.

**Who is this NOT for?**
Pre-offer founders with no proof yet, anyone who wants volume at any cost, anyone unwilling to approve messages or appear on a short Loom, and transactional or PLG motions where 1:1 outbound is the wrong tool. If you want a blast machine, we're the wrong call — on purpose.
