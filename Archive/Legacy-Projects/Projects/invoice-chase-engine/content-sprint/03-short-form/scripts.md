# Shorts/Reels Scripts

## 1. QuickBooks was not the problem

**Hook:** QuickBooks was not the problem. The follow-up was.

**Spoken script:**

A 25-year business had QuickBooks and still had $650K sitting in open invoices.

That sounds like a software problem, but it was not.

The invoices were being sent.

The issue was what happened after that.

The owner was still emailing clients one by one, trying to remember who needed a reminder, who promised to pay, and who had gone quiet.

So I connected QuickBooks to a Claude-powered follow-up workflow and helped create reminder sequences that made the chase more consistent.

That workflow helped recover $650K in open invoices over 90 days.

Now I am testing whether this can become a boring little AI service.

**On-screen text:**

QuickBooks tracked the invoices.

The follow-up leaked.

**Caption:**

The boring AI business might be hiding after the invoice gets sent.

## 2. How $650K got stuck

**Hook:** This is how $650K gets stuck in open invoices.

**Spoken script:**

It usually does not happen in one dramatic moment.

It happens quietly.

An invoice gets sent.

No one confirms the right person received it.

There is no pre-due reminder.

The due date passes.

Someone says, "I'll pay next week."

Nobody tracks that promise.

Then the invoice is 30, 60, 90 days old, and the owner is personally chasing payment from their inbox.

That was the real problem behind the $650K recovery workflow I helped build.

Not "AI replaces accounting."

More like: "AI helps make the boring follow-up actually happen."

**On-screen text:**

Invoice sent

No confirmation

No reminder

No promise-to-pay tracking

Cash gets stuck

**Caption:**

AR leakage is usually boring. That is why it is easy to miss.

## 3. The after-the-invoice problem

**Hook:** The invoice process does not end when you send the invoice.

**Spoken script:**

Most businesses treat invoice sending like the finish line.

But the real workflow starts after the invoice goes out.

Did the right billing contact get it?

Was receipt confirmed?

Was there a reminder before it was due?

What happens at 3, 7, and 14 days overdue?

Who tracks disputes?

Who escalates when a client goes quiet?

Who sends the owner a weekly report?

That is the after-the-invoice workflow.

And for a lot of businesses, it is trapped in someone's inbox.

That is the part I am trying to turn into a repeatable AI service.

**On-screen text:**

The leak happens after the invoice.

**Caption:**

QuickBooks can track the invoice. Someone still has to run the follow-up system.

## 4. Why owners hate chasing payments

**Hook:** Business owners hate chasing payments because it feels weird.

**Spoken script:**

Nobody starts a business because they are excited to send, "Hey, just checking in on invoice 1047."

It is awkward.

It is repetitive.

It is easy to delay.

And if the owner is the one doing it manually, it competes with actual work.

That was the pain in the invoice recovery workflow I helped build.

The goal was not to make some aggressive collections machine.

The goal was to make polite, consistent follow-up happen without everything living in the owner's head.

That is a much better use of AI than making another chatbot nobody asked for.

**On-screen text:**

Awkward

Repetitive

Easy to delay

Perfect for a system

**Caption:**

AI is useful when the workflow is boring, repetitive, and already costing attention.
