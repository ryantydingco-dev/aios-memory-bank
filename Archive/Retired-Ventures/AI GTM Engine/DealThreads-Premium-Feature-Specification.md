### 1. FEATURE OVERVIEW

**Feature Name**: DealThreads Signal Engine & ICP Gap Campaigns (Premium Tier)

**Feature Category**: Enhancement

**Priority Level**: Should-Have

**Target Release**: Premium Beta v0.1 (upsell to the 5 managed core beta clients; CSM-operated managed service; 4-6 week build on top of Core v1.1)

**Problem Statement**:
The DealThreads Core ($2,500/mo) Conversational Buyer-Profile Widget is reactive: it only acts on inbound visitors who choose to start a conversation, and it goes quiet the moment the visitor leaves. The Premium tier ($5,000/mo, an upsell that **layers on top of** — never replaces — the $2,500/mo core) closes two gaps that the core cannot touch and that today cost a mid-market B2B team running $10K+ deals roughly $280K/yr of headcount.

- **No continuous read on target accounts / ICP.** When a tracked account raises funding, swaps a tech-stack tool DealThreads sells against, churns a leader, or surges hiring, the rep finds out late (if at all), so the timely, dated "why now" that wins meetings is lost. The four things worth watching — `funding_round`, `tech_stack_change`, `leadership_change`, `hiring_surge` — are exactly the four canonical SIGNAL TYPES Premium monitors. To cover this job today a RevOps leader hires a **marketing analyst (~$160K/yr)** to assemble account-intelligence and pipeline reporting by hand, stale by the time it ships.
- **High-fit accounts leak away with no proactive follow-up.** The highest-fit accounts that visited the site, were enriched, or match the ICP but never converted are never re-engaged, and the gap between the tenant's *stated* ICP and *who actually buys* is never quantified or worked. To cover this job today a RevOps leader hires a **sales coordinator (~$120K/yr)** to build and police targeted outbound lists.

There is no managed, productized service that fuses continuous account-signal monitoring, a synthesized monthly intelligence report, ICP gap analysis, and triggered, compliant outbound campaigns into one motion for the mid-market. The Premium tier turns DealThreads from reactive intake into a proactive **signal engine** built on two bundled pillars: **Pillar 1 — Continuous Intelligence & Monthly Reports** (near-real-time `AccountSignal` alerts plus a monthly `IntelligenceReport`, the productized replacement for the marketing analyst) and **Pillar 2 — ICP Gap Campaigns** (an `IcpGapAnalysis` that quantifies the gap, feeding CSM-managed, email-first outbound campaigns — including signal-triggered campaigns keyed to a `funding_round` or `tech_stack_change` — the productized replacement for the sales coordinator). The decisive bet is the **Synergy Loop**: a detected signal feeds a timely, relevant campaign; the campaign's outcomes feed the next monthly report; the report informs the next cycle's targeting — signal → campaign → report → signal — producing booked meetings and campaign-sourced pipeline the tenant cannot get from inbound alone, at a price ($60K/yr) that displaces ~$280K/yr of headcount. A standalone Advanced Intelligence Reports SKU exists at ~$1,000/mo (Pillar 1 only); the $5,000/mo tier bundles both pillars. In the beta this is a **managed service** — the DealThreads CSM configures `SignalSubscription`s, builds and launches `Campaign`s, and reviews the `IntelligenceReport` live with the client (consistent with the manual 5-beta-client model).

**Success Criteria**:
Targets are scoped to the 4-6 week managed-beta window across the 5 core beta tenants. All metric names are the canonical metricCatalog names.

1. **Premium upsold and operated for ≥ 3 of the 5 managed core beta clients.** For each, target accounts / ICP loaded and approved, `SignalSubscription` configured (`PUT /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/signal-subscriptions`), the signal pipeline live, and the first monthly `IntelligenceReport` delivered on its canonical delivery day (1st business day of the month, for the prior calendar month) with all five canonical sections in order — Lead Trends, ICP Gaps, Response-Time Performance, Account Signal Movements, Campaign Outcomes. (*Premium Pilot-to-Paid Conversion ≥ 50%; ≥ 3 of 5.*)
2. **Signal pipeline accuracy and freshness hold the bar.** ≥ 80% **Signal Precision** (sampled, human-verified true positives among surfaced `funding_round | tech_stack_change | leadership_change | hiring_surge` signals); **Signal Freshness SLA Attainment** ≥ 95% — a detected source event reaches a rep/RevOps alert within **≤ 24 hours p95** (distinct from the core Speed-to-Profile ≤ 2 min p95). The recency guardrail is never violated: **no `UNDATED` (or otherwise stale) signal is ever promoted to a fresh trigger**, and dates are never fabricated (recency-guardrail compliance = 100%).
3. **ICP Gap Campaigns produce measurable booked meetings with zero compliance incidents.** ≥ 1 CSM-managed, email-first campaign per active premium tenant runs to completion, achieving ≥ 5% **Signal-to-Meeting Conversion** on signal-triggered targets and ≥ 2% **Campaign Meeting Booked Rate** across all contacted targets, with **ZERO suppression violations** (no email ever sent to an `active_deal`, `open_opportunity`, `unsubscribed`, or `competitor` account) and every send honoring CAN-SPAM + GDPR (recorded lawful basis, visible/working one-click unsubscribe, suppression). **Deliverability & Compliance Health:** hard-bounce < 3%, spam-complaint < 0.1%, all sends inside the per-domain and per-tenant daily send caps.

He monitors live campaigns (`GET /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/campaigns/{campaignId}`): per-target statuses including `suppressed` + `suppression_reason`, the `CampaignActivity` rollup (sent/opened/replied/bounced/unsubscribed), deliverability health, and first-touch/last-touch attributed pipeline. He receives ops alerts when bounce/spam-complaint thresholds (hard-bounce < 3%, spam-complaint < 0.1%) or a send cap are breached (a cap breach pauses sending) and works with the CSM and Nadia to tune targeting and suppression — the productized version of the manual coordinator motion.

**Edge Cases**:

1. **Signal false positive (mis-detected funding round).** A source mis-reports a `funding_round` for a tracked account. Because every `AccountSignal` carries a `source`, `signal_date` + `signal_date_source`, 0–1 confidence, and recency tier, a low-confidence or `UNDATED` candidate is down-ranked and never fires an alert. If it did surface and is wrong, Marcus or Devin calls `POST /api/v1/signals/{signalId}/dismiss` (`new|alerted → dismissed`), emitting an AnalyticsEvent that feeds Signal Precision (≥ 80% bar) so the source/threshold can be tuned. **A dismissed or undated signal must never enroll a `signal_triggered` `CampaignTarget`.**
2. **A month with no notable signals — report still useful.** In a quiet month with few or zero new `AccountSignal`s, the `IntelligenceReport` still generates on the canonical delivery day with all five sections in order. The Account Signal Movements section reports "no material signals this cycle" with the comparison baseline, and the other four sections (Lead Trends, ICP Gaps, Response-Time Performance, Campaign Outcomes) carry the value. **The report is never skipped, reordered, or reduced below five sections** (100% of active premium tenants receive a report every cycle).
3. **ICP gap analysis with too little converted data.** A newly onboarded tenant has few `qualified`/closed-won Leads, so "who actually converted" is statistically thin. The `IcpGapAnalysis` flags low sample size in its `gap_summary_json`, falls back to the EnrichmentProfile-based `high_fit` segment (ICP Fit Score ≥ 75) to populate dormant-high-fit targets, and presents the target list as provisional. **It must not fabricate a converted profile or assert an unsupported gap;** Nadia and the CSM review before any campaign is built from it.
4. **Campaign targeting an account already in an active deal — must be suppressed.** When a `CampaignTarget` matches an `active_deal` or `open_opportunity` (derived from `CrmSyncRecord`/CRM state), suppression sets `status = suppressed` with `suppression_reason ∈ active_deal | open_opportunity` **before any send** — it is never contacted. This also guards the synergy path: a `funding_round` on an account already in flight still alerts the rep but is suppressed from outbound. **Zero suppression violations is a hard acceptance bar, not a tolerance.**
5. **Email bounce / spam-complaint handling.** ESP delivery webhooks record `bounced`/`unsubscribed` as `CampaignActivity` and update the `CampaignTarget` status. The campaign-send worker enforces deliverability thresholds (hard-bounce < 3%, spam-complaint < 0.1%) and the per-domain/per-tenant daily send caps; **breaching a cap pauses sending and exceeding a threshold raises an ops alert to Devin**, protecting the sending domain's reputation (Deliverability & Compliance Health).
6. **Unsubscribe mid-campaign.** A contact clicks the one-click unsubscribe while a campaign is `active`. The ESP unsubscribe webhook writes a `CampaignActivity` (`unsubscribed`), moves the `CampaignTarget` to `unsubscribed`, and **automatically creates a `SuppressionEntry` (`reason = unsubscribed`)** so that contact is suppressed across all current and future campaigns for the tenant — **suppressed immediately on webhook receipt, and in all cases within 24 hours**, so no further email is sent, satisfying CAN-SPAM + GDPR.
7. **LinkedIn/ads requested as a campaign step.** Any LinkedIn or ads step is surfaced only as a **human-handoff task** (`CampaignActivity.activity_type = handoff_task_logged`); DealThreads **never** sends LinkedIn messages or spends ad budget programmatically. `Campaign.channel` is `email`.
8. **CSM-approval gate (managed service).** A `Campaign` is created in `draft`; it can only begin sending after explicit CSM approval moves it `draft|scheduled → active` (`POST /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/campaigns/{campaignId}/launch`). **There is no unattended self-serve authoring-and-send in v1** — every launch is CSM-approved.

### 4. FUNCTIONAL REQUIREMENTS

#### Core Functionality

The Premium tier — **DealThreads Signal Engine & ICP Gap Campaigns** — decomposes into five core functions layered on (never replacing) the DealThreads Core Conversational Buyer-Profile Widget. Each is specified with **Input**, **Processing**, **Output**, **Validation**, and **Edge Cases**. Every function operates within row-level `tenant_id` scoping plus Postgres RLS in PostgreSQL 16, runs on NestJS (TypeScript) on Render, and uses BullMQ + Redis for all async/scheduled work — the inherited stack, with **no new scheduler or stack technology introduced**. New work is driven by four BullMQ workers — `signal-poller`, `icp-gap-recompute`, `campaign-send`, and `report-generator` — and two new swappable adapter interfaces, `SignalProvider` (Crunchbase / BuiltWith / the secondary enrichment provider) and `EmailProvider` (Postmark / SendGrid), following the existing core enrichment- and CRM-adapter patterns. Premium is a **managed service** in beta: every `SignalSubscription`, `Campaign`, and `IntelligenceReport` is configured, launched, or reviewed by the DealThreads CSM — there is no full self-serve campaign builder and no unattended autonomous sending in v1.

**Account-key note.** Throughout this section `account_id` is the tenant-scoped tracked-account/domain key shared by `AccountSignal`, `CampaignTarget`, and the `SignalSubscription.target_accounts_json` list. It is the join key between a detected signal and the account it concerns, and (where a core `Lead` exists for that account) `CampaignTarget.lead_profile_id` links the target back to the inherited `Lead` so the two tiers stay consistent.

**Confidence-and-recency note.** Every `AccountSignal` carries BOTH a 0–1 numeric `confidence` AND a `recency_tier ∈ FRESH (≤30d, ×1.0) | RECENT (31–90d, ×0.7) | AGING (91–365d, cap MEDIUM) | STALE (>365d, cap LOW) | UNDATED (datable but no date, cap LOW) | N/A (structural)`. This reuses the inherited Recency Guardrail / Signal Taxonomy verbatim. A datable signal that cannot be timestamped is stored as `recency_tier = UNDATED`, down-ranked, and **NEVER promoted to a fresh trigger** — dates are never fabricated (Business Rule 3).

**Signal Freshness clock note.** The Signal Freshness SLA is measured on a single canonical clock: **from the source's published event date to the `signal_alerted` `AnalyticsEvent`** (≤ 24 h p95). The internal `detected_at → signal_alerted` interval (the pipeline latency from detection to alert, on the order of seconds-to-minutes) is tracked **separately as an internal pipeline metric** and is NOT the freshness gate; the freshness SLA the rep relies on is source-published-date → alert. This clock is used identically wherever the SLA is stated (Business Rule 4).

##### 4.1 Function (a): Continuous Signal Monitoring & Detection Engine

The proactive engine that turns each tenant's tracked accounts / ICP into a stream of dated, scored `AccountSignal` rows across the four canonical SIGNAL TYPES, and fires near-real-time rep/RevOps alerts within the Signal Freshness SLA. This is the atomic unit of Pillar 1 and the trigger source for signal-triggered campaigns (Pillar 2). Implemented by the `signal-poller` BullMQ repeatable job calling the `SignalProvider` adapter, with the LLM adapter (Claude `claude-sonnet`) summarizing the dated `why_now`.

**Input**
- The tenant's active `SignalSubscription` (configured by the CSM via `PUT /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/signal-subscriptions`): `enabled_signal_types` (a subset of `funding_round | tech_stack_change | leadership_change | hiring_surge`), `target_accounts_json` (account list / ICP filter), `min_confidence`, and `alert_channel ∈ slack | email`.
- Provider pulls per source on the `signal-poller` cadence: **Crunchbase** (primary) + the secondary enrichment provider (corroboration/fallback) for `funding_round`; **BuiltWith** technographics plus deltas against the stored core `EnrichmentProfile.result_json` tech stack for `tech_stack_change`; **the secondary enrichment provider** people/headcount data plus a news/press source for `leadership_change` and `hiring_surge`.
- Operator dismissals via `POST /api/v1/signals/{signalId}/dismiss` (feedback into Signal Precision).

**Processing**
1. The `signal-poller` runs as a BullMQ repeatable/cron job over Redis at a per-source cadence (hourly-to-daily) sized to meet the **≤ 24 h p95 Signal Freshness SLA** measured from the source's published event date to the `signal_alerted` `AnalyticsEvent` (Business Rule 4); it polls only the accounts in each active subscription's `target_accounts_json`.
2. Each provider candidate is normalized through the `SignalProvider` adapter into a candidate `AccountSignal`: `signal_type`, `source` (e.g. `crunchbase | builtwith | secondary_provider | clearbit | news`), `detail_json` (round/amount, added/removed tool, role change, hiring counts), and a 0–1 `confidence`.
3. **Dating + recency classification (mandatory):** the engine extracts `signal_date` and `signal_date_source` (the exact phrase the date came from). It then assigns `recency_tier` per the inherited guardrail. A datable signal with no sourceable date is set to `recency_tier = UNDATED`; structural facts are `N/A`. Dates are **never fabricated** (Business Rule 3).
4. Claude (`claude-sonnet`) synthesizes the **dated** `why_now` prose anchored to `signal_date` (e.g. "Raised a $12M Series A on 2026-05-18 — expansion budget unlocked"). If the anchor is undated, confidence is capped at LOW and the signal is not alert-eligible as a fresh trigger.
5. The candidate is persisted as an `AccountSignal` (`status = new`) and an `AnalyticsEvent` `event_type = signal_detected` is emitted (`properties_json` carries `account_signal_id`).
6. **Alert gate (explicit freshness boundary):** a signal is alert-eligible only if `confidence ≥ SignalSubscription.min_confidence` AND its `recency_tier` clears the freshness bar. The freshness bar is **FRESH or RECENT only**: `AGING` (cap MEDIUM), `STALE` (cap LOW), and `UNDATED` (cap LOW) signals **never fire a fresh alert** (and never auto-enroll a `signal_triggered` campaign) — they are persisted, down-ranked, and surfaced for human review, but the recency guardrail forbids asserting them as a fresh "why now" (Business Rule 2). Eligible signals fire a near-real-time alert to the subscription's `alert_channel` (Slack/email, the same notification channel as core routing) carrying the dated `why_now`, `confidence`, `recency_tier`, and a deep link to the account; `status → alerted` and an `AnalyticsEvent` `event_type = signal_alerted` is written (this event timestamp is the endpoint of the canonical freshness clock).
7. `funding_round` and `tech_stack_change` signals at/above threshold **and** in a FRESH/RECENT tier are additionally evaluated against any active `signal_triggered` `Campaign` for auto-enrollment (handed to §4.4).

**Output**
- An `AccountSignal` with `signal_type`, `source`, `signal_date` + `signal_date_source`, `recency_tier`, 0–1 `confidence`, dated `why_now`, `detail_json`, and `status (new | alerted | dismissed | actioned)`, readable via `GET /api/v1/accounts/{accountId}/signals`.
- A rep/RevOps alert (Marcus) on the configured channel and the paired `signal_detected` / `signal_alerted` `AnalyticsEvent` rows that feed Signal Precision, Signal Freshness SLA Attainment (measured source-published-date → `signal_alerted`), and the report's Account Signal Movements section.

**Validation**
- `signal_type` MUST be one of exactly `funding_round | tech_stack_change | leadership_change | hiring_surge`; any other value is rejected (no new categories in v1).
- Every datable signal MUST carry `signal_date` + `signal_date_source` or be stored `recency_tier = UNDATED`; a `confidence` outside `[0,1]` is rejected.
- No alert fires below `SignalSubscription.min_confidence`, and **no alert fires for an `AGING`, `STALE`, or `UNDATED` anchor** — only FRESH/RECENT clears the freshness bar (recency-guardrail compliance = 100%, a hard bar).
- A `tech_stack_change` is asserted only against a dated delta (BuiltWith observation date or the stored `EnrichmentProfile` snapshot date); an undated tool list cannot assert a "change."
- Signal Freshness SLA Attainment is computed on the canonical clock (source published event date → `signal_alerted` `AnalyticsEvent`), not the internal `detected_at → signal_alerted` pipeline interval.
- Tenant isolation: `GET /api/v1/accounts/{accountId}/signals` returns only `account_signals` rows owned by the caller's tenant (RLS-enforced).

**Edge Cases**
- *Undated "news" (the systemic guardrail case)*: a funding/award/hire announcement with no sourceable date is stored `UNDATED`, capped LOW, never alerted as fresh, and surfaced for human re-verification — the fix is never to guess a date.
- *Bare-year date*: a low-precision date (e.g. "in 2025") can never be FRESH/RECENT; it is capped at AGING and therefore never fires a fresh alert.
- *Provider outage / 429 / 5xx*: retried with exponential backoff up to the bounded ceiling on BullMQ; on exhaustion the source is skipped that cycle and an ops alert is raised — the SLA clock and precision are measured only over signals that were actually retrievable.
- *Duplicate signal*: the same source event re-observed re-attaches to the existing `AccountSignal` (idempotent on `tenant_id + account_id + signal_type + signal_date + source`) rather than spawning a duplicate or re-alerting.
- *False positive*: an operator dismissal (`/signals/{signalId}/dismiss`) transitions `new|alerted → dismissed`, emits an `AnalyticsEvent`, and feeds the sampled Signal Precision measure so the source/threshold can be tuned.
- *Stale-cache technographic*: a `tech_stack_change` derived from an `EnrichmentProfile` older than its TTL is re-corroborated before alerting, never asserted on stale technographics.

##### 4.2 Function (b): Monthly Intelligence Report Generation

The synthesized, CSM-reviewed monthly report that is Pillar 1's headline deliverable and the productized replacement for the marketing-analyst output (also sold standalone as the ~$1,000/mo Advanced Intelligence Reports SKU; the $5,000/mo Premium tier bundles it with Pillar 2). Generated by the `report-generator` BullMQ job on the canonical cadence and rendered as a branded PDF plus an in-console dashboard view.

**Input**
- The scheduled monthly trigger (BullMQ cron firing on the canonical delivery day) or a CSM-initiated `POST /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/reports/generate` (returns `202` with a job id).
- Source data for the prior calendar-month window `[period_start, period_end]`: PostgreSQL materialized-view rollups over `analytics_events` / `leads` / `campaign_activities` (extending the core `mv_funnel_daily` pattern), the latest `IcpGapAnalysis`, this cycle's `Campaign` / `CampaignTarget` / `CampaignActivity` outcomes, and `AccountSignal` movements.

**Processing**
1. The `report-generator` worker assembles the **five canonical sections in order**: **(1) Lead Trends, (2) ICP Gaps, (3) Response-Time Performance, (4) Account Signal Movements, (5) Campaign Outcomes** (Business Rule 8). Section names and order are fixed.
2. Heavy aggregations are served from the premium materialized-view rollups (lead/funnel trends, response-time inputs from `signal_alerted`/`campaign_*` events and core routing, signal-movement counts by `signal_type` and `recency_tier`, campaign-outcome funnels and first-touch/last-touch attributed pipeline).
3. Claude (`claude-sonnet`), behind the existing LLM adapter, synthesizes the narrative prose for each of the five sections from the structured rollups; every numeric claim in prose is backed by a rollup value (no fabricated figures).
4. Server-side PDF generation (headless-Chromium/Puppeteer rendering a branded HTML template, or an equivalent HTML-to-PDF service) produces the branded PDF; the same `sections_json` renders the in-console dashboard view in the React 18 SPA.
5. The `IntelligenceReport` is persisted (`cadence = monthly`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `sections_json`, `pdf_url`, `delivered_on`, `status = generating → delivered`) linked to the `icp_gap_analysis_id` it drew from, and surfaced via `GET /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/reports` and `.../reports/{reportId}`.
6. On the canonical delivery day the report is delivered to Maya/Nadia; when the CSM reviews it live, `status → reviewed` (feeding Monthly Report Open/Review Rate).

**Output**
- An `IntelligenceReport` with all five sections as structured `sections_json` plus a branded `pdf_url`, delivered on the canonical day and listed/openable in the console; campaign outcomes in this cycle feed the next cycle (the Synergy Loop).

**Validation**
- `cadence` MUST be `monthly`; the report MUST contain exactly the five canonical sections in the canonical order — generation fails (and ops is alerted) rather than delivering a report missing or reordering a section.
- `period_start`/`period_end` MUST bound the prior calendar month; rollups outside the window are excluded.
- Every figure rendered in prose MUST trace to a materialized-rollup value; campaign pipeline/revenue figures MUST use **first-touch + last-touch attribution only** (Business Rule 7).
- Tenant isolation: a report aggregates and exposes only the caller-tenant's `analytics_events`, `leads`, and campaign data (RLS-enforced).

**Edge Cases**
- *No campaigns / no signals this cycle*: the relevant section renders an explicit "no activity in period" state with the zero counts — the section is never omitted and the five-section contract holds.
- *Rollup refresh lag at generation time*: the worker refreshes the premium materialized views before aggregating; if a refresh fails it retries on BullMQ and holds the report in `generating` rather than delivering stale numbers.
- *PDF render failure*: retried with backoff; on exhaustion the dashboard view (from the same `sections_json`) is still available and an ops alert is raised so the CSM can regenerate via `.../reports/generate`.
- *First active month*: trends with no prior month render current-period values with prior-period marked "n/a (first cycle)" rather than a fabricated baseline.
- *CSM regeneration*: a re-run for the same period supersedes the prior `IntelligenceReport` for that window deterministically (same inputs → same structured sections) so report changes are attributable.

##### 4.3 Function (c): ICP Gap Analysis

The computed snapshot that quantifies the gap between the tenant's defined ICP and (a) who actually converted and (b) dormant high-fit accounts (high_fit accounts that visited/were enriched but never became an opportunity), producing the ranked target list that seeds ICP Gap Campaigns. Reviewed by Nadia and the CSM. Implemented by the `icp-gap-recompute` BullMQ job reading inherited `Lead`, `EnrichmentProfile`, and `CrmSyncRecord` data read-only.

**Input**
- `POST /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/icp-gap-analysis/recompute` (returns `202` with a job id, mirroring the core enrich pattern), or the snapshot read via `GET /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/icp-gap-analysis`.
- Read-only inputs: the tenant's stated ICP definition; core `Lead` rows (status enum `qualified | disqualified | incomplete | needs_review`, `icp_score` 0–100, `score_json.segment ∈ high_fit | nurture | low_fit`); `EnrichmentProfile.result_json` (firmographic/technographic basis for fit); `CrmSyncRecord` (opportunity/closed-won linkage).

**Processing**
1. The `icp-gap-recompute` worker snapshots `icp_definition_json` and computes three sets: **converted** (`qualified` Leads / closed-won read via `CrmSyncRecord`), **dormant high-fit** (`segment = high_fit`, i.e. ICP Fit Score ≥ 75, that visited and/or were enriched but never became an opportunity), and the **quantified gap** (`gap_summary_json` — e.g. industries/company-sizes overrepresented in the stated ICP but absent from won).
2. It ranks the dormant/unidentified high-fit accounts into a `target_list_json` (fit strength, recency of activity, and any open `AccountSignal` boosting "why now").
3. The snapshot is persisted as an `IcpGapAnalysis` (`status = draft`) and surfaced to Nadia/CSM for review; on review `status → reviewed`, and when a campaign consumes it `status → used`.

**Output**
- An `IcpGapAnalysis` with `converted_segment_json`, `dormant_high_fit_json`, `gap_summary_json`, and a ranked `target_list_json` that seeds gap_driven campaigns and feeds the report's ICP Gaps section.

**Validation**
- "Converted" is derived only from the inherited `Lead.status` enum (`qualified`) and CRM closed-won — the enum is consumed verbatim and never extended/altered by Premium.
- "High-fit" / "dormant high-fit" uses the inherited segment enum and thresholds verbatim: `high_fit` = ICP Fit Score **≥ 75**; an account scored < 75 can never appear as dormant high-fit.
- Reads are strictly read-only against core entities; the analysis never mutates a `Lead`, `EnrichmentProfile`, or `CrmSyncRecord`.
- Tenant isolation: only the caller-tenant's Leads/profiles/CRM records are read (RLS-enforced).

**Edge Cases**
- *Sparse history (early beta tenant)*: with too few converted Leads to quantify a stable gap, the snapshot flags low confidence and leans on `dormant_high_fit_json` rather than asserting a spurious ICP-vs-won gap.
- *Account already an open opportunity*: an account with an open opportunity (via `CrmSyncRecord`) is excluded from the dormant set and pre-marked for suppression so it can never seed a campaign target (defense-in-depth ahead of §4.4 suppression).
- *Account both converted and re-engaged*: a previously-won account is classified as converted, not dormant, to avoid re-targeting a customer as a net-new gap.
- *ICP redefinition mid-cycle*: each snapshot pins `icp_definition_json` at compute time so gap changes are attributable to the ICP version that produced them.

##### 4.4 Function (d): Campaign Orchestration (Email-First Send & Sequencing)

CSM-managed, **email-first** outbound that works the dormant/unidentified high-fit accounts (gap_driven) or auto-enrolls accounts on a detected `funding_round` / `tech_stack_change` (signal_triggered), enforcing **mandatory suppression**, dual daily send caps, and CAN-SPAM + GDPR compliance on every send. LinkedIn/ads are a documented **human-handoff task list, never auto-sent**. Implemented by the `campaign-send` BullMQ worker calling the `EmailProvider` adapter (Postmark/SendGrid).

**Input**
- `POST /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/campaigns` to create a `Campaign` (`type = gap_driven` from an `IcpGapAnalysis` target list, or `type = signal_triggered` keyed to a `trigger_signal_type`), in `status = draft`.
- `POST /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/campaigns/{campaignId}/launch` — explicit CSM approval gating `draft|scheduled → active`.
- For signal_triggered campaigns: matching `funding_round` / `tech_stack_change` `AccountSignal` rows from §4.1 that are at/above threshold AND in a FRESH/RECENT tier.
- ESP delivery webhooks (opens/replies/bounces/unsubscribes) and the tenant's `SuppressionEntry` set, `send_window_json`, and `compliance_json` (lawful basis, unsubscribe footer, sender identity).

**Processing**
1. **Materialize + suppress (before any send):** on create, the server materializes `CampaignTarget` rows from the gap target list or matching signals and checks **every** target against the tenant's `SuppressionEntry` set. A match sets `status = suppressed` with `suppression_reason ∈ active_deal | open_opportunity | unsubscribed | competitor`; suppressed targets are **never sent to** (Business Rule 5). Surviving targets are `queued`.
2. **CSM review + launch:** Devin reviews suppression coverage and deliverability via `GET .../campaigns/{campaignId}` and `GET .../suppression`; the campaign sends only after the CSM moves it `draft → active` via `.../launch` (`Campaign.status ∈ draft | scheduled | active | paused | completed | archived`; no unattended self-serve send — Business Rule 9).
3. **Send + sequence (active only):** the `campaign-send` worker sends email-first via the `EmailProvider` within the `send_window_json`, enforcing the **per-sending-domain daily cap AND per-tenant daily cap** (conservative beta defaults, on the order of 50/day per mailbox-domain; the per-tenant cap is a tunable per-tenant default of the same conservative order) and CAN-SPAM + GDPR (recorded lawful basis, visible one-click unsubscribe, suppression). A target that has been sent to moves to `status = contacted`; each send sets `CampaignTarget.first_touch_at` (first send) / `last_touch_at` (latest touch) and writes a `CampaignActivity` (`activity_type ∈ sent | opened | replied | bounced | unsubscribed | handoff_task_logged`) plus an `AnalyticsEvent` `event_type = campaign_sent`.
4. **Re-suppress at send time:** immediately before each individual send the worker re-checks the target against the live `SuppressionEntry` set (CRM state can change after enrollment); a newly-matched target flips to `suppressed` and is dropped from the send — defense against emailing an account that became an active deal post-enrollment.
5. **Caps + deliverability gates:** breaching a domain or tenant daily cap **pauses** sending (excess re-queues for the next window, never silently dropped); crossing the hard-bounce (< 3%) or spam-complaint (< 0.1%) threshold raises an ops alert to Devin and pauses the affected domain (Business Rules 5–6).
6. **Human-handoff steps:** any LinkedIn/ads step is surfaced as a task and recorded only as `CampaignActivity.activity_type = handoff_task_logged` — DealThreads never sends a LinkedIn message or spends ad budget programmatically (Business Rule 4).
7. **Inbound webhooks advance state:** a reply → `status = replied` (+ `campaign_replied` `AnalyticsEvent`); a bounce → `bounced`; an unsubscribe → `unsubscribed` AND creation of a `SuppressionEntry (reason = unsubscribed)`. The unsubscribe is **suppressed immediately on webhook receipt** — the contact is removed from all sending the moment the event is processed, and in all cases the suppression is in force within 24 hours of receipt (Business Rule 6) — so no later send in this or any future campaign can re-contact them.

**Output**
- A `Campaign` with materialized `CampaignTarget` rows (per-target `status ∈ queued | suppressed | contacted | replied | meeting_booked | bounced | unsubscribed`), an append-only `CampaignActivity` log, deliverability-health rollups, and (suppressed targets) the matching `suppression_reason` — all visible on Devin's `GET .../campaigns/{campaignId}` monitoring surface.

**Validation**
- `Campaign.channel` MUST be `email`; LinkedIn/ads may appear only as `handoff_task_logged` activity — a programmatic LinkedIn/ads send is a defect.
- Sending occurs **only** in `status = active`; a `draft|scheduled|paused|completed|archived` campaign cannot send. The `draft → active` transition requires CSM approval via `.../launch`.
- **Zero suppression violations** is a hard acceptance bar: no email is ever sent to an `active_deal`, `open_opportunity`, `unsubscribed`, or `competitor` account; both enrollment-time and send-time checks must pass.
- Every send MUST carry a working one-click unsubscribe and a recorded lawful basis (CAN-SPAM + GDPR); a send lacking either is blocked.
- Per-domain and per-tenant daily caps are real gates in the worker, not advisory; a send that would breach a cap does not go out.
- Tenant isolation: a campaign can only enroll/contact the caller-tenant's accounts and reads only that tenant's suppression set (RLS-enforced).

**Edge Cases**
- *Account becomes an active deal after enrollment*: caught by the send-time re-suppression in step 4; the target flips to `suppressed (active_deal)` and is never contacted.
- *Signal-triggered enrollment on an undated/low-confidence signal*: blocked — only `funding_round`/`tech_stack_change` signals at/above threshold with a FRESH/RECENT anchor auto-enroll; AGING/STALE/UNDATED signals never auto-enroll (consistent with §4.1 and Business Rule 2).
- *Cap reached mid-send*: sending pauses and the remaining queue rolls to the next send window; no target is dropped and the domain reputation is protected.
- *Hard-bounce or spam spike*: the affected sending domain is paused and Devin is alerted before the thresholds are exceeded across the cohort.
- *Duplicate enrollment*: an account already an active `CampaignTarget` in the campaign is not enrolled twice (idempotent on `tenant_id + campaign_id + account_id`).
- *Unsubscribe mid-sequence*: the unsubscribe is suppressed immediately on webhook receipt (in all cases within the Business Rule 6 24-hour window), the target → `unsubscribed`, and a permanent `SuppressionEntry` is created so no later campaign can re-contact them.
- *Competitor account surfaced as high-fit*: blocked by the `competitor` `SuppressionEntry` (sourced from the tenant competitor list) regardless of fit score.

##### 4.5 Function (e): Attribution & Campaign Analytics

The instrumentation layer that links a campaign-sourced reply/meeting to a CRM opportunity and attributes pipeline/revenue under **first-touch + last-touch only**, feeding the report's Campaign Outcomes section and the Premium business metrics. Reuses the inherited `AnalyticsEvent` stream and `CrmSyncRecord` CRM linkage (HubSpot fully exercised first; Salesforce via the existing adapter).

**Input**
- `CampaignActivity` events (`sent | opened | replied | bounced | unsubscribed | handoff_task_logged`) and the paired `AnalyticsEvent` rows (`campaign_sent | campaign_replied | meeting_booked`).
- A positive reply / booked meeting on a `CampaignTarget`, and the inherited `CrmSyncRecord` linkage to the CRM opportunity (`crm_opportunity_id`).

**Processing**
1. On a positive reply / meeting booked, the target advances to `status = meeting_booked`, `crm_opportunity_id` is set via the inherited idempotent HubSpot-first / Salesforce-second CRM adapter, and an `AnalyticsEvent` `event_type = meeting_booked` is emitted.
2. **Attribution (first-touch + last-touch ONLY):** campaign-sourced pipeline (open-opportunity value) and revenue (closed-won, read back through `CrmSyncRecord`) are credited to the target's `first_touch_at` and `last_touch_at` campaign touches. **No** multi-touch / weighted / U-shaped / W-shaped / time-decay / data-driven attribution exists in v1 (Business Rule 7).
3. **Funnel and conversion metrics are computed over `CampaignTarget` states, all using a `contacted`-based denominator so the rates are mutually consistent:** **Signal-to-Meeting Conversion** = of **contacted** signal-triggered targets (`source_signal_id` set AND `status` reached `contacted` — i.e. passed suppression and were sent to; suppressed/queued targets are excluded), the percentage reaching `meeting_booked` (target ≥ 5%); **Campaign Meeting Booked Rate** = of all **contacted** targets, the percentage reaching `meeting_booked` (target ≥ 2%); **ICP Gap Closure Rate** over the `IcpGapAnalysis` target list; **Deliverability & Compliance Health** (hard-bounce < 3%, spam-complaint < 0.1%, unsubscribe rate monitored, **zero suppression violations**) over `CampaignActivity`.
4. These rollups feed the monthly report's Campaign Outcomes (and Response-Time Performance) sections and the `GET .../campaigns/{campaignId}` monitoring view.

**Output**
- A `CampaignTarget` advanced to `meeting_booked` and linked to `crm_opportunity_id`; first-touch/last-touch attributed pipeline and revenue; and the computed campaign metrics surfaced to Devin/Nadia/CSM and the report.

**Validation**
- Attribution MUST use first-touch + last-touch only; any other model is a defect.
- A `meeting_booked` target MUST carry both `first_touch_at`/`last_touch_at` and a resolved `crm_opportunity_id` (no unattributed conversions).
- Signal-to-Meeting Conversion and Campaign Meeting Booked Rate MUST both be computed over a **contacted** denominator; suppressed and queued targets are excluded from both so the two rates share a consistent base.
- CRM linkage is idempotent (stored external object IDs); re-running linkage updates, never duplicates, an opportunity.
- Suppression Violation count surfaced by Deliverability & Compliance Health MUST be **0** for the cohort to pass acceptance.
- Tenant isolation: attribution joins only the caller-tenant's `analytics_events`, `campaign_*`, and `crm_sync_records` (RLS-enforced).

**Edge Cases**
- *Reply without a booked meeting*: the target rests at `replied`; it counts toward reply/engagement metrics but not Signal-to-Meeting / Campaign Meeting Booked until it reaches `meeting_booked`.
- *Account already in CRM (dedupe hit)*: linkage updates the existing opportunity in place via the inherited adapter rather than creating a duplicate.
- *Single touch only*: if first and last touch are the same send, both `first_touch_at` and `last_touch_at` point to that touch (still valid attribution).
- *Opportunity later closed-lost*: pipeline credit is removed and revenue credit is never asserted; only closed-won read back via `CrmSyncRecord` counts as Campaign-Sourced Revenue.
- *Handoff-task-sourced meeting*: a meeting booked off a logged LinkedIn/ads handoff still attributes via the recorded `handoff_task_logged` touch under the same first/last-touch rule.

#### Business Rules

**Rule 1 — Premium bundle and price (verbatim).** Premium is the **$5,000/mo** tier, an **upsell on the $2,500/mo core** (it layers on, never replaces it), that **BUNDLES both pillars** — Pillar 1 (Continuous Intelligence & Monthly Reports) + Pillar 2 (ICP Gap Campaigns). A standalone **Advanced Intelligence Reports SKU exists at ~$1,000/mo** (Pillar 1 only); the $5,000/mo tier includes both. These figures are used verbatim; Premium is never described as reports-only or as replacing the core.

**Rule 2 — Signal confidence threshold + recency before a signal is shown or triggers a campaign.** Every `AccountSignal` carries a 0–1 `confidence` AND a `recency_tier ∈ FRESH (≤30d, ×1.0) | RECENT (31–90d, ×0.7) | AGING (91–365d, cap MEDIUM) | STALE (>365d, cap LOW) | UNDATED (datable, no date, cap LOW) | N/A (structural)`. A signal alerts a rep, or auto-enrolls a `signal_triggered` campaign target, **only** when `confidence ≥ SignalSubscription.min_confidence` AND the anchor is **FRESH or RECENT**. The freshness boundary is explicit and testable: **AGING, STALE, and UNDATED signals never fire a fresh alert and never auto-enroll a campaign** — they are persisted, down-ranked, and surfaced for human review only. A datable signal that cannot be timestamped is `UNDATED`, down-ranked, and **NEVER promoted to a fresh trigger**; the dated `why_now` anchor is mandatory and dates are never fabricated. Recency-guardrail compliance (no UNDATED/AGING/STALE signal surfaced as fresh) is a 100% hard bar.

**Rule 3 — Anti-fabrication of signal dates.** The fix for an undated datable signal is **never** to guess a plausible date. It is marked `UNDATED`, down-ranked, and routed to human re-verification; "I don't know when this happened" is a first-class, visible state, not a silent fresh trigger. Only the four canonical SIGNAL TYPES (`funding_round | tech_stack_change | leadership_change | hiring_surge`) exist in v1 — no patents, web-traffic spikes, or intent keywords.

**Rule 4 — Email-first channel rule (no automated LinkedIn/ads/SMS).** Campaigns are **email-first** in beta. `Campaign.channel = email`. LinkedIn and ads are a documented **human-handoff task list, NEVER automated/sent by DealThreads** — they appear only as `CampaignActivity.activity_type = handoff_task_logged`. The platform never spends paid-ads budget via Google/LinkedIn Ads APIs, never sends LinkedIn messages programmatically, and runs no SMS/cold-call automation.

**Rule 5 — Mandatory suppression (the compliance backbone; zero violations).** DealThreads **NEVER** emails an account/contact that is in an `active_deal`, an `open_opportunity`, is `unsubscribed`, or is a known `competitor`. Every `CampaignTarget` is checked against the tenant's `SuppressionEntry` set **before any send** (at enrollment) AND re-checked immediately before each individual send; a match sets `status = suppressed` with `suppression_reason ∈ active_deal | open_opportunity | unsubscribed | competitor`, and a suppressed target is never sent to. `active_deal`/`open_opportunity` entries are derived from `CrmSyncRecord`/CRM state; `unsubscribed` from ESP unsubscribe webhooks; `competitor` from the tenant competitor list. **Zero suppression violations is a hard acceptance bar** — a send to any suppressed account is a defect, not a tolerance.

**Rule 6 — Outbound compliance: send caps, deliverability, and unsubscribe window (CAN-SPAM + GDPR).** Outbound email is governed by a **per-sending-domain daily send cap AND a per-tenant daily send cap** (both configured per tenant; conservative beta defaults on the order of 50/day per mailbox-domain to protect reputation, with the per-tenant cap a tunable per-tenant default of the same conservative order — there is no canonical fixed per-tenant number, only the dual-cap structure), enforced as real gates in the `campaign-send` worker — breaching a cap **pauses** sending (excess re-queues, never drops). Every send honors **CAN-SPAM + GDPR**: a recorded **lawful basis**, a **visible one-click unsubscribe**, and suppression. An unsubscribe is **suppressed immediately on webhook receipt** — the target moves to `unsubscribed` and a permanent `SuppressionEntry (reason = unsubscribed)` is created the moment the event is processed, and in all cases the suppression is in force **within 24 hours of receipt** — so no future campaign can re-contact them. Deliverability thresholds: **hard-bounce < 3%**, **spam-complaint < 0.1%** (both hard gates); the **unsubscribe rate is monitored and reported but carries no hard numeric gate** in beta. Exceeding the hard-bounce or spam-complaint threshold raises an ops alert to Devin and pauses the affected domain.

**Rule 7 — First-touch + last-touch attribution only.** Campaign-sourced pipeline and revenue use **first-touch + last-touch attribution ONLY** in beta. `CampaignTarget` records `first_touch_at` and `last_touch_at`, joined to the CRM opportunity via the inherited `CrmSyncRecord` linkage. There is **no** multi-touch / weighted / U-shaped / W-shaped / time-decay / data-driven attribution in v1.

**Rule 8 — Monthly report cadence and canonical sections.** The `IntelligenceReport` cadence is **monthly** (not weekly/quarterly), delivered on a fixed canonical delivery day (the 1st business day of each month, for the prior calendar month). Every report contains **exactly these five sections, in order: (1) Lead Trends, (2) ICP Gaps, (3) Response-Time Performance, (4) Account Signal Movements, (5) Campaign Outcomes** — delivered as a branded PDF + in-console dashboard view, then reviewed live with the CSM. Section names and order are canonical and never altered.

**Rule 9 — Managed-service gate (CSM-in-the-loop, no autonomous send).** In the beta, Premium is a **managed service**: the DealThreads CSM configures `SignalSubscription`s, builds and launches `Campaign`s, and reviews the monthly `IntelligenceReport` live with the client. Every campaign launch is **CSM-approved** (the `draft → active` transition is gated behind `.../campaigns/{campaignId}/launch`); there is no full self-serve campaign builder and no unattended autonomous sending in v1. The console exposes setup/monitoring/review surfaces, not authoring-and-send without the CSM in the loop.

**Rule 10 — Premium reuses core consent, enrichment, and CRM data read-only and consistently.** Premium **reuses** inherited core entities verbatim and does not redefine them: it reads `Lead` (status enum `qualified | disqualified | incomplete | needs_review`; segment enum `high_fit | nurture | low_fit`; ICP Fit Score 0–100), `EnrichmentProfile`, `CrmSyncRecord`, and the existing `ConsentRecord`/consent posture read-only as the conversion/fit ground truth and the suppression source. - **PII handling & encryption at rest**: `CampaignTarget.contact_json` (name, work email, role) is **application-layer (column/field-level) encrypted** — envelope-encrypted with a per-record data key wrapped by the managed KMS — exactly like the core `Lead.contactJson` pattern; it is decrypted in the application layer only for an authenticated, tenant-scoped principal, so the contact PII used for outbound is never persisted in plaintext.
- **Reuse of core consent / no over-collection**: Premium reads inherited `Lead`, `EnrichmentProfile`, `CrmSyncRecord`, and the existing consent posture read-only; it never re-collects data the core already holds and never overrides core consent. Outbound email rests on its own recorded **CAN-SPAM + GDPR lawful basis** per `Campaign.compliance_json`.
- **Outbound compliance as a data-protection control**: every send carries a recorded lawful basis and a working one-click unsubscribe; an unsubscribe is **suppressed immediately on webhook receipt** — and in all cases within **24 hours of receipt** — permanently suppressing the contact via a `SuppressionEntry (reason = unsubscribed)`. The dual per-domain/per-tenant daily send caps and the hard-bounce (< 3%) / spam-complaint (< 0.1%) thresholds are enforced as real gates in the `campaign-send` worker; the unsubscribe rate is monitored and reported but carries no hard numeric gate in beta (Business Rule 6).
- **Suppression integrity (zero violations)**: the canonical suppression rule (`active_deal | open_opportunity | unsubscribed | competitor`) is enforced at enrollment AND re-checked immediately before each send; **zero suppression violations** is a hard bar — a send to any suppressed account is a defect (Business Rule 5).
- **Encryption (transit & secrets)**: data in transit over TLS 1.2+ via Cloudflare; data at rest encrypted in managed PostgreSQL/Redis on Render with the application-layer PII encryption layered on top; all signal-provider, ESP, and CRM secrets held in the managed secrets store, never in the database in plaintext.
- **Erasure consistency with core**: a validated deletion/erasure request that touches a contact also purges/anonymizes premium PII — `campaign_targets.contact_json` is crypto-shredded by destroying its per-record key, and an `unsubscribed`/erased contact yields a permanent suppression so it can never be re-contacted; premium `analytics_events` rows are anonymized in place (stripping/hashing `lead_profile_id`/identity in `properties_json`) while preserving the funnel counts the metrics depend on.

**Audit Trail**
- The following are audit-logged: every `SignalSubscription` create/update (which CSM/admin, what was enabled, when); every signal `dismiss` action and its reason (feeding Signal Precision); every `IcpGapAnalysis` recompute (the pinned `icp_definition_json` version); every `Campaign` create and `launch` approval (which CSM principal, which version, when — the managed-service gate); every `SuppressionEntry` add and its `source` (`crm_sync | unsubscribe_event | manual | competitor_list`); every `CampaignActivity` (`sent | opened | replied | bounced | unsubscribed | handoff_task_logged`) and its `esp_message_id`; and every `IntelligenceReport` generation/delivery/review transition. Send-cap, bounce/spam-threshold, suppression-conflict, and signal-source-outage events raise operational alerts to Devin. The `signal_detected` / `signal_alerted` / `campaign_sent` / `campaign_replied` / `meeting_booked` `AnalyticsEvent` rows give an append-only, tenant-scoped audit of the full signal → campaign → meeting loop.

### 6. TECHNICAL SPECIFICATIONS

This section specifies the engineering build for the **DealThreads Signal Engine & ICP Gap Campaigns (Premium Tier)** — the $5,000/mo upsell on the $2,500/mo DealThreads Core that **bundles** Pillar 1 (Continuous Intelligence & Monthly Reports) and Pillar 2 (ICP Gap Campaigns). A standalone Advanced Intelligence Reports SKU exists at ~$1,000/mo (Pillar 1 only); the $5,000/mo tier includes both. Premium **layers on** the Core (it never replaces it) and reuses the Core stack, entities, endpoints, and API conventions verbatim: NestJS (TypeScript) on Render, PostgreSQL 16 as system of record with `tenant_id` row-level scoping + Postgres RLS, BullMQ + Redis for async jobs and scheduling, Claude `claude-sonnet` behind the existing LLM adapter, Clearbit→the secondary enrichment provider enrichment, the HubSpot-first / Salesforce-second idempotent CRM adapter, and the React 18 + Vite operator/admin SPA. API base path `/api/v1`; admin surface under `/api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/...`; standard error envelope `{ "error": { "code": <HTTP>, "type": "<machine_code>", "message": "<human>", "details": [...] } }`; prefixed string PKs; `created_at timestamptz default now()` on every table. There is **no new visitor-facing UI** in Premium — every surface is internal/CSM-operated, consistent with the managed-service model (the DealThreads CSM configures `SignalSubscription`s, builds and launches `Campaign`s, and reviews the monthly `IntelligenceReport` live with the client; every campaign launch is CSM-approved and there is no unattended autonomous sending in v1).

**Canonical identifiers and enums used throughout this section** (reused verbatim from the kernel invariants — never redefined or renamed):

- **`signal_type` enum (the only four canonical SIGNAL TYPES in Premium v1):** `funding_round` | `tech_stack_change` | `leadership_change` | `hiring_surge`. `funding_round` and `tech_stack_change` are the **only two** that can trigger a `signal_triggered` campaign. No new categories (patents, web-traffic, intent keywords) in v1. Enforced by a Postgres CHECK on `account_signals.signal_type`.
- **`recency_tier` enum (inherited Recency Guardrail, reused verbatim):** `FRESH` (≤ 30d, ×1.0) | `RECENT` (31–90d, ×0.7) | `AGING` (91–365d, cap MEDIUM) | `STALE` (> 365d, cap LOW) | `UNDATED` (datable but no sourced date, cap LOW) | `N/A` (structural). Every datable `AccountSignal` **must** carry `signal_date` + `signal_date_source` or be stored as `UNDATED` and down-ranked — **a datable signal that cannot be timestamped is NEVER promoted to a fresh trigger, and dates are never fabricated.** `confidence` is numeric `0–1`.
- **`Campaign.status` enum:** `draft` | `scheduled` | `active` | `paused` | `completed` | `archived`. New campaigns are created in `draft`; CSM approval (launch) moves a reviewed campaign to `active`; **sending only occurs in `active`.**
- **`CampaignTarget.status` enum:** `queued` | `suppressed` | `contacted` | `replied` | `meeting_booked` | `bounced` | `unsubscribed`. `meeting_booked` is the conversion state metrics are computed against; a `suppressed` target carries a `suppression_reason` and is **never** sent to.
- **`CampaignActivity.activity_type` enum:** `sent` | `opened` | `replied` | `bounced` | `unsubscribed` | `handoff_task_logged`. LinkedIn/ads steps are **only ever** `handoff_task_logged` — DealThreads never sends LinkedIn messages or spends ad budget programmatically (email-first only; no SMS/cold-call automation).
- **`suppression_reason` / `SuppressionEntry.reason` enum:** `active_deal` | `open_opportunity` | `unsubscribed` | `competitor`. DealThreads **NEVER** emails an account/contact matching any of these; every `CampaignTarget` is checked against the tenant's `SuppressionEntry` set **before any send**. Zero suppression violations is a hard acceptance bar — a send to a suppressed account is a defect, not a tolerance.
- **Inherited `Lead.status` enum (REUSED VERBATIM, not extended):** `qualified` | `disqualified` | `incomplete` | `needs_review`. Premium reads `Lead` read-only: `qualified`/closed-won define "who actually converted"; `high_fit` `Lead`s that never became opportunities define dormant high-fit.
- **Inherited segment enum (REUSED VERBATIM):** machine values `high_fit` | `nurture` | `low_fit`, rendered **Hot/High-Fit** (ICP ≥ 75), **Warm/Nurture** (50–74), **Low-Fit** (< 50); ICP Fit Score is `0–100`. A **Dormant High-Fit Account** is specifically a `high_fit`-segment account (ICP ≥ 75) that visited/was enriched but never became an opportunity.
- **`Signal Freshness SLA` (Premium-specific, distinct from the Core's Speed-to-Profile ≤ 2 min p95):** a detected source event reaches a rep/RevOps alert within **≤ 24 hours p95**, measured from the source's published event date / `detected_at` to the `signal_alerted` `AnalyticsEvent`.
- **`Attribution model` (the only model in v1):** **first-touch + last-touch only**. `CampaignTarget.first_touch_at` and `last_touch_at` join to the CRM opportunity via the inherited `CrmSyncRecord` linkage. **No multi-touch / weighted / U-shaped / W-shaped / time-decay / data-driven attribution.**
- **Outbound send governance (canonical):** a **per-sending-domain daily send cap AND a per-tenant daily send cap**, both enforced as a **real gate** in the campaign-send worker. The only canonical numeric default is the **per-sending-domain cap, on the order of ~50/day per mailbox-domain** (a conservative beta default to protect reputation, tunable per tenant); the **per-tenant daily cap has no fixed canonical value — it is configured per tenant** (a conservative beta default that the CSM sets, illustrated as `200` in the endpoint examples below purely as a sample figure, not a canonical constant). Deliverability thresholds are **hard-bounce < 3%, spam-complaint < 0.1%**; every send carries a recorded lawful basis and a visible/working one-click unsubscribe (CAN-SPAM + GDPR). An unsubscribe is **suppressed immediately on ESP webhook receipt — and in all cases within 24 hours — after which no further email is ever sent** to that contact. Breaching either cap **pauses** sending; exceeding a bounce/spam threshold raises an ops alert to Devin.

##### API Endpoints

All Premium admin endpoints sit under `/api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/...`; the two rep-facing signal endpoints sit under `/api/v1/...`. All are tenant-scoped (row-level isolation + RLS) and audited where they mutate. Inherited Core endpoints (`/api/v1/leads/...`, `/api/v1/crm/sync`, `/api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/analytics/funnel`, etc.) are reused as-is and not re-specified here.

**1. `PUT /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/signal-subscriptions`** — create/update the tenant's `SignalSubscription`; this is what the CSM sets to turn the Continuous Intelligence pillar on for a tenant.

- *Request body:*
  ```json
  {
    "enabledSignalTypes": ["funding_round", "tech_stack_change", "leadership_change", "hiring_surge"],
    "targetAccounts": { "accountIds": ["acct_midco", "acct_northwind"], "icpFilter": { "industry": ["B2B SaaS"], "sizeMin": 100, "minIcpScore": 75 } },
    "minConfidence": 0.6,
    "alertChannel": "slack",
    "status": "active"
  }
  ```
- *Response (success `200`):*
  ```json
  { "id": "sub_19", "tenantId": "ten_001", "enabledSignalTypes": ["funding_round","tech_stack_change","leadership_change","hiring_surge"], "minConfidence": 0.6, "alertChannel": "slack", "status": "active", "updatedAt": "2026-06-01T17:00:00Z" }
  ```
- *Response (error):* `401 unauthorized`; `403 wrong_tenant`; `422 subscription_validation_error` (e.g. `enabledSignalTypes` contains a value outside the canonical four, or empty target definition).
- *Validation:* Admin/CSM role + tenant membership required. `enabledSignalTypes` **must** be a non-empty subset of `funding_round | tech_stack_change | leadership_change | hiring_surge` (any other value → `422`). `minConfidence` ∈ `[0,1]`. `alertChannel` ∈ `slack | email`. At least one of `accountIds`/`icpFilter` must be present. Idempotent upsert per `tenant_id` (one active subscription per tenant in v1); change is audited (who/when). Governs what the `signal-poller` looks for and what fires an alert.

**2. `GET /api/v1/accounts/{accountId}/signals`** — list detected `AccountSignal`s for a tracked account; powers the rep's account view and the deep link from a signal alert.

- *Request body:* None (path param + bearer/tenant auth). Optional query `signalType`, `minConfidence`, `status`.
- *Response (success `200`):*
  ```json
  {
    "accountId": "acct_midco",
    "signals": [
      { "id": "sig_4f2", "signalType": "funding_round", "source": "crunchbase",
        "signalDate": "2026-05-28", "signalDateSource": "crunchbase_press",
        "recencyTier": "FRESH", "confidence": 0.88,
        "whyNow": "MidCo raised a $24M Series B on 2026-05-28 (Crunchbase) — budget and headcount expansion likely.",
        "detail": { "round": "Series B", "amount": "$24M" }, "status": "alerted" },
      { "id": "sig_5a1", "signalType": "tech_stack_change", "source": "builtwith",
        "signalDate": null, "signalDateSource": null,
        "recencyTier": "UNDATED", "confidence": 0.41,
        "whyNow": "BuiltWith shows a competing form tool removed; date not sourced — not asserted as a fresh trigger.",
        "detail": { "removed": "Typeform" }, "status": "new" }

Every datable signal carries `signalDate` + `signalDateSource` **or** is returned as `recencyTier: "UNDATED"` with confidence capped LOW; an `UNDATED` signal is **never** represented as `FRESH`. `confidence` ∈ `[0,1]`; `signalType` is one of the canonical four.

**3. `POST /api/v1/signals/{signalId}/dismiss`** — mark an `AccountSignal` as dismissed (false positive / not relevant); feeds Signal Precision and source/threshold tuning. Used by Marcus/Devin.

- *Request body:* `{ "reason": "false_positive" }` (optional; `reason` ∈ `false_positive | not_relevant | already_known`).

- *Request body:* None. Optional query `analysisId` (defaults to latest).
- *Response (success `200`):*

  {
    "id": "gap_07", "tenantId": "ten_001", "generatedAt": "2026-05-31T02:00:00Z", "status": "reviewed",

- *Validation:* Tenant-scoped, read-only. "Who actually converted" is derived **read-only** from inherited `Lead`s (`qualified`/closed-won) and `CrmSyncRecord`s; "dormant high-fit" from `high_fit`-segment `Lead`s/`EnrichmentProfile`s that never became an opportunity. - *Request body:*
  ```json
  {

  ```

#### Database Changes

PostgreSQL 16 (managed on Render), the **same single schema** as the Core, **row-level tenant scoping** on every Premium table (`tenant_id` on all rows; enforced in the data layer and via Postgres RLS policies). **JSONB** holds provider payloads, signal detail, gap snapshots, report sections, and compliance config. All PKs are prefixed string IDs (e.g. `sig_4f2`, `cmp_22`). All tables carry `created_at timestamptz default now()`. `CampaignTarget.contact_json` PII is encrypted at the application layer using the Core's KMS-wrapped per-record key pattern (same as `Lead.contactJson`); ESP API keys live in the managed secrets store, never in Postgres.

**New Tables** — the **eight new kernel `dataEntities`** (names/key fields verbatim), all tenant-scoped with RLS, prefixed string IDs, JSONB payloads, and `created_at default now()`. They sit alongside the Core's existing tables. Their foreign keys fall into two groups: FKs **into inherited Core tables** (`tenants`, `leads`) and FKs **between the new Premium tables themselves** (`campaign_targets` → `account_signals`, `campaign_targets`/`campaigns` → `icp_gap_analyses`, `campaign_targets`/`campaign_activities` → `campaigns`, `campaign_activities` → `campaign_targets`):

- **`account_signals`** (`AccountSignal`) — `id (PK)`, `tenant_id (FK→tenants)`, `account_id`, `signal_type` (CHECK ∈ `funding_round | tech_stack_change | leadership_change | hiring_surge`), `source`, `signal_date`, `signal_date_source`, `recency_tier` (CHECK ∈ `FRESH | RECENT | AGING | STALE | UNDATED | N/A`), `confidence numeric` (CHECK `0 ≤ confidence ≤ 1`), `why_now text`, `detail_json jsonb`, `status` (CHECK ∈ `new | alerted | dismissed | actioned`), `detected_at`, `created_at`. A datable signal lacking a sourced date is stored `recency_tier = 'UNDATED'`; dates are never fabricated.
- **`signal_subscriptions`** (`SignalSubscription`) — `id (PK)`, `tenant_id (FK→tenants)`, `enabled_signal_types jsonb` (subset of the four), `target_accounts_json jsonb`, `min_confidence numeric`, `alert_channel` (CHECK ∈ `slack | email`), `status` (CHECK ∈ `active | paused`), `created_at`, `updated_at`.
- **`icp_gap_analyses`** (`IcpGapAnalysis`) — `id (PK)`, `tenant_id (FK→tenants)`, `generated_at`, `icp_definition_json jsonb`, `converted_segment_json jsonb`, `dormant_high_fit_json jsonb`, `gap_summary_json jsonb`, `target_list_json jsonb`, `status` (CHECK ∈ `draft | reviewed | used`), `created_at`.
- **`campaigns`** (`Campaign`) — `id (PK)`, `tenant_id (FK→tenants)`, `name`, `type` (CHECK ∈ `gap_driven | signal_triggered`), `trigger_signal_type` (nullable; CHECK ∈ `funding_round | tech_stack_change | leadership_change | hiring_surge`), `icp_gap_analysis_id (FK→icp_gap_analyses, nullable)`, `channel` (CHECK = `email`), `status` (CHECK ∈ `draft | scheduled | active | paused | completed | archived`), `send_window_json jsonb`, `compliance_json jsonb`, `created_by`, `created_at`, `updated_at`. A composite CHECK additionally enforces that `trigger_signal_type` is **non-null and ∈ `funding_round | tech_stack_change` when `type = 'signal_triggered'`, and `NULL` when `type = 'gap_driven'`** (see Schema Updates).
- **`campaign_targets`** (`CampaignTarget`) — `id (PK)`, `tenant_id (FK→tenants)`, `campaign_id (FK→campaigns)`, `account_id`, `contact_json jsonb` (application-layer encrypted), `source_signal_id (FK→account_signals, nullable)`, `lead_profile_id (FK→leads, nullable)`, `status` (CHECK ∈ `queued | suppressed | contacted | replied | meeting_booked | bounced | unsubscribed`), `suppression_reason` (nullable; CHECK ∈ `active_deal | open_opportunity | unsubscribed | competitor`), `first_touch_at`, `last_touch_at`, `crm_opportunity_id` (nullable), `created_at`.
- **`campaign_activities`** (`CampaignActivity`) — `id (PK)`, `tenant_id (FK→tenants)`, `campaign_id (FK→campaigns)`, `campaign_target_id (FK→campaign_targets)`, `activity_type` (CHECK ∈ `sent | opened | replied | bounced | unsubscribed | handoff_task_logged`), `channel` (CHECK ∈ `email | linkedin | ads`), `esp_message_id` (nullable), `detail_json jsonb`, `occurred_at`, `created_at`. Append-only (no UPDATE/DELETE via app), mirroring the `analytics_events` pattern.
- **`suppression_entries`** (`SuppressionEntry`) — `id (PK)`, `tenant_id (FK→tenants)`, `match_type` (CHECK ∈ `email | domain | account_id`), `match_value`, `reason` (CHECK ∈ `active_deal | open_opportunity | unsubscribed | competitor`), `source` (CHECK ∈ `crm_sync | unsubscribe_event | manual | competitor_list`), `expires_at` (nullable), `created_at`.
- **`intelligence_reports`** (`IntelligenceReport`) — `id (PK)`, `tenant_id (FK→tenants)`, `period_start`, `period_end`, `cadence` (CHECK = `monthly`), `delivered_on`, `sections_json jsonb` (the five canonical sections), `pdf_url`, `status` (CHECK ∈ `generating | delivered | reviewed`), `icp_gap_analysis_id (FK→icp_gap_analyses, nullable)`, `created_at`.

*Inherited Core tables reused read-only or for linkage (NOT redefined):* `leads` (conversion ground truth + dormant-high-fit source; `campaign_targets.lead_profile_id → leads`), `enrichment_profiles` (firmographic/technographic ICP-fit basis + `tech_stack_change` corroboration), `analytics_events` (Premium emits the new `event_type` values onto the same append-only stream), `crm_sync_records` (suppression source for `active_deal`/`open_opportunity` + `crm_opportunity_id` linkage), and `tenants` (root of isolation).

**Schema Updates**

- Add Postgres **CHECK constraints** on every enumerated Premium column listed above — most importantly `account_signals.signal_type` and `recency_tier`, `campaigns.status` and `type`/`trigger_signal_type`, `campaign_targets.status` and `suppression_reason`, `campaign_activities.activity_type`, and `suppression_entries.reason` — against the **canonical enums verbatim**, plus a `confidence` range CHECK (`0 ≤ confidence ≤ 1`) on `account_signals` and a `NOT NULL tenant_id` on every Premium table.
- Add a CHECK enforcing `campaigns.channel = 'email'` (email-first; LinkedIn/ads never a send channel) and a **single composite CHECK** that fully constrains `campaigns.trigger_signal_type` by `type`: it must be **non-null and ∈ `funding_round | tech_stack_change` when `type = 'signal_triggered'`** (only those two are valid triggers) **and must be `NULL` when `type = 'gap_driven'`** — so a `gap_driven` row can never carry `leadership_change`/`hiring_surge` (or any) trigger and a `signal_triggered` row can never omit one. Concretely: `CHECK ((type = 'signal_triggered' AND trigger_signal_type IN ('funding_round','tech_stack_change')) OR (type = 'gap_driven' AND trigger_signal_type IS NULL))`.
- Add a uniqueness guard on `signal_subscriptions (tenant_id)` so there is one active subscription per tenant in v1, and a uniqueness guard on `suppression_entries (tenant_id, match_type, match_value)` to back the idempotent suppression upsert.
- Add Postgres **RLS policies** keyed on the request's `tenant_id` claim for all eight Premium tables so cross-tenant reads are impossible even on a query bug — identical posture to the Core.
- Provision new **materialized views** extending the Core's `mv_funnel_daily` pattern — `mv_signal_movements_daily`, `mv_campaign_funnel_daily`, and `mv_response_time_daily` over `account_signals` / `campaign_activities` / `analytics_events` / `leads` — to serve the `IntelligenceReport`'s five sections (including the descriptive Response-Time Performance section) and the campaign/deliverability rollups cheaply, refreshed on a schedule.

**Indexes**

- `account_signals (tenant_id, account_id, detected_at DESC)` — the rep account view and the alert deep link; plus `(tenant_id, signal_type, recency_tier)` for the `IntelligenceDashboard` filter and the Account Signal Movements rollup.
- `account_signals (tenant_id, status)` — surfacing `new`/`alerted` signals and dismissal/precision sampling.
- `signal_subscriptions (tenant_id)` **UNIQUE** — one subscription per tenant; drives the `signal-poller`.
- `icp_gap_analyses (tenant_id, generated_at DESC)` — latest-snapshot resolution for `GET .../icp-gap-analysis`.
- `campaigns (tenant_id, status)` — campaign listing and the `campaign-send` worker's "active campaigns" scan.
- `campaign_targets (tenant_id, campaign_id, status)` — the per-campaign target funnel and the send queue; plus `(tenant_id, source_signal_id)` for Signal-to-Meeting Conversion and `(tenant_id, lead_profile_id)` for the `Lead` linkage.
- `campaign_activities (tenant_id, campaign_id, activity_type, occurred_at)` — deliverability/health rollups and first-touch/last-touch attribution joins; `BRIN (occurred_at)` on this append-only table for time-range scans.
- `suppression_entries (tenant_id, match_type, match_value)` **UNIQUE** — the pre-send suppression lookup (the hot path that guarantees zero violations); plus `(tenant_id, reason)` for coverage views.
- `intelligence_reports (tenant_id, period_start DESC)` — report listing and the canonical-delivery-day generation guard.
- GIN indexes on heavily-queried Premium JSONB (`account_signals.detail_json`, `icp_gap_analyses.target_list_json`, `intelligence_reports.sections_json`) for filter/aggregation queries.

**Migrations**

- Managed via the **same versioned, forward-only migration tool** the Core uses (TypeORM/Prisma-style migrations run from the NestJS app on Render deploy; each reversible in code and reviewed before publish). Premium migrations **build on the Core's existing baseline schema** (the Core's earlier migrations created the Core tables and the Core materialized views) — they do **not** recreate or alter any Core table, only add the eight Premium tables, the FKs from those tables into the Core `tenants`/`leads` tables, and the FKs among the new Premium tables themselves. Premium migration **0003** creates `account_signals`, `signal_subscriptions`, `icp_gap_analyses`, `campaigns`, `campaign_targets`, `campaign_activities`, `suppression_entries`, and `intelligence_reports` with their enums/CHECKs (including the composite `type`/`trigger_signal_type` CHECK above), the FKs into Core `tenants`/`leads` plus the intra-Premium FKs (`campaign_targets → account_signals`/`campaigns`, `campaigns`/`intelligence_reports → icp_gap_analyses`, `campaign_activities → campaigns`/`campaign_targets`), RLS policies, and the indexes above (using `CREATE INDEX CONCURRENTLY` to avoid write locks; beta volume is small at the 5 managed clients). Premium migration **0004** ships the new materialized views (`mv_signal_movements_daily`, `mv_campaign_funnel_daily`, `mv_response_time_daily`) and their refresh jobs once event volume exists. No destructive/down-migrations run against tenant data without an explicit operator-approved backup; `campaign_activities` and `consent`/`suppression` rows tied to compliance are exempt from routine deletes.

- *`ReportViewer`:* a list view (delivered reports by period/date/status) and a detail view rendering the **five canonical sections in fixed order** with the branded-PDF download and a Mark-reviewed action used during the CSM's live walkthrough.

**Navigation**

- Left-nav primary navigation under the locked URL tenant scope (`/t/:tenantId/...`); breadcrumbs back from a campaign monitor to the campaign list and from a report to the report list.

Switching tenants is an explicit top-bar action; a user can never load another tenant's data (route guard).

**Information Hierarchy**

- *`IntelligenceDashboard` (rep/RevOps scan):* the dated **"why now"** is the hero of each row, immediately followed by `recency_tier` and `confidence` so Marcus can judge whether to lead with it; `FRESH`/`RECENT` signals sort to the top and `UNDATED`/`STALE` are visibly down-ranked and **never styled as fresh**. Source and `detail_json` are secondary, on expand.
- *`CampaignMonitor` (Devin's compliance-first scan):* the **deliverability + send-cap meters and any Suppression Violation** are the dominant elements (a violation renders red and is treated as a defect), then the target funnel and attributed pipeline. - *`ReportViewer` (Maya's executive scan):* the five sections render in canonical order with **Campaign Outcomes** (meetings booked, first/last-touch-sourced pipeline) and **ICP Gaps** surfaced as the renewal-justifying headline; the Time-Saved-vs-Headcount narrative crediting Premium against the ~$280K/yr it displaces is framed at the top of the report.

#### Interactive Elements

**Buttons**

#### Responsive Design

The admin console is **desktop-first** (it is internal/managed, not visitor-facing). Config-heavy screens are optimized for desktop; the read-only signal-alert account view and `ReportViewer` are usable on mobile.

**Desktop** (viewport ≥ 1024 px)

- Persistent left nav; `IntelligenceDashboard` dense multi-column signal table with all filters; `IcpGapAnalysisView` three-column comparison side-by-side; `CampaignBuilder` stepped form with a live suppression-preview pane; `CampaignMonitor` full two-column (funnel/activity left, deliverability/attribution right); `SuppressionManager` full table with provenance; `ReportViewer` renders the five sections plus charts in a multi-column grid.

**Tablet** (viewport 768–1023 px)

- Left nav collapses to an icon rail. `IcpGapAnalysisView` keeps two of the three comparison columns side-by-side and stacks the third; `CampaignMonitor` keeps two columns where space allows, else stacks deliverability/attribution below the funnel; tables drop lower-priority columns first (signals keep "why now", `recency_tier`, `confidence`, account; campaigns keep target status, deliverability, sourced pipeline); the `CampaignBuilder` stepped form remains usable but is recommended on desktop.

**Mobile** (viewport ≤ 767 px)

- Left nav becomes a hamburger drawer; the console is **functional but read-optimized**. The **signal-alert account view** stacks single-column so Marcus can read a fresh, dated "why now" with `recency_tier`/`confidence` from a Slack/email deep link on his phone and act while the trigger is hot. The **`ReportViewer`** stacks the five canonical sections in order with the branded-PDF download so Maya can open the monthly report on the go. Config-heavy screens (`SignalSubscriptionEditor`, `CampaignBuilder`, `SuppressionManager`, `IcpGapAnalysisView` editing) are **explicitly desktop-optimized** and the mobile view nudges the CSM/operator to a larger screen for setup and launch — there is no campaign launch or suppression edit flow tuned for mobile, consistent with the CSM-operated managed-service model.

**Unit Tests**:

- **Recency-tier classifier (`ACCOUNT_SIGNAL_CONFIDENCE_MODEL`)**: Given a `signal_date` and `detected_at`, assert the classifier returns the exact tier and multiplier: `FRESH` (≤30d, ×1.0), `RECENT` (31–90d, ×0.7), `AGING` (91–365d, capped at MEDIUM), `STALE` (>365d, capped at LOW). Assert prompt-construction for the Claude `claude-sonnet` `why_now` summarizer injects the sourced date and never asks the model to infer one.
- **`signal_type` enum guard (`SIGNAL_TYPE_ENUM`)**: Assert `signal_type` accepts only `funding_round | tech_stack_change | leadership_change | hiring_surge` (DB CHECK constraint + DTO validation), and that any other value (e.g. `patent_filing`) is rejected with a 422 envelope. Work sequences strictly: signal engine → report engine → gap analysis → campaign engine, so the highest-value, lowest-compliance-risk capability (intelligence) lands first and the compliance-heavy outbound capability lands last with the most hardening time.

**Development Phases**:

**Phase 1 — Signal Engine + foundations (10 working days)**. Deliverables: new PostgreSQL tables `account_signals` and `signal_subscriptions` (tenant-scoped, RLS, prefixed string IDs, JSONB, `created_at default now()`) with `signal_type` CHECK = `funding_round | tech_stack_change | leadership_change | hiring_surge`; the `SignalProvider` swappable adapter interface with first implementations (Crunchbase primary + the secondary enrichment provider fallback for `funding_round`; BuiltWith primary + delta against stored core `EnrichmentProfile` tech-stack for `tech_stack_change`; the secondary enrichment provider people/headcount + news for `leadership_change`/`hiring_surge`), each carrying `signal_date` + `signal_date_source` or marked `UNDATED`; the recency-tier classifier and confidence model (`FRESH ×1.0 | RECENT ×0.7 | AGING cap MEDIUM | STALE cap LOW | UNDATED cap LOW | N/A`) with the hard no-fabricated-dates guarantee; the `signal-poller` BullMQ repeatable job sized to meet the ≤ 24h p95 Signal Freshness SLA; alerting to Slack/email with the dated `why_now`, confidence, tier, and deep link; the new `signal_detected` and `signal_alerted` `AnalyticsEvent` types; endpoints `PUT /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/signal-subscriptions`, `GET /api/v1/accounts/{accountId}/signals`, `POST /api/v1/signals/{signalId}/dismiss`; the `SignalSubscriptionEditor` React screen; per-call signal-cost tracking. **Phase 3 — Campaign Engine + suppression + attribution (13 working days)**. **Risk Assessment**:

- **Technical Risks**:
  - **Signal data cost & accuracy** — provider data may be expensive, rate-limited, or noisy (false positives, stale or missing dates), undermining the ≥ 80% Signal Precision bar and the "why now."
  - **Signal freshness** — provider publish lag or poller cadence could miss the ≤ 24h p95 Signal Freshness SLA.
  - **Report-data integrity** — incorrect rollups or LLM-introduced numbers in the `IntelligenceReport` would erode buyer trust in the productized analyst deliverable.
  - **Attribution correctness** — mislinking touches to the wrong CRM opportunity, or double-counting on CRM-sync replay, would misstate campaign-sourced pipeline.

- **Timeline Risks**:
  - **Sending-domain warm-up is calendar-bound** — reputation ramp cannot be parallelized with more engineers and gates Phase 3's first live send.
  - **External contract/onboarding lead times** — finalizing Crunchbase/BuiltWith/the secondary enrichment provider and ESP agreements (and per-tenant CRM authorization) can slip and stall the dependent phase.
  - **Phase 3 compression** — the compliance-heavy campaign engine carries the most hardening; squeezing it to fit a 4-week target risks shipping suppression/attribution under-tested.

- **Mitigation Strategies**:
  - **Recency guardrail enforced in code** — every datable signal must carry `signal_date` + `signal_date_source` or be stored `UNDATED` (capped LOW) and down-ranked, never promoted to a fresh trigger; dates are never fabricated. Pair with the `min_confidence` SignalSubscription threshold, human-verified Signal Precision sampling, and `POST /signals/{signalId}/dismiss` feedback to tune sources/thresholds. Track per-call provider cost against a per-tenant signal-cost budget to control spend.
  - **Meet the SLA with cadence + corroboration** — size the BullMQ `signal-poller` cadence (hourly-to-daily per source) and run a multi-source corroboration/fallback (Crunchbase↔the secondary enrichment provider, BuiltWith↔stored `EnrichmentProfile`) so a single provider's lag does not breach the ≤ 24h p95 bar; alert ops if the SLA attainment drops below 95%.
  - **Compute numbers, synthesize only prose** — all report figures come from deterministic materialized-view rollups; Claude `claude-sonnet` writes narrative around pre-computed numbers only, with a validation step asserting every section is present, in canonical order, and reconciles to source — and that `UNDATED` signals never appear as fresh movements.
  - **Suppression as a hard gate, not a tolerance** — mandatory suppression check at materialization AND a defense-in-depth re-check immediately before each send; CRM-derived `active_deal`/`open_opportunity`, ESP-unsubscribe, and competitor-list entries; 100% branch coverage on the suppression and send-cap paths; a continuous "zero suppression violations" assertion in monitoring; Devin's `SuppressionManager` + deliverability dashboard for pre-launch verification. Every send carries a working one-click unsubscribe and recorded lawful basis (CAN-SPAM + GDPR), and the dual per-domain/per-tenant daily caps plus hard-bounce < 3% / spam-complaint < 0.1% thresholds pause sending and alert Devin on breach.
  - **CSM-gated launch (managed service)** — the `draft → active` transition requires explicit CSM approval (no unattended self-serve send), giving a human checkpoint on targeting and suppression before any campaign goes live, consistent with the 5-beta-client model.
  - **De-risk the timeline** — start ESP onboarding and domain warm-up on Day 1 in parallel with Phase 1; lock provider and CRM contracts during Phase 1; reserve the final 2–3 days of Phase 3 exclusively for compliance/deliverability/suppression/attribution hardening; if the 4-week target is at risk, ship the standalone ~$1,000/mo Advanced Intelligence Reports SKU (Pillars 1 only, available after Phase 2) first and complete the $5,000/mo bundle (both pillars) as Phase 3 lands.

**Technical Metrics**

These hold the trust bar (signal accuracy/freshness), the report-pipeline reliability bar, and — most importantly — the compliance backbone (suppression + deliverability). Suppression violations and recency-guardrail breaches are **hard acceptance bars, not tolerances**.

- **Signal Precision (Technical).** Sampled, human-verified true-positive rate among surfaced `AccountSignal`s across the four canonical SIGNAL TYPES — **`signal_type ∈ funding_round | tech_stack_change | leadership_change | hiring_surge`** (true relevant, correctly-dated signals ÷ total sampled surfaced signals). Dismissals via `POST /api/v1/signals/{signalId}/dismiss` (status `new|alerted` → `dismissed`) feed this measure. **Target: ≥ 80% precision on sampled surfaced signals; no UNDATED signal ever surfaced as a fresh trigger (recency-guardrail compliance = 100%).** This enforces the confidence/recency model: every `AccountSignal` carries a 0–1 numeric `confidence` AND a `recency_tier ∈ FRESH (≤30d, ×1.0) | RECENT (31–90d, ×0.7) | AGING (91–365d, cap MEDIUM) | STALE (>365d, cap LOW) | UNDATED (datable but no date, cap LOW) | N/A (structural)`. A datable signal that cannot be timestamped is `UNDATED` and down-ranked — **NEVER promoted to a fresh trigger; dates are never fabricated**. *Instrumentation:* weekly CSM/QA sampling of `signal_detected` events tagged true/false-positive; dismissal-rate trend per `source` (`crunchbase | builtwith | secondary_provider | clearbit | news`) and per `signal_type`.
- **Signal Freshness SLA Attainment (Technical).** Percentage of detected `AccountSignal`s whose rep/RevOps alert is delivered within **≤ 24 hours p95** of the underlying source event. **The canonical freshness clock runs from the source's published event date (`signal_date`, captured with its `signal_date_source`) to the `signal_alerted` `AnalyticsEvent`** — this is the real "is the why-now still actionable" measure the SLA intends; the start of the clock is the source-published event date, not `detected_at`. The internal pipeline latency (`detected_at` → `signal_alerted`) is tracked **separately** as an operational metric and is **not** the SLA denominator (it is seconds-to-minutes and would trivially pass ≤ 24h). **Target: ≥ 95% of alerts delivered within the ≤ 24h p95 Signal Freshness SLA** (source-published-date → `signal_alerted`). This is the Premium-tier freshness SLA and is **distinct from the core Speed-to-Profile (≤ 2 min p95)** — do not conflate them. *Instrumentation:* latency histogram with `signal_date` as t0 → `signal_alerted` as t1 (the SLA series), plus a separate `detected_at` → `signal_alerted` pipeline-latency series; only signals at/above the `SignalSubscription.min_confidence` threshold and freshness bar fire an alert. The BullMQ signal-poller cadence (hourly-to-daily per source) is sized to hold this bar. (Signals marked `UNDATED` carry no `signal_date` and are therefore excluded from the freshness-SLA denominator; they are governed by the recency guardrail above, never surfaced as fresh triggers.)
- **Deliverability & Compliance Health (Technical).** Composite of outbound email hard-bounce rate, spam-complaint rate, and unsubscribe rate against configured thresholds, plus a binary **Suppression Violation count** and CAN-SPAM + GDPR conformance. **Targets: hard-bounce < 3%, spam-complaint < 0.1%, unsubscribe rate < 2% (per-campaign, against sends), all inside the per-sending-domain AND per-tenant daily send caps (conservative beta defaults, on the order of 50/day per mailbox-domain); ZERO suppression violations; 100% of sends carry a working one-click unsubscribe and a recorded lawful basis.** (The unsubscribe-rate threshold is a tunable beta default like the send caps; breaching it raises an ops alert to Devin to review targeting/relevance, while hard-bounce and spam-complaint remain the hard deliverability gates that pause sending.) A **suppression violation** = any `campaign_sent` to an account/contact that is in an `active_deal`, an `open_opportunity`, is `unsubscribed`, or is a known `competitor` — DealThreads **NEVER** emails these; every `CampaignTarget` is checked against the tenant's `SuppressionEntry` set BEFORE any send, and a match sets `status` = `suppressed` with `suppression_reason ∈ active_deal | open_opportunity | unsubscribed | competitor`. A send to any suppressed account is a **defect, not a tolerance**. *Instrumentation:* ESP delivery webhooks → `CampaignActivity` (`activity_type ∈ sent | opened | replied | bounced | unsubscribed | handoff_task_logged`); the campaign-send worker enforces both caps (breaching a cap pauses sending) and re-checks suppression at send time; a post-send reconciliation job cross-checks every `sent` activity against the suppression set and `CrmSyncRecord` state to detect any violation. Note: **LinkedIn/ads are never sent programmatically** — they appear only as `activity_type = handoff_task_logged` (the email-first / human-handoff rule).
- **Report generation success & on-time delivery (Technical).** Percentage of scheduled `IntelligenceReport` runs that complete (`status` `generating` → `delivered`) on the canonical delivery day with a non-null `pdf_url` and all five canonical sections populated in `sections_json`. **Target: 100% on-time delivery for every active premium tenant each cycle; report-worker job success rate ≥ 99%.** *Instrumentation:* report-generator BullMQ job outcome + `delivered_on` vs the scheduled 1st-business-day; alert on any failed or late run (see below).
- **Attribution integrity (Technical).** Campaign-sourced pipeline and revenue use **FIRST-TOUCH + LAST-TOUCH attribution ONLY** (no multi-touch / weighted / U-shaped / W-shaped / time-decay / data-driven modeling in v1). Each `CampaignTarget` records `first_touch_at` and `last_touch_at`, joined to the CRM opportunity (`crm_opportunity_id`) via the inherited `CrmSyncRecord` linkage under the idempotent HubSpot-first / Salesforce-second adapter. **Target: 100% of campaign-attributed opportunities carry a valid first-touch and last-touch timestamp and a resolved `crm_opportunity_id`.** *Instrumentation:* validation check on the `meeting_booked` event → `CampaignTarget` → `CrmSyncRecord` join.

- **Daily send-cap breach** (per-sending-domain or per-tenant) pauses sending and alerts; sending resumes the next window.
- **Missing/unset unsubscribe footer or missing recorded lawful basis** blocks the send and alerts (CAN-SPAM + GDPR conformance must be 100%).
- **Recency-guardrail breach** — any attempt to surface an `UNDATED` (or undated) signal as a fresh trigger — is logged as a defect and alerted (target 100% compliance).
- **Report generation failure or late delivery** (job failed, or `delivered_on` past the 1st-business-day target) alerts the report on-call so the CSM can trigger `POST /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/reports/generate` for regeneration.
- **Signal-provider/ESP/CRM adapter failures** (SignalProvider, EmailProvider, or the inherited CRM adapter) alert with retry/backoff via BullMQ, consistent with the core's job-failure handling.

**Performance monitoring.** Key SLOs and budgets: **Signal Freshness SLA ≤ 24h p95** measured on the canonical clock (**source-published event date `signal_date` → `signal_alerted` `AnalyticsEvent`**), tracked continuously with alert if p95 approaches the bound; the internal **`detected_at` → `signal_alerted` pipeline latency** is tracked as a separate operational series (expected seconds-to-minutes) so a stalled poller is caught even though it is not the SLA denominator; **report-generator** completion well within the 1st-business-day window with job-success ≥ 99%; **campaign-send worker** throughput tracked against the dual daily send caps; **`icp-gap-recompute`** and **`POST /api/v1/admin/tenants/{tenantId}/icp-gap-analysis/recompute`** jobs monitored (202 + job-id pattern, mirroring the core enrich pattern) for completion and queue depth. BullMQ queue depth, job latency, and failure rate are monitored per worker (`signal-poller`, `campaign-send`, `report-generator`, `icp-gap-recompute`) so the Premium scheduler scales independently of the web tier. **Per-call signal-provider cost is tracked like core enrichment cost** to manage the cost/accuracy risk of funding & tech-stack data.

**User feedback collection.** Multiple loops, consistent with the managed-service model: (1) **Signal dismissals** via `POST /api/v1/signals/{signalId}/dismiss` are the primary structured feedback signal from Marcus Reed and Devin Brooks — they directly feed Signal Precision and drive source/threshold tuning on the `SignalSubscription`. | AccountSignal | A single detected, dated intelligence event about a tracked account — one of the four canonical SIGNAL TYPES (funding_round \| tech_stack_change \| leadership_change \| hiring_surge) — carrying a source, a dated 'why now', a 0–1 confidence, and a recency tier; the atomic unit of Pillar 1 and the trigger for signal-triggered campaigns. |
| SIGNAL TYPE | One of the four canonical machine values DealThreads Premium monitors: funding_round, tech_stack_change, leadership_change, hiring_surge. funding_round and tech_stack_change are the two that can automatically trigger a campaign. |
| Signal Freshness SLA | The Premium-tier guarantee that a detected source event reaches a rep/RevOps alert within ≤ 24 hours p95 of the source event date, so the 'why now' is still actionable. Distinct from the core's Speed-to-Profile (≤ 2 min p95) SLA. |
| Recency Tier | The inherited freshness classification applied to every datable signal — FRESH (≤30d), RECENT (31–90d), AGING (91–365d, cap MEDIUM), STALE (>365d, cap LOW), UNDATED (datable but no sourced date, cap LOW), N/A (structural). A datable signal that cannot be timestamped is UNDATED and down-ranked, never asserted as a fresh trigger; dates are never fabricated. |
| IcpGapAnalysis | A computed snapshot quantifying the gap between the tenant's defined ICP and (a) who actually converted (closed-won/qualified Leads) and (b) dormant high-fit accounts (high_fit-segment accounts that visited or were enriched but never became an opportunity); produces the ranked target list that seeds ICP Gap Campaigns. |
| Dormant High-Fit Account | An account in the high_fit segment (ICP Fit Score ≥ 75) that visited and/or was enriched but never became an opportunity — the primary re-engagement target for gap_driven campaigns. Reuses the inherited segment enum and ICP score verbatim. |
| ICP Gap Campaign | A CSM-managed, email-first targeted outbound campaign (gap_driven or signal_triggered) that works dormant/unidentified high-fit accounts to convert them into booked meetings; LinkedIn/ads steps are a human-handoff task list, never automated. |
| Signal-Triggered Campaign | A Campaign of type signal_triggered whose enrollment is keyed to a detected SIGNAL TYPE (funding_round or tech_stack_change): a matching AccountSignal automatically enrolls the account as a CampaignTarget with source_signal_id set — the operational heart of the signal → campaign synergy loop. |
| SuppressionEntry / Suppression | A do-not-contact rule and the act of enforcing it: every CampaignTarget is checked before any send so DealThreads NEVER emails an account in an active_deal or open_opportunity, an unsubscribed contact, or a known competitor. The compliance backbone of Pillar 2; zero violations is a hard bar. |
| IntelligenceReport | The synthesized monthly report (Pillar 1's headline deliverable and the productized marketing-analyst output, also the standalone ~$1,000/mo SKU), delivered on the canonical monthly delivery day as a branded PDF + dashboard view, containing exactly five sections — Lead Trends, ICP Gaps, Response-Time Performance, Account Signal Movements, Campaign Outcomes — and reviewed live with the CSM. |
| Human-Handoff Task | A LinkedIn or ads outreach step that DealThreads surfaces as a documented task for a human to execute manually (recorded as CampaignActivity.activity_type = handoff_task_logged) rather than sending automatically; encodes the canonical email-first / no-automated-LinkedIn-or-ads rule. |
| First-Touch + Last-Touch Attribution | The only attribution model in Premium v1: campaign-sourced pipeline/revenue is credited to a target's first recorded touch (first_touch_at) and last recorded touch (last_touch_at) before the opportunity, with no multi-touch/weighted/time-decay modeling. |
| Synergy Loop | The closed proactive loop the Premium tier is built to prove: a detected AccountSignal triggers a timely, relevant Campaign; the campaign's outcomes (meetings, pipeline) feed the next monthly IntelligenceReport; the report informs the next cycle's targeting — signal → campaign → report → signal. |
