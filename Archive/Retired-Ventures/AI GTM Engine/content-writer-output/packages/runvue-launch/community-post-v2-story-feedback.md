# Post title
**I was the dashboard. So I built one that does my job while I sleep — looking for a few agencies to break it.**

# Body

A few weeks back a client messaged me: "hey, any update on the campaigns?"

The engine had been running all day for them. Emails going out, replies coming in, lists getting built. None of it visible. The work was happening and the only proof was me — every status update was me typing a Telegram message, every Friday recap was me writing it, every "what's happening" got answered out of my head. There was no account management system. There was just me.

That bugged me enough that I built a fix and pointed it at my own engine first.

It's a white-label portal that plugs into SmartLead, my dialer, and HubSpot, and just... shows the client everything, live. Right now mine shows 36,337 emails sent, 147 replies, 11 interested prospects across 20 campaigns. Real numbers, synced automatically.

Then it got a little out of hand, in a good way:

- The AI audit that starts the engagement lives in the portal instead of dying in a PDF. Every leak we found, the severity, and the live status of fixing each one. The client watches the promise get kept, and the renewal conversation basically writes itself.
- It writes a 60-second weekly briefing from the real numbers and reads it in a clone of my voice. I never record anything.
- Clients approve lead lists with one tap, from the portal or straight from the digest email.
- There's a watchdog that monitors bounce rates. I fire-drilled it this week: it paused a campaign on its own, posted the incident, and the approve button genuinely restarted sending.
- An agent reads every client's numbers overnight hunting for expansion revenue. It found $1,750 a month of upsells in my own engine, including eleven warm replies nobody had called, and drafted the pitch emails for me to send.
- And as of this morning, clients can literally ask it questions. "How did this week go?" gets a real answer from their data, in my voice if they want. The thing answers like I would, at 11pm on a Sunday, without me.
