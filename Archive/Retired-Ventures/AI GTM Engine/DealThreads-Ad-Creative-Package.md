### Voice & Tone

1. **Concrete over abstract.** "Your rep Googles the budget 10 minutes after the form submits" beats "improve lead intelligence." Show the moment, not the category.
2. **Lead with the buyer's reality, not the product.** Open on the stalled deal, the 5 PM callback, the Googling — earn the product mention by naming their day first.
3. **Numbers over adjectives.** $30K, $280K, 20 minutes, 5 seats, $10K+ deals. Specific figures carry the weight; drop "powerful," "seamless," "revolutionary."
4. **No hype words.** Ban "game-changer," "supercharge," "unleash," "revolutionary," "cutting-edge," "10x." If a line would survive a skeptical RevOps lead's eye-roll, keep it.
5. **Short, declarative, confident.** Sentences a busy operator scans in two seconds. One idea per line. Let white space and full stops do the work.
6. **Honest by default.** Pre-launch means we sell the opening and the math, never invented results. Confidence comes from the argument, not from fake proof.

### Headlines to Test (6)

Each is labeled with its type and the single hypothesis it tests. Run as the primary hook variable. Keep the image and CTA fixed while these compete.

**1. Benefit-focused**
> "Your rep opens every lead already knowing the budget, the timeline, and who signs."
- **Type:** Benefit / outcome-forward.
- **Hypothesis:** Leading with the rep's improved end-state (full profile at first contact) out-pulls leading with the pain. Tests whether the audience is moved more by the aspiration than the wound.

**2. Problem-focused**
> "A contact form hands your rep a name and an email. The deal was decided by the 20 minutes of research it refused to do."
- **Type:** Problem / pain-naming.
- **Hypothesis:** Naming the hidden lead-leak tax (the gap the form left) stops the scroll harder than the benefit. Tests pain-first vs benefit-first hooking on a problem-aware audience.

**3. Question-based**
> "How long after a lead submits your form does a rep start Googling them?"
- **Type:** Question / self-implicating.
- **Hypothesis:** A question that forces the reader to picture their own broken process drives higher engagement and qualified clicks than a declarative claim. Tests reader-implication as the engagement mechanism.

**4. Number/stat-based**
> "$30K a year does the job you've been quietly budgeting $280K for."
- **Type:** Number / money-math.
- **Hypothesis:** The arithmetic argument ($30K vs ~$280K in salary budget) is the strongest single hook because it reframes cost as savings. Tests whether the specific-figure swap beats narrative and question hooks on click-through and CPL. (This is the safe-to-lead-with proof — expect it to be the benchmark to beat.)

**5. Contrarian (bonus)**
> "You don't have a headcount problem. You have a tooling gap."
- **Type:** Contrarian / belief-flip.
- **Hypothesis:** Challenging the buyer's default assumption ("we need to hire a coordinator and an analyst") creates a pattern-interrupt that earns the click from RevOps leaders who think they've already diagnosed the problem. Tests reframe-against-belief vs straight value claims.

**6. Curiosity-gap (bonus)**
> "The rep who called back at 5 PM took the meeting. The faster competitor never saw it coming."
- **Type:** Curiosity gap / story-open.
- **Hypothesis:** An open narrative loop (who won, how, why) pulls the reader into the body copy and lifts time-on-creative and downstream conversion, even if top-line CTR is similar. Tests story-driven curiosity vs the explicit money-math hook.

**Discovery-focused** (low-friction, learn-first):

### Testing Plan

**Test order and priority — isolate one variable at a time:**
1. **Hook first (highest leverage).** The headline drives the majority of performance variance, so settle it first. Run the 6 headlines against one fixed image (start with the stat card or before-after) and one fixed CTA. Cheapest variable to change, biggest swing.
2. **Creative second.** Take the 1–2 winning hooks, lock them, and rotate the 4 image concepts underneath. Now you're optimizing the scroll-stop, with the message already proven.
3. **CTA third (refinement).** With hook and image fixed, test CTAs — within-group first, then the three group winners head-to-head. This is a polish lever, not a savior; it tunes a working ad rather than rescuing a broken one.

**How to read results:**
- Compare against the **single primary metric for that funnel stage** (below) — not vanity metrics. A high-CTR hook that produces no qualified pilots loses to a lower-CTR hook that fills the founding-5.
- Let a variant accumulate the minimum volume (below) **before** calling anything. Early leaders routinely regress; do not kill or crown on day one.
- Declare a winner only on a **meaningful, stable separation** — roughly a 20%+ gap on the stage metric that holds steady over the final third of the test window. If two variants stay within noise of each other, keep the cheaper/simpler one (usually the stat card) and move to the next variable.
- Kill clear losers early to reallocate budget; keep ambiguous ones running to volume.

**Minimum spend / impressions per variant before calling it:**
- **Hook test:** ~1,000–1,500 impressions per headline, or ~50–75 clicks, whichever comes first. At a ~$250 target CAC, budget roughly **$150–$250 per variant** for a directional read; do not judge a hook on under ~1,000 impressions.
- **Creative test:** ~1,500 impressions per image (visual effects need slightly more exposure to separate).
- **CTA test:** ~100 clicks per variant, since CTA impact shows up at the click→lead step, not at impression.
- **Floor for any decision:** never call a test on fewer than ~30 conversions total across the test, or one outlier lead skews everything. If volume is thin, extend the window rather than lowering the bar.

**The single primary metric per funnel stage** (judge each stage on one number only):
| Funnel stage | What the creative variable is doing | **Primary metric** |
|---|---|---|
| **Impression → click** (hook + image) | Stopping the scroll and earning the click | **CTR** (click-through rate) |
| **Click → lead** (landing page + CTA) | Converting the click into a captured, qualified lead | **Cost per qualified lead** (target ~$250) |
| **Lead → pilot** (offer + follow-up) | Turning a lead into a paid $500 pilot | **Pilot conversion rate** (target ~25%) |
| **Pilot → retained** (product + onboarding) | Keeping the client past onboarding | **Churn** (target <10%) |

Read top-down: a creative that wins on CTR but loses on cost-per-qualified-lead is a false positive — the next variant should fix the message-to-offer match, not chase more clicks. The founding-5 cap means lead *quality* outranks lead *volume* throughout: optimize the whole matrix toward the 5 right pilots, not the most clicks.

## 7. Audience Targeting Notes

Pre-revenue reality: no first-party data, no conversion pixel history, no seed audiences. The first 4-6 weeks build the assets (pixel data, contact lists, video-viewer pools) that the better-performing targeting modes depend on. Plan accordingly — lookalikes and retargeting are Phase 2+ levers, not Day 1.

### LinkedIn (primary channel — ~60% of budget)

This is where the ICP lives at job-title precision. Lead here.

**Targeting facets to stack (use 2-3 max per audience; over-stacking shrinks reach below LinkedIn's ~50K delivery floor and inflates CPM):**

- **Job titles (primary lever):** RevOps Manager, Director of Revenue Operations, VP RevOps, Sales Operations Manager, VP Sales, VP Revenue, Head of Growth, Director of Demand Generation, Head of GTM, Chief Revenue Officer (CRO at the smaller end of the size band). Build two title clusters so you can read which persona converts: an **Ops cluster** (RevOps / Sales Ops) and a **Leadership cluster** (VP Sales / CRO / Head of Growth).
- **Job function + seniority (broader fallback when title targeting under-delivers):** Functions = Sales, Operations, Marketing; Seniority = Manager, Director, VP, CXO. Run this as a separate, clearly-labeled "broad" audience — do not blend it with title targeting or you lose the read.
- **Company headcount:** 51-200, 201-500, 501-1000. Exclude 1-50 (below ICP / sub-scale) and 1001+ (drifts enterprise, where Qualified already plays). Mid-market is the whole thesis — hold this line.
- **Industry:** Software & IT (B2B SaaS), Financial Services / fintech, Professional Services, Business Consulting. These map to $10K+ deal sizes with inbound forms worth replacing.
- **Member Groups (layer onto an interest audience, not as a sole filter — Group audiences are often too thin to run alone):** RevOps Co-op, Modern Sales Pros, RevGenius, Sales Hacker, Demand Gen / marketing-ops communities.

**Matched Audiences (the compounding asset — set up Week 1 even though they're thin until traffic accrues):**

- **Website retargeting:** install the LinkedIn Insight Tag Day 1. Retarget all site visitors, with a tighter segment for pricing-page and demo-page viewers. This pool is empty at launch — it's a Phase 2 lever that you seed now.
- **Contact-list upload:** upload any founder/hand-built prospect list (the AI Ark-sourced ICP list) as a Matched Audience for direct ABM-style targeting. Realistic floor: LinkedIn needs ~300 matched members to activate; a hand-built list may not clear it at first — keep feeding it.
- **Lookalikes:** build off the contact list and (later) converters. Genuinely Phase 2 — lookalikes need a seed of meaningful size and quality before they're worth running.

**Format note:** lead with Single Image and Document/carousel ads to the Ops and Leadership clusters; reserve Conversation/Message ads for the warmest segments only (matched ABM list, retargeting) — they're higher CPC and feel intrusive cold.

### Meta (secondary — retargeting + cheap reach/video, ~15-20% of budget)

Meta cannot target job titles, so it is **not** a cold-prospecting ICP channel here. Its jobs: (1) cheap top-of-funnel video views to build a retargeting pool, (2) retargeting site visitors who came from LinkedIn/Google, (3) testing the "money math" hook against a broad business-owner audience at low CPM.

- **Demographics:** 28-55, weighted toward founders/operators; can layer employer-size and job-title-adjacent signals via detailed targeting, but treat these as loose proxies, not ICP-grade.
- **Interests / behaviors:** Salesforce, HubSpot, Pipedrive, RevOps, marketing automation, B2B marketing, SaaS, Business software, Small business owners, Marketing Land / industry publications. Stack an interest with a behavior ("Business page admins," "Engaged shoppers" off-bounds — keep it B2B) to tighten.
- **Custom Audiences:** site visitors (Meta Pixel installed Day 1), video viewers (25%/50%/75% completions of the hero "5 PM callback" video), and any uploaded lead-magnet / email list. These are the real Meta play — built over the first few weeks.
- **Lookalikes:** 1-3% lookalikes off the video-viewer and site-visitor Custom Audiences once they cross ~1,000 members. Phase 2+.
- **Exclusions:** current customers/beta clients, existing leads in pipeline, job seekers, students, and (via interest exclusion) people interested in "make money online" / generic marketing-guru content that drags in non-ICP clicks.

### Google (intent capture — Search + a thin retargeting layer, ~20-25% of budget)

Google catches buyers actively shopping the category. Keep it lean and intent-tight; broad match will burn a pre-revenue budget fast.

- **Search keyword themes (exact + phrase match only at this budget; no broad match):**
  - *Category / competitor-replacement:* "ai chat widget for website," "replace contact form," "conversational lead capture," "ai lead enrichment tool," "Drift alternative," "Qualified alternative," "Chili Piper alternative," "Clearbit alternative."
  - *Problem-aware:* "lead response time tool," "automatic lead enrichment," "website lead qualification software," "speed to lead software."
  - Keep competitor-alternative and category terms in separate ad groups so you can read which intent converts.
- **In-market audiences (layer as "observation," not "targeting," on Search; use for bid adjustments, not hard filters):** CRM Software, Marketing Automation, Sales Automation, Web Design & Development Services.
- **Custom intent / custom segments:** build from the competitor + category keywords above and from URLs of competitor/category sites — use these to extend reach on Display retargeting and YouTube.
- **YouTube (tie to the "AI-automation tutorial" channel signal):** run the hero video as in-stream/in-feed against custom-intent segments and competitor-term audiences — but only after Search proves the message; don't open YouTube cold on a lean test.
- **Display:** restrict to **retargeting only** (site visitors). Do not run cold Display prospecting on this budget — it's where pre-revenue test dollars go to die on junk placements.
- **Exclusions (account-level negative keywords + placement exclusions):** "free," "jobs," "career," "salary," "internship," "course," "template," "wordpress plugin free," DIY/open-source queries, and irrelevant consumer-chat terms. Exclude mobile-game and made-for-AdSense placements on any Display/YouTube delivery.

### Exclude across all platforms

- Current customers and active beta/pilot clients (the founding 5).
- Existing pipeline leads (suppress so paid doesn't re-bill you for people you're already working).
- Competitors and their employees (LinkedIn: exclude competitor company names; Google: negative-match competitor-brand terms you don't want to pay to defend).
- Sub-scale companies (<50 employees) and sub-$10K-deal-size businesses — below ICP economics.
- Agencies / brokers / resellers where out of ICP (consistent with the broader GTM call to skip agencies and brokers).
- Job seekers, students, and "how to get into RevOps/sales" career-intent traffic — high click, zero buying authority.

## 8. Budget & Bidding Recommendations

All figures are for a lean pre-revenue **test**, not a scaled program. The goal of spend in Phase 1 is a *clean read on message-market fit and a defensible CPL* — not volume. Tie everything back to the unit economics: target **CAC ~$250**, pilot conversion **~25%**, entry offer **$500 pilot → $2,500/mo core**.

**CPL math that anchors the budget:** at a 25% pilot-conversion rate, 4 qualified leads should yield 1 pilot. If target CAC is ~$250, that implies a blended **target CPL of roughly $60-65** to hit CAC on the pilot alone — and the pilot is just the entry point to $2,500-5,000/mo, so there's real headroom to pay above that once retention is proven. Treat **$60-65 CPL as the "healthy" line and ~$120-150 CPL as the soft ceiling** for a cold B2B test before a channel gets cut or reworked. (All directional — a brand-new account with no Quality Score / relevance history will run hot for the first 2-3 weeks; judge channels on the trend, not Week 1.)

### Total starting budget & split

**Recommended starting budget: $4,000-6,000/mo** (~$130-200/day total). Below ~$3K/mo you can't gather statistically meaningful signal across three platforms in a reasonable window; above ~$6K/mo you're scaling before you've earned the read.

Use **$5,000/mo (~$165/day)** as the planning midpoint:

| Platform | % split | Monthly | Daily | Primary job in the test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LinkedIn** | 60% | $3,000 | ~$100 | ICP prospecting at title precision — the real read |
| **Google Search** | 25% | $1,250 | ~$40 | Capture active-intent / competitor-alternative demand |
| **Meta** | 15% | $750 | ~$25 | Cheap video reach + retargeting pool, hook-testing |

Notes on the split:
- LinkedIn is deliberately heavy — it's the only channel that targets the ICP by title, and the whole thesis is mid-market B2B precision. Underfunding it to spread across three platforms is the most common way this kind of test fails.
- Google Search is small but high-intent; ~$40/day on tight exact/phrase terms is enough to learn whether category demand exists without bleeding on broad match.
- Meta is intentionally the smallest line — at this stage it earns its keep building retargeting/video pools and stress-testing the money-math hook cheaply, not as a primary lead source.
- **Reserve note:** if you can only commit $3K/mo, do **not** thin all three — run **LinkedIn + Google only** (~$2,000 / $1,000) and add Meta once there's a retargeting pool worth feeding.

### Bid strategy per platform

- **LinkedIn:** start on **manual CPC with bids near the low end of LinkedIn's suggested range**, objective = Website Conversions if the pixel is firing, otherwise Lead Gen Forms (native forms convert better cold and seed your list faster). Manual control matters early — LinkedIn's auto-bid will spend to the top of the range on a cold account. Use **Lead Gen Forms** for at least one variant to lower friction and start building the contact/lookalike asset. Move toward Maximum Delivery only once a campaign shows a stable, acceptable CPL.
- **Meta:** start on **lowest-cost (Automatic) bidding** to gather pixel data — *not* cost-cap on Day 1, because with no conversion history a cost-cap will simply fail to spend. Once the pixel logs a meaningful number of conversions (rule of thumb ~50/week per ad set, which a pre-revenue test likely won't hit fast), introduce a **cost-cap near your target CPL** to defend efficiency. Until then, optimize cheaper upper-funnel events (video views, landing-page views, leads) rather than starving on a rare purchase event.
- **Google:** start Search on **Manual CPC (or Maximize Clicks with a max-CPC cap)** while there is zero conversion data — **do not** open on Max Conversions / tCPA, which need ~15-30 conversions in 30 days to function and will mis-spend without them. Once the account accrues that conversion history, **switch to Maximize Conversions, then layer a target CPA** near your CAC tolerance. Keep Display/YouTube on retargeting with conservative caps.

### Success metrics / benchmarks (all directional — B2B, treat as goalposts not guarantees)

| Metric | LinkedIn | Google Search | Meta | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **CTR** | ~0.4-0.6% | ~2-4% (branded/competitor higher) | ~0.8-1.2% | LinkedIn CTR is structurally low; don't panic below 0.5% |
| **CPC** | ~$8-14 | ~$3-8 (competitor terms can spike) | ~$1-3 | LinkedIn clicks are expensive — judge on CPL, not CPC |
| **CPL** | ~$60-120 | ~$50-100 | ~$30-70 (often lower-intent) | Blended target **$60-65**, soft ceiling ~$120-150 |
| **Landing-page conversion** | ~3-6% | ~5-10% (high intent) | ~2-4% | Lead Gen Forms convert higher but lower-intent; qualify hard |
| **Lead → pilot** | — | — | — | Target **~25%** (your assumption); 4 leads → 1 pilot |
| **Target CAC** | — | — | — | **~$250** on the pilot; headroom from $2.5-5K/mo retention |

Caveats to set expectations honestly:
- A brand-new ad account has no Quality Score, relevance, or pixel history — **expect CPCs and CPLs 20-40% above these ranges for the first 2-3 weeks**, then improving as the algorithms learn. Judge the trend.
- Lead-magnet / Lead-Gen-Form leads are cheaper but colder than a demo-request; a $40 form-fill and a $120 "book a demo" are not the same lead — track them as separate conversion types and weight by quality, not just cost.
- With a pre-revenue test you will likely see only a **handful of pilots, not a clean conversion rate**, in the first 60 days. The 25% figure is a planning assumption to validate, not a benchmark to expect on Day 30.

### Phased ramp (test → learn → scale)

**Phase 0 — Pre-flight (before any spend):** Install LinkedIn Insight Tag, Meta Pixel, and Google conversion tracking. Define conversion events (demo request = primary; lead-form / content download = secondary). Stand up the pilot-offer landing page and at least one Lead Gen Form. Load the AI Ark-sourced ICP list as a Matched/Custom Audience. *Do not spend a dollar until tracking fires correctly* — untracked spend on a pre-revenue test is wasted money.

**Weeks 1-2 — Test (full budget live, low expectations):** Run LinkedIn (Ops cluster vs Leadership cluster), Google Search (category vs competitor-alternative ad groups), Meta (hook test + video). 2-3 creative variants per ad set; do not touch bids or audiences for the first ~7-10 days — let delivery stabilize. Watch CTR and CPC for early signal; CPL data will be thin. **Kill rule:** pause any single ad (not the whole campaign) with sub-0.2% CTR after ~1,000-1,500 impressions — it's a clear creative dud.

**Weeks 3-4 — Learn (first real cuts):** Now there's enough data to read CPL by audience cluster and ad group. **Kill rule:** cut any audience/ad group whose CPL is tracking above ~$150 (soft ceiling) with no improving trend, or that's clearly the weaker of an A/B pair. **Reallocate** that budget into the best-performing LinkedIn cluster or Google ad group. Begin building Phase 2 audiences: site-visitor retargeting and video-viewer pools should now have enough members to activate. Refresh fatigued creative.

**Weeks 5-8 — Scale (only what's earning):** **Scale rule:** for any audience/ad group consistently at or below the **$60-65 healthy CPL** with stable delivery, increase its daily budget **~20-30% at a time, no more than ~2x/week** (jumping budgets harder resets the learning phase, especially on Meta). Turn on the retargeting layer across all three platforms now that pools exist. On Google, if conversion volume has accrued, switch Search to Maximize Conversions and test a target CPA. On LinkedIn, build a lookalike off any converters and test it against the cold clusters. **Hard stop / re-evaluate rule:** if after ~8 weeks and ~$10-12K total spend no channel is delivering qualified leads under the ~$120-150 ceiling and at least one pilot is not in motion, **pause and rework offer/message/landing page before adding budget** — a scaling problem is rarely solved by spending more into a message that isn't landing.

**Quick-reference kill/scale rules:**
- **Kill (ad level):** sub-0.2% CTR after ~1,000-1,500 impressions.
- **Kill (audience/ad-group level):** CPL above ~$150 with no improving trend by Week 3-4, or the losing side of an A/B pair.
- **Scale:** CPL at/below ~$60-65 with stable delivery → +20-30% budget per step, max ~2x/week.
- **Hard stop:** ~$10-12K spent over ~8 weeks with no channel under the ~$120-150 ceiling and no pilot in motion → fix message/offer before adding spend.
