## 1. THE BET

For 60 days Ryan sells exactly one thing: done-for-you invoice chasing for owner-led agencies and professional-services firms. Two primary channels, both already live in the stack: phone (Salesfinity, 2,880 loaded leads, zero dials so far) and LinkedIn (Sendr, 8-step video sequence already running on 151 Tier-A agencies). The anchor is the only real proof Ryan owns: $650K recovered for Creative Alternatives in 90 days, half of it in the first 30. Cold email is a hedge budgeted at zero closes because its observed rate is 0.09-0.17%, not the 1-3% benchmark.

The close motion doesn't exist yet (0 meetings booked ever, across 2,985 contacts). The plan manufactures one and prices its inexperience honestly: 15% readout-to-close base case until 8 real readouts say otherwise.

### The offer

**Name:** Invoice Chase Engine (by Dealthreads). One name, one canonical PRODUCTS.md. "AI GTM Engine," "GTM Experiment Engine," and "$500 Leak Audit sprint" labels go in the drawer. The offer locks end of week 1 and can't change until 8 teardowns have been delivered.

**Promise:** "We get your stuck invoices paid. Our first client had $650K outstanding; we recovered it in 90 days, half in the first 30, without their team sending a single chase email."

**The ladder:**

1. **Free AR teardown.** For cold prospects the default ask is a 15-minute screen-share: "Don't send me anything. Share your QuickBooks screen for 15 minutes and I'll mark up your aging live." That converts the trust cliff (handing a stranger a file that names your delinquent clients) into the readout itself, and collapses two scheduling events into one. The file-export path (aging export, teardown back in 48 hours, readout booked at delivery) is reserved for warm and inbound prospects. Intake email includes a rough 60+ AR band pre-qual question, NDA by default, and a redacted-names option. The deliverable is thicker than reading their own report back: what's stuck at 30/60/90, what's realistically collectable (with the n=1 Creative Alternatives basis stated honestly), a ready-to-send first chase message drafted in their voice for their oldest invoice, and a cost-of-doing-nothing line.
2. **Install: $2,000 one-time, live in 7 days.** Rises to $2,500 after 3 logos, and every proposal says so.
3. **Managed AR: $1,000/mo.** Default-on in the agreement as a visible checkbox with a plain-English opt-out line (hiding it trades a cleaner close now for a cancellation fight at day 45). Month 1 bills with the install, guarantee window credited against it; that single paperwork change pulls real billing MRR inside the 60-day window. 90-day initial term, then month to month. Delivered through the Runvue white-label portal: automated chasing in their brand voice, renewal invoicing, weekly "who still owes you" digest, monthly collections report, escalation flags. The portal is what justifies $1,000 over the old $500.
   - **Attach rate is a hypothesis, not a fact.** Zero buyers have ever taken Managed AR at any price. Base case models 70% attach until 2-3 clients sign without striking the line. At 70% attach, $10K MRR needs ~14 clients, not 11.
   - **Founding rate:** first 3 clients pay $750/mo locked 12 months in exchange for a written testimonial. Every founding proposal carries a 48-hour deadline, and the clock starts at proposal delivery, not verbal yes.

**Relationship safety is the lead feature, not a footnote.** The kill objection at every readout will be "I'm not letting an automated system dun my best client in my name." So the offer sells the controls first: client-by-client opt-in list, owner approves the first sequence per debtor, a visible pause-this-client button in the Runvue portal, escalation routes to the owner before tone sharpens. The line is "you approve every word before it sends."

**Guarantee:** recover at least 3x the install fee ($6,000) within 60 days or full refund of the install fee and they keep the system. Offered only after the teardown and only when the aging shows at least $50K at 60+ days. "Recovered" is defined in the contract: all payments received on invoices that were 60+ days past due at kickoff, full stop. That definition is generous to Ryan and kills the "those invoices would've been paid anyway" dispute.

**Sub-$50K variant (built now, not improvised on a call):** Creative Alternatives' $650K book is an outlier; a typical 5-50 person agency carries $10-30K at 60+ days. For books under the guarantee bar: $1,000-1,500 install with a 2x guarantee, or the performance-priced install below. Guarantee-eligibility % gets tracked from teardown #1 as a leading indicator on both close rate and whether the ICP pain thesis holds.

**Performance-priced install (replaces the 10% Recovery Share):** $0 today; the standard $2,000 install is paid only out of the first dollars recovered, capped at $2,000; Managed AR starts day 31 as normal. Same zero-risk psychology, but no percentage-of-collections structure (a 10% contingency on another company's receivables is collection-agency fee structure, licensed or regulated in several states Ryan is soliciting into), and unlike the old Recovery Share it carries real MRR. This is the DEFAULT second option in readouts 1-5, not a stall-triggered fallback; zero-risk options roughly double close rates on early reps.

**Contract (this was missing entirely):** one 3-4 page agreement, MSA plus SOW skeleton, e-sign ready on a free PandaDoc tier. Contains: authority to send communications in the client's name, QuickBooks/Xero access terms, the recovered-dollars definition, the Managed AR default-on checkbox with opt-out, month-1-bills-with-install, confidentiality over the AR file, the performance-install terms. Operating rule: agreement sent within 1 hour of verbal yes.

**ICP:** founder-led US agencies and pro-services firms (creative, video production, marketing, digital, IT-on-QuickBooks, construction 3-11 FTE), 5-50 staff, recurring client invoicing, no finance team. Exactly the 151 Tier-A + 720 Tier-B Sendr lists, the 2,880 Salesfinity leads, and the existing Origami tables. Zero new lists through week 3; week 3 carries a list-quality kill gate (Section 4) because the "zero new lists" rule must not lock in a bad-list diagnosis for 60 days.

**Back-pocket upsell, strictly capped:** the GTM Experiment Engine ($2,000-3,500/mo) gets no cold motion and no proof-asset work before week 6. It's pitched only (a) inside a live AR conversation when the founder says "my real problem is new clients, not unpaid invoices," or (b) to an AR client after their 30-day guarantee window proves out. One signed Starter in weeks 7-8 closes the founding-discount gap to $10K. A card in the pocket, not a lane on the calendar.

**If 1 close/wk is the requirement, here is what buys it (the old plan never said):** 7+ teardown commitments/wk at 15% close, or a measured 40% close rate on ~2.5 readouts held/wk. Neither is assumed. The week-4 memo re-bases everything on observed rates.
