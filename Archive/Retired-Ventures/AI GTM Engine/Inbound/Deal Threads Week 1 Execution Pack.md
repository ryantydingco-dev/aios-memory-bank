# Monday

## Theme

The dead form problem.

## LinkedIn Post

```text
Most B2B demo forms are optimized for the wrong finish line.

They celebrate the submit.

But sales pays for what happens after.

A typical form gives the rep:
- name
- email
- company
- maybe a phone number
- one vague message box

Then the real work starts.

The rep opens LinkedIn.
Then the company site.
Then the CRM.
Then maybe Apollo, Crunchbase, BuiltWith, Google, funding/news searches, and a few tabs that should come with a warning label.

That research determines whether the buyer is high-fit, urgent, senior, funded, technical, or worth calling right now.

But the form did not capture it.

So the buyer waits.
The rep guesses.
The CRM gets another half-empty record.

I think the next version of the B2B contact form is not a longer form.

It is a buyer-profile builder.

The form should not just collect the lead.

It should prepare sales for the callback.
```

# Tuesday

## Theme

What a buyer profile should include.

## LinkedIn Post

```text
A form fill is not a buyer profile.

A form fill says:
"Someone raised their hand."

A buyer profile tells sales:
- who they are
- what company they are from
- why they probably care
- whether they fit the ICP
- how urgent the need is
- what they already told you
- what the rep should ask next
- what is still unknown

That last one matters.

If the data is unknown, the CRM should say unknown.

Not guessed.
Not hallucinated.
Not dressed up as confidence.

The job is not to make the lead look complete.

The job is to make the rep better prepared.

That is the standard I think B2B forms should move toward:

less "thanks, we got your message"

more "here is the buyer context sales needs before the callback."
```

# Wednesday

## Theme

Before and after.

## LinkedIn Post

```text
Here is the difference I am trying to make obvious:

Raw form submission:

"Hi, I want to learn more."

Rep reaction:

"Who is this? What do they do? Are they a fit? Is this urgent? Is this a real buyer? What should I say?"

Buyer profile:

"VP Ops at a 70-person logistics SaaS company. Likely evaluating workflow automation. Company uses HubSpot and Salesforce-adjacent tools. High ICP fit. Timeline sounds near-term. Open with operational bottleneck, not generic product pitch. Unknown: budget and decision process."

That changes the first reply.

It changes who gets called first.

It changes whether the rep sounds prepared.

And it changes whether a serious buyer feels like the company actually understood the signal they just sent.

Most B2B forms do the first version.

They should be moving toward the second.
```

# Thursday

## Theme

Trust, implementation, and accuracy.

## LinkedIn Post

```text
My rule for AI in the CRM:

If it is unknown, mark it unknown.

Do not make the CRM sound confident when the data is not.

That is one of the reasons I do not like pitching this as "AI magic."

The useful version is more boring:

- ask the buyer a few better questions
- enrich the company behind the scenes
- separate buyer-provided data from inferred data
- push a readable profile into the CRM
- mark gaps clearly
- help the rep follow up better

The goal is not to make every lead look perfect.

The goal is to make every serious lead easier to understand before the callback.

Bad AI makes things sound complete.

Good workflow design makes the next action obvious.
```
