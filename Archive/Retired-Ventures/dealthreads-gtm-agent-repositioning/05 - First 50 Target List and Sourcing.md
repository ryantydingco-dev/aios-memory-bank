> The build that unblocks selling the GTM offer: how to assemble the first ~50 named targets, who exactly to look for, and the exact searches to run.

## 0. The one-line filter (memorize this)

> A **US boutique retained-executive-search or specialized-perm recruiting firm, ~5–50 people**, where the **Managing Partner / Owner** is active on LinkedIn — and whose **client-side** business development (landing the companies that pay the fees) runs on referrals with no real outbound system.

Fail any of those → not in the 50. **The whole pitch is client-side BD, never candidate sourcing** (they're elite at candidates; that's not the gap).

---

## 1. Targeting filter (inclusion / exclusion)

**Firmographic (include):**
- Company type: **retained executive search**, **specialized/vertical perm recruiting**, or **boutique search firm** (tech, healthcare, finance, manufacturing, legal, exec-level niches). Perm/retained — NOT high-volume temp/staffing-by-the-hour.
- Headcount: **~5–50** (sweet spot 5–25). Big enough to afford ~$2–5k/mo, small enough that the MP/owner still personally does business development.
- Deal value: **placements ≥ ~$25k fee** (i.e. placing roles ≥ ~$100k comp). One placement must dwarf the retainer. (Proxy: "retained," "executive search," "C-suite/VP/Director searches," "% of first-year comp.")
- Geo: **United States only.**
- Buyer sells **to companies** (the client side), even though their "product" is candidates.

**Buyer role (the person you contact):**
- **Managing Partner / Founder / Owner / Principal.** In a boutique search firm this person owns client acquisition. (Not "Recruiter" / "Talent Partner" — that's the candidate-side delivery seat.)

**Signal — the "why now" tells (need ≥1; this is what makes them worth contacting *now*):**
- **MACRO (applies to the whole segment, 2025–26):** their inbound-req engine is contracting — "finding new clients" became the #1 staffing-industry challenge in 2025. This is the segment-wide hook even with no per-firm signal.
- **HIRING (own BD):** posting for a "Business Development Manager / Client Partner / new-business" role → they feel the client-side gap and are about to spend on it.
- **PAIN:** MP posts/comments about a slow market, "client side is tough right now," diversifying beyond referrals, pipeline pressure.
- **VOLUME/CRAFT:** lots of "placed a [role]" / candidate content, but **nothing about how they win new client companies** → classic craft-rich, BD-poor.
- **MOVE:** new firm, new partner, new vertical, recently went independent from a bigger search firm.

**Exclude:**
- High-volume temp/contract staffing where per-placement margin is thin (deal value won't clear the retainer cleanly).
- Big national staffing brands (have internal BD/marketing teams; long multi-stakeholder sale).
- Solo recruiters with no real firm / no budget.
- In-house corporate TA / RPO (not the buyer).
- Anyone clearly running a mature outbound machine already.
- Lead-gen / sales agencies (competitors).

## 2. The sub-niche archetypes (so the 50 aren't generic)

Boutique search splits by the vertical they place into. Build ~6–8 names per archetype. Each gets a tailored opening angle — all riding the same macro "clients used to come to you; that flipped in 2025" hook.

| # | Archetype (vertical search niche) | Why they fit | Opening angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Tech / engineering executive search** | High fees, MP on LinkedIn daily, tech hiring softened in 2025 = acute client-side pain | "Tech reqs dried up this year — the firms still winning are the ones doing real client-side outbound for the first time." |
| 2 | **Healthcare / life-sciences search** | High-comp placements, relationship-driven, referral-dependent BD | "Healthcare placements are steady, but landing *new* client systems is still all referrals. That's the part we systematize." |
| 3 | **Finance / accounting / PE-portfolio search** | Very high fees (C-suite/PE), LinkedIn-active partners | "PE portfolio searches pay well — but client-side BD between mandates is feast-or-famine. We make it a system." |
| 4 | **Manufacturing / industrial / supply-chain search** | Under-pitched niche, high comp roles, owner-led BD | "Industrial search is a referral business. The firms pulling ahead added one repeatable outbound lane." |
| 5 | **Legal / professional-services search** | High fees, conservative, craft-rich/BD-poor | "Placing partners is your craft. Winning the *firms* that need them is the part most search shops never systematize." |
| 6 | **Sales / GTM / revenue-leadership search** | They'll instantly get the offer (they place the exact roles) | "You place VPs of Sales — you know a real outbound motion when you see one. Here's one running on your own client side." |
| 7 | **Boutique generalist retained search** (5–25 ppl) | Owner does BD, broad, lots of them | "Retained search lives on the next mandate. We keep client-side pipeline moving so it's never just referrals." |
| 8 | **Newly-independent search principals** (left a big firm) | Hungry, no BD infrastructure yet, building from zero | "Going independent means you ARE the business-development engine now. We run that lane so you can run searches." |

> Note: #6 (GTM/sales-leadership search) is the *warmest cold* archetype — they place the roles the offer is about, so they grok it instantly and can become referrers even if they don't buy. Consider starting there.

## 4. Prioritization — rank into 3 tiers

Score each 0–2 on: **Fit** (retained/perm, right size, deal value clears retainer), **Signal** (a real "why-now" BD tell beyond the macro), **Reachability** (MP active on LinkedIn + email findable), **Vertical-warmth** (a niche Ryan can speak to credibly, e.g. GTM/sales search #6).

- **Tier A (send first):** 6–8/8 with a specific signal. ~10–15 firms. Start with archetype #6 (sales/GTM search) + anyone with a live BD-hire signal.
- **Tier B:** 4–5/8. The bulk.
- **Tier C (nurture/skip):** ≤3/8 — re-qualify or drop. Don't pad to 50 with junk.

> Rule (from the offer itself): **no signal, no send.** The segment-wide macro shift counts as a baseline signal, but a firm-specific tell (BD hire, slow-market post) moves them to Tier A.

## 8. What NOT to do (anti-patterns)
- ❌ Don't pitch **candidate sourcing** — it's their strength, not the gap. Client-side BD only.
- ❌ Don't target **high-volume temp staffing** — margins too thin to clear the retainer.
- ❌ Don't buy a 5,000-row list and blast it — that's the spray-and-pray the offer sells against; torches the domain.
- ❌ Don't target lead-gen/sales agencies or AAA peers — competitors.
- ❌ Don't pad to exactly 50 with low-fit names. 30 sharp search firms beat 50 with 20 tire-kickers.
- ❌ Don't enrich all 50 before sending any. Research Tier A, send, learn, then enrich the rest (mirrors the weekly loop).
- ⚠️ Don't treat "low saturation" as proven — `01c` flags it as a hypothesis. If search firms turn out heavily pitched, the pain + economics still hold; just expect more competition in the inbox and lean harder on the Loom differentiator.
