## Core positioning

Dealthreads turns a dead B2B contact form into an AI buyer-profile engine.

The simple promise:

> Your form should not just collect a name and email. It should give your rep the buyer profile before the callback.

The buyer does not buy "AI chat." They buy:

- less manual research per inbound lead
- faster, sharper callbacks
- cleaner CRM context
- better lead prioritization
- fewer good-fit buyers cooling off in the gap between form submit and rep follow-up

## First ICP

Start with small, founder-led B2B companies, roughly 5-40 people, selling high-ACV products or services.

Best early-fit traits:

- $8k+ deal size or $3k+/mo retainers
- real "request demo" or "contact sales" form
- founder/small team still handles inbound personally
- no mature RevOps team
- sales-led motion
- enough traffic that form quality matters
- buyer research currently happens manually after the form submit

Best first verticals:

1. vertical SaaS
2. freight/logistics software
3. B2B services with demo/contact forms
4. fintech/regtech SaaS if privacy claims are handled carefully

Avoid for now:

- tiny businesses with no inbound volume
- PLG companies where the form is not a meaningful sales path
- enterprise companies with procurement/security complexity
- teams that already have mature routing/enrichment and no urgency

## Content pillars

### 1. The dead contact form problem

Teach that the issue is not "forms are ugly." The issue is that the form throws away buyer context, then makes the sales team rebuild it manually.

Example angles:

- "Your form collects a lead. Your rep still has to build the buyer profile."
- "The buyer gave you a buying signal. Your CRM got a name, email, and a shrug."
- "The cost is not the form. The cost is the 20 minutes after the form."

### 2. Buyer profile education

Teach what should exist before the first callback.

Fields to repeat often:

- company summary
- company size
- industry and segment
- buyer role/seniority
- ICP fit
- funding/growth signals where available
- tech stack clues
- intent/budget/timeline
- likely pain
- recommended next question
- CRM owner and next action

### 3. Teardown-led proof

Before case studies exist, use finished teardowns as proof.

The recurring content format:

1. show the current form
2. show what it captures
3. show what sales still has to research
4. show the AI-built buyer profile
5. explain what the rep can do differently now

### 4. RevOps-lite for small B2B teams

Make the message founder-friendly:

> You do not need enterprise RevOps to stop calling leads blind.

This keeps the wedge away from giant enterprise chat tools and toward founder-led teams with acute pain.

### 5. Trust, accuracy, and implementation

AI in sales workflows creates immediate trust concerns. Address them directly.

Recurring positions:

- unknown fields should say unknown
- show sources when possible
- do not fake enrichment certainty
- start with HubSpot/manual CRM sync before overbuilding
- beta clients get managed install, not a dashboard maze

### 6. Building in public

Use Ryan's voice and proof-on-screen style.

Good formats:

- "I rebuilt a dead contact form into a buyer profile engine."
- "Here is what broke when I tested it."
- "Here is the field I thought mattered that reps probably do not care about."
- "This is why boring AI workflows are the best ones."

## Content mix

Recommended mix for first 90 days:

- 45% educational/problem teaching
- 20% teardown/proof content
- 15% product education
- 10% founder/build-in-public
- 10% conversion/beta CTA

This is intentionally more educational than promotional. The market needs the category explained before it buys.

## Production workflow

### Weekly planning

Every Friday afternoon:

1. Review top posts, replies, comments, teardown requests, and booked calls.
2. Pull 3-5 real prospect questions or objections.
3. Pick next week's teardown target(s).
4. Choose one lead magnet or proof asset to improve.
5. Queue posts for Monday-Wednesday. Leave Thursday/Friday flexible for live learnings.

### Daily workflow

Morning:

- publish one primary post
- comment on 5 relevant posts
- send 3-5 thoughtful connection requests to ICP prospects
- log comments/replies/questions

Midday:

- record or clip one visual proof asset if scheduled
- respond to comments and DMs
- invite qualified engagers to send their form URL

Evening:

- update the teardown/proof library
- write down one insight from buyer conversations
- turn it into a future post seed

### Quality control checklist

Before publishing:

- Does this teach the dead-form problem or buyer-profile solution?
- Is there a specific example, screenshot, or scenario?
- Does it avoid fake results?
- Is the CTA singular?
- Would Ryan actually say it out loud?
- Does it make Dealthreads feel practical, not like AI agency slop?

## Monthly review cadence

### Month 1 metrics

Goal: learn which problem language creates qualified conversations.

Track:

- profile views
- comments from ICP people
- teardown requests
- form URLs received
- booked teardown calls
- best problem phrases

### Month 2 metrics

Goal: prove the teardown format creates trust.

Track:

- teardown completion rate
- Loom watches/replies
- discovery call conversion
- most common objections
- most compelling before/after screenshots

### Month 3 metrics

Goal: close beta installs.

Track:

- beta applications
- proposals sent
- paid installs
- setup revenue
- monthly recurring revenue
- proof assets created from beta work

## First 14 days execution priority

If time gets tight, do not try to execute all 90 days perfectly. Do this first:

1. Publish 5 LinkedIn posts around the dead contact form problem.
2. Record 2 teardown videos from real public B2B forms.
3. Build the Buyer Profile Checklist lead magnet.
4. Send 25 targeted LinkedIn messages with a finished teardown angle.
5. Ask every qualified person: "Want me to show what your form is not telling your rep?"

The calendar is the map. The teardown call is the money path.
