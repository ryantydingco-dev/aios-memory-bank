## HOUSE TITLE STYLE (from Ryan 2026-07-21: "none of the clickbaity fucking titles" — this overrides the packaging tactics above)

Titles read like a lab notebook, not a movie trailer. The anti-hype IS the thumbnail strategy: deadpan sentences stand out in a feed of shouting.

Rules:
- No caps-lock words, no "KILLED / DESTROYS / SHOCKING / INSANE", no fake urgency, no withheld secrets ("...you won't believe what happened").
- Numbers welcome, as flat facts: "I sent 4,278 cold emails last week. 26 replied." The honesty is the hook.
- Verdicts allowed in the title, including underwhelming ones: "it mostly works", "I'm cancelling it", "not worth it yet".
- Curiosity comes from SPECIFICITY (a roofing company in a staffing list) never from withholding.
- Understatement over promise, always. If a title feels like it oversells by 5%, cut it back 10%.
- Tradeoff accepted knowingly: plain titles may cost some CTR early. They buy the only thing that converts staffing owners: trust. This is the brand, not a tactic.

Episode titles, house style:
1. "I built an AI sales team. Here's the honest tour."
2. "I sent 4,278 cold emails last week. 26 replied."
3. "My AI finds companies hiring salespeople. I call them first."
4. "The LinkedIn play every guru recommends cost me $199 per lead."
5. "A roofing company ended up in my staffing lead list."
6. "75 cold calls with AI-written call notes. Here's what happened."
7. "My cold email campaigns improve themselves weekly. With my approval."
8. "Watch AI build a client list for a staffing firm in 20 minutes."
9. "I mined a 25-year-old customer database. 981 win-back leads."
10. "AI replaced our $100-per-job artist. The owner had feelings about it."
11. "My whole company fits on one phone screen at 7:10 every morning."
12. "Every AI tool I pay for, the full bill, and what I'd cancel."
2. Two audiences, one channel, deliberately: staffing owners (buyers, search-intent titles) AND AI-operator audience (the formats above). The AI-operator videos build the audience; the staffing videos convert it. Same split the big channels run (course videos build, "how I sold" videos convert).
3. The reaction lane is the growth hack for a zero-subscriber channel: breakouts don't need an audience.
4. The mega-course is the 90-day flag-plant. Do not build it now; assemble it from episodes.

## The editorial constitution (from Ryan, 2026-07-21 — this outranks every format above)

The channel's position: **anti-hype, verdict-driven, real business or it doesn't air.** The space is clickbait selling dreams; Ryan is the guy who pays for the hype so the viewer doesn't have to, and publishes the verdict either way.

1. **Every claim has a receipt on screen.** If the number can't be shown live in a dashboard, it doesn't go in the video or the title.
2. **Verdicts, not pitches.** Every tool/play covered ends with: does it work, what it cost, would I keep paying. "No" is a valid and frequent verdict — the "no" videos build more trust than the wins.
3. **Formats yes, clickbait no.** The proven packaging (dollar titles, time-compression) is allowed ONLY when the number is real and the video delivers it. "I paid $200 per lead" is a compliant title; "This AI makes $10K/day" is banned forever.
4. **The shiny-object story is core canon, not a secret.** Ryan chased Invoice Chase, Oloxa, Meeting Engine, Runvue and more before Dealthreads. That pattern — new toy, dream of money, no business built — is the viewer's pattern. Name it, own it, warn about it. It is the channel's origin story.
5. **Never sell the dream.** The honest pitch: this is hard, replies are ~1%, most experiments fail, and the machine still wins because it compounds. Buyers (staffing owners with real money) trust slow honesty over fast hype; dreamers can go watch someone else.

### The recurring segment this creates: "Does It Actually Work?"
Take one hyped play or tool per episode (or standalone episodes), run it in the real business with real money, publish cost + result + verdict.

Future candidates: AI SDR tools, buying-intent data vendors, "AI personalization" at scale, done-for-you inboxes, Apollo vs owned scraping, every shiny thing Ryan is tempted by — the temptation itself becomes the content pipeline, which is the ultimate anti-shiny-object defense: now the new toy has to EARN its keep on camera.
