## The chosen beachhead: Agencies & consultants selling high-ticket B2B services

**Pick this and sell it this week.** It's the highest-scoring ICP *and* it's highest on the two factors that should decide a first beachhead. The other axes don't force a tradeoff — pain, budget, and urgency are all High too — but proof and delivery are where this one separates from the pack.

**Why it wins on proof speed (the #1 constraint):**
- **The deal math forgives low reply volume.** Honest cold reply rates are 1–5% (~300 emails per meeting). For most ICPs that means you need real volume before the signal is convincing inside 30 days. Here, engagements are $10k–$100k+, so *a handful of qualified conversations is ROI on its own*. You don't need a flood — you need 3–5 right-fit conversations, which is deliverable in a month.
- **They judge quality the way the engine measures it.** This buyer lives on consultative outreach and conversation quality — exactly what Dealthreads instruments (response *and* conversation quality, not vanity reply rate). The proof artifact (research cards + weekly reviews) maps 1:1 onto how they already think. No education tax on "why conversation quality matters."
- **The proof is attributable and clean.** Starting from a clear ICP with a fresh motion means a clean before/after. No inherited attribution fight (unlike ICP #6), no rep self-sourcing to argue against (unlike ICP #3).

**Why it wins on 30-day delivery feasibility:**
- **The buyer is the decision-maker.** Founder/principal buys and approves — no multi-stakeholder loop, no finance gate, no rep buy-in. Fastest path from "yes" to "first message out."
- **LinkedIn + Loom is native to how they already sell.** Zero channel-adoption friction. The wedge channel *is* their channel.
- **English-speaking, on-LinkedIn, easy to enrich.** TAM-building and research-card generation run smoothly; onboarding isn't bottlenecked on messy data or a non-native channel (the exact things that sink ICPs #5 and #6).

**Why pain/budget/urgency also line up (no tradeoff):**
- **Pain:** Lumpy, referral-/network-dependent pipeline is a felt, recurring wound. Feast-or-famine is personal — they feel it every slow month. This is the literal "founder GTM chaos" the engine removes.
- **Budget:** One closed deal pays for 6–12 months of the Growth tier (~$3.5k/mo). They already buy intangible expert services, so a GTM retainer is a familiar, low-friction purchase.
- **Urgency:** A dry quarter creates immediate buying intent. This is a bleeding-now problem, not a someday-problem.

**Strategic bonus — the proof compounds.** Win #1 here gives Dealthreads a case study in the *exact* category that buys consulting-style retainers most easily. That reference then unlocks ICPs #2 and #4 far faster than starting anywhere else.

## Disqualified-for-now (and exactly why)

These aren't bad markets — they're wrong-*first*. Each fails on proof speed or delivery feasibility, the two things pilot #1 cannot afford to compromise.

**❌ #6 — Companies running LinkedIn outbound poorly (the seductive trap).**
Looks ideal because the channel matches the wedge. It's the *hardest* place to manufacture first proof: you inherit a throttled/poisoned profile, an audience already fatigued by their bad messaging, and a brutal attribution problem — every lift gets argued against their baseline, every miss gets blamed on you. Proving *incremental* value against an active-but-bad incumbent is harder than proving value from zero. **What NOT to build:** do not make "fix broken LinkedIn motions" the headline beachhead. Revisit as ICP #2+ *after* a clean-slate case study exists.

**❌ #5 — Vertical B2B with messy prospecting (off-wedge).**
The TAM/enrichment fix is real, but these buyers often aren't LinkedIn-active, which blunts the LinkedIn+Loom wedge and pushes toward email/call/text where Ryan's differentiated motion is weaker. Messy-data onboarding (cleanup, vertical context-building) also eats the 30-day window. A win is possible but slow and off-channel — wrong shape for first proof. **What NOT to build:** don't sell "verticals" generically; if/when you enter, pick ONE vertical (real estate is the warm option given origin) and treat it as a focused expansion, not the beachhead.

**⏳ #3 — Small sales teams without SDR/RevOps (great later, slow now).**
Best *budget* conversation of all six (~$90k/yr cheaper than an SDR hire, live week one). But multi-stakeholder buying (sales leader + ops + sometimes finance) slows the sale, and rep self-sourcing creates attribution debates that muddy the 30-day story. Strong second-wave target once you have a reference and a tighter sales process — not the fastest first proof.

**⏳ #2 — B2B SaaS founders (close call, deferred on math).**
Scores an 8 and the Oloxa proof is most analogous here. Deferred only because the low-ACV cohort breaks the 30-day proof math (needs volume before signal is convincing) and requires hard qualification on ability to pay. **Pursue in parallel, but only mid-to-high-ACV founders** where a few conversations = clear ROI; skip pre-revenue/bootstrapped.

**⏳ #4 — Companies with case studies, weak pipeline (best #2, by design).**
Highest ease-of-proof outside the winner — existing case studies are rocket fuel you plug straight into LinkedIn+Loom openers, lifting reply quality immediately. Held at #2 only because urgency is chronic-tolerated rather than acute, and they may expect outbound to be "cheap" vs content (needs an economics reframe). **This is your designated second beachhead** — start prospecting it the moment pilot #1 is live.

## Winner's outreach channel (make it concrete + trackable)

**Primary: LinkedIn + Loom from Ryan's own profile.** Founder-to-founder, warm, peer tone. This is the wedge Ryan already runs on Oloxa — same muscle, new vertical.

**The motion (per account):**
1. **Connection request** — no pitch. One line referencing something specific (a post, their niche, a recent win).
2. **On accept → first DM** — name the wound in their language ("pipeline goes quiet between referrals"), one sentence on the engine, soft ask to send a 90-second Loom. No link dump.
3. **Personalized Loom (60–90s)** — screen-share *their* world: a draft research card for one of their likely target accounts (company, contact, signal, evidence, pain hypothesis, personalized opener). Show, don't tell. End on a low-friction CTA ("worth 15 min to see the weekly loop?").
4. **Structured follow-up** — 2–3 touches over ~10 days, each adding value (a second angle, a relevant proof artifact), never "just bumping this."
5. **Optional secondary channel:** email *only* if you have a strong reason and a clean address — keep it low-volume, high-relevance (post-Feb-2024 deliverability rules punish blast; the premium wedge is human-QA'd, not volume).

**What to track from day one (conversation quality, not vanity):**
- Connection accept rate → DM reply rate → Loom view rate → **qualified-conversation rate** (the metric that matters).
- Tag every reply by quality (cold / curious / qualified / booked), not just yes/no.
- Log it all in research cards + the weekly review so the *artifacts themselves* become the proof you show the next prospect.

## First offer angle (sell it this week)

**Anchor the Growth tier (~$3,500/mo). Setup $1,500–$2,500, billed separately. Frame as a 90-day pilot.**

> *"I run this exact GTM engine on my own company, live — I'm client #0. Let me stand up a weekly experiment loop on your top 100 accounts: human-approved LinkedIn + Loom outreach, research cards you can actually see, and weekly reviews on what's converting and what's not. You stop depending on referrals and your next slow quarter, and start learning what outbound actually works for *your* offer — with me running it, not a junior SDR you'd wait 3–4 months to ramp."*

**Make these moves in the pitch:**
- **Sell economics + targeting + learning speed — never guaranteed revenue or meeting floods.** Honest framing (1–5% reply, ~300 emails/meeting) *builds* trust with this buyer and separates you from the spray-and-pray crowd.
- **Lead with founder-run credibility.** "Same engine I run on Oloxa" is your strongest asset with no external case study. Show real artifacts (research cards, a weekly review) on the call — that *is* the proof.
- **Anchor against the SDR hire:** live week one vs a 3–4 month ramp; ~$90k/yr cheaper than a fully-loaded US SDR; ~1.5yr average SDR tenure means you're also de-risking turnover.
- **Human approval = the on-brand, non-spammy guarantee.** They approve every message. That's what protects a high-ticket brand and what cheap automation can't offer.

## Honest critique — weak assumptions + what NOT to build yet

- **"Agencies" is still too broad — narrow it before you prospect.** Pick a sub-slice you can speak to credibly and enrich cleanly (e.g. *boutique B2B marketing/growth agencies* or *fractional-exec / specialist consultancies, $10k–$100k engagements*). One tight list beats a generic "agencies" universe.
- **Don't over-engineer the product before pilot #1.** No fancy multi-agent naming, no dashboard build-out, no extra channels. The MVP is: tight ICP list → research cards → human-approved LinkedIn+Loom → weekly review. Ship that, learn, then add.
- **Don't promise pipeline volume.** With this ICP the win is *qualified conversations that justify a $10k–$100k deal*, not reply-rate vanity. If you anchor on volume you'll lose on honest math; anchor on economics and learning speed and you win.
- **Don't start with ICPs #5 or #6 because the channel "matches" or the niche is familiar.** Both actively undermine first-proof (attribution baggage / off-wedge channel). Resist the pull.
- **The pilot's real deliverable is a case study.** Treat pilot #1 as proof-manufacturing: instrument everything, keep the artifacts clean, get a quote. That asset is what unlocks ICPs #2 and #4 — so optimize the pilot for *provability*, not just for the client's result.
