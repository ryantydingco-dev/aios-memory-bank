## Executive Summary

Deal Threads's funnel should not behave like a normal SaaS funnel yet. The product is early, high-trust, implementation-sensitive, and sold to founder-led or RevOps-light B2B teams. The funnel has to create proof before it asks for money.

The core funnel:

```text
Content / LinkedIn / cold email / partner intro
        -> Dead Form Teardown request
        -> custom teardown page + Loom
        -> 15-minute install scoping call
        -> pay-when-it-works install
        -> enriched profiles landing in CRM
        -> paid Founder Install / Growth Managed
        -> reports, routing, ICP campaigns, expansion
```

The funnel should feel helpful at every step. The prospect should never feel pushed into buying software they do not understand. They should feel like someone looked at their actual form, showed a real gap, and offered to install a lightweight workflow that fixes it.

Create recognition of the "dead contact form" problem.

"Your contact form gives sales a name and email. It should give them the buyer profile."

Primary traffic channels:

- LinkedIn founder-led content.
- targeted LinkedIn DMs.
- cold email to high-fit founder-led B2B companies.
- webinar invitations.
- partner introductions from HubSpot/Webflow/RevOps consultants.
- SEO/blog posts over time.

Content themes:

- hidden cost of dead contact forms.
- raw lead versus buyer profile.
- speed-to-lead versus prepared follow-up.
- why good and bad leads look identical in CRM.

Traffic generation tactics:

- 5 LinkedIn posts/week from the 90-day content calendar.
- 5-10 targeted comments/day on ICP-relevant posts.
- 40-60 LinkedIn touches/week.
- 50-150 cold emails/week after domain warmup.

Awareness KPIs:

Goal:

Show the buyer exactly what Deal Threads would do for their form and earn an install scoping call.

Custom Dead Form Teardown.

1. Current form snapshot.
2. What the form captures today.

5. CRM handoff recommendation.
6. Honest unknowns and data gaps.
7. Install recommendation: install, audit, or wait.

Delivery format:

- custom Loom, 2-5 minutes.
- teardown page or PDF.
- short email summary.
- CTA to 15-minute scoping call.

Consideration KPIs:

| Conversion | Target |

| delivered teardown to call booked | 25-40% |

### Decision Stage

Goal:

Remove implementation risk and close a scoped install.

Primary decision assets:

- live demo.
- install scope.
- pay-when-it-works terms.
- HubSpot access checklist.
- CRM field map.
- FAQ/objection guide.
- beta success definition.

Decision offer:

Founder Install:

- USD 2K setup.
- USD 1K/month.

Growth Managed:

- USD 2.5K setup.
- USD 2.5K/month.

Decision-stage conversion tactics:

- start with one form and one CRM.
- sync notes before complex custom fields.
- use a controlled production test.
- avoid long contracts for first proof clients.
- make access requirements clear.
- define "working" as enriched buyer profiles landing in CRM.

Decision KPIs:

- proposal-to-approval rate.
- time from call to approval.
- access handoff time.
- install start rate.
- install completion rate.

### Activation Stage

Goal:

Get the first enriched profile into the client's CRM and prove usefulness.

Activation milestones:

1. Access received.
2. Form/page selected.
3. CRM connection configured.
4. profile schema approved.
5. test submission complete.
6. first enriched buyer profile lands.
7. sales owner reviews and confirms usefulness.

Activation success definition:

- widget or form workflow is live on approved page.
- qualified submissions create a readable buyer profile.
- profile lands in HubSpot or approved CRM.
- sales/founder can use profile for follow-up without manual research.
- unknown fields are labeled, not invented.

Activation KPIs:

- days from approval to access.
- days from access to first profile.
- CRM sync success rate.
- profile usefulness score.
- support hours per client.

### Retention Stage

Goal:

Turn "it works" into ongoing value and paid retention.

First 30-day retention sequence:

- Week 1: install and test.
- Week 2: review first profiles and tune questions.
- Week 3: review profile completeness and rep usefulness.
- Week 4: baseline report: leads processed, high-fit leads, data gaps, next tuning.

Ongoing retention activities:

- monthly optimization report.
- profile quality review.
- enrichment miss review.
- conversion and follow-up review.
- field/question tuning.
- routing or alert improvements.

Retention KPIs:

- active profiles/month.
- profile usefulness score.
- rep/founder usage.
- client-reported time saved.
- paid conversion after proof period.
- churn risk.
- expansion interest.

### Expansion Stage

Goal:

Move valuable clients from one workflow to broader inbound intelligence.

Expansion offers:

- additional forms/pages.
- high-priority Slack/email alerts.
- monthly intelligence reports.
- ICP gap reports.
- outbound campaigns based on inbound patterns.
- Salesforce/deeper CRM implementation.
- partner/client white-label reporting.

Expansion triggers:

- more than 50 enriched profiles/month.
- multiple forms need coverage.
- sales team asks for routing.
- founder asks "what else can this automate?"
- marketing wants segment insights.
- CRM operator wants data quality reporting.

## 2. Core Funnel Paths

### Funnel Path A: Proof-Led Outbound

Best for:

Founder-led B2B companies with visible demo/contact forms.

Flow:

```text
Target account list
  -> LinkedIn view/comment/connect
  -> "I made / can make you a teardown"
  -> form URL confirmed
  -> teardown + Loom delivered
  -> 15-min install scoping call
  -> pay-when-it-works install
```

Target activity:

- 25 high-fit targets/week.
- 10-15 personalized LinkedIn touches/week.
- 10-25 cold email touches/week.
- 3-5 teardowns/week.

Expected performance:

- 10-25% positive reply from high-fit personalized targets.
- 25-40% teardown-to-call.
- 20-35% call-to-install.

### Funnel Path B: Webinar Funnel

Best for:

Warm audience, content audience, partner audiences, founder communities.

Flow:

```text
LinkedIn/email invite
  -> webinar registration
  -> live session / replay
  -> free teardown CTA
  -> teardown delivered
  -> install scoping call
```

Expected performance:

- 25-50 registrants first session.
- 35-50% attendance.
- 35-50% attendee-to-teardown request.
- 20-40% teardown-to-call.

### Funnel Path C: Content/Search Funnel

Best for:

Longer-term inbound authority and sales collateral.

Flow:

```text
LinkedIn/blog/video
  -> Dead Form Audit Kit
  -> email nurture
  -> webinar or teardown request
  -> install call
```

Expected performance:

- slower than outbound.
- higher trust over time.
- strongest once proof examples exist.

### Funnel Path D: Partner Funnel

Best for:

HubSpot consultants, Webflow agencies, RevOps consultants, GTM advisors.

Flow:

```text
Partner outreach
  -> partner demo
  -> free teardown for one client
  -> client intro
  -> teardown + install scope
  -> referral fee / co-branded install
```

Expected performance:

- low volume.
- higher trust.
- slower activation.
- strong long-term leverage.

### Lead Scoring

Score leads out of 100.

Firmographic fit: 30 points.

- B2B: 10.
- 5-150 employees: 10.
- high-ticket deal or high LTV: 10.

Inbound fit: 25 points.

- has demo/contact form: 10.
- gets meaningful monthly form submissions: 10.
- sales-led follow-up: 5.

Ops pain: 20 points.

- manual research today: 10.
- thin CRM records: 5.
- slow follow-up concern: 5.

Implementation fit: 15 points.

- HubSpot or simple CRM: 8.
- can install script or change form: 5.
- decision-maker involved: 2.

Urgency: 10 points.

- wants fix this month/quarter: 5.
- recent tool change, hiring, or inbound push: 5.

Priority:

- 80-100: high priority, offer install call quickly.
- 60-79: teardown and nurture.
- 40-59: audit/webinar nurture.
- below 40: low priority or not a fit.

### Sales-Ready Lead Definition

A lead is sales-ready when:

- they submitted a form URL or attended webinar and requested teardown.
- they sell B2B high-value products/services.
- they have a current inbound form.
- they can identify CRM or follow-up process.

## 6. Sales Process Optimization

### Discovery Framework

Use a modified BANT plus implementation fit.

Budget:

- "What is one qualified inbound lead worth?"
- "Have you considered hiring SDR/ops help for this?"
- "Would USD 1K-2.5K/month make sense if this improves follow-up?"

Authority:

- "Who owns the website form?"
- "Who owns CRM fields?"
- "Who decides whether this goes live?"

Need:

- "What happens after a form submit today?"
- "Who researches the lead?"
- "What context is usually missing?"
- "Which leads get mishandled?"

Timeline:

- "Is this a now problem or a later cleanup?"
- "Any campaign, launch, or lead flow increasing soon?"

Implementation:

- "What CRM do you use?"
- "Can you add a script or modify the form?"
- "Do you want a note-only first install or structured fields?"

### 15-Minute Install Scoping Call

Agenda:

1. Confirm current form workflow.
2. Review teardown finding.
3. Confirm CRM and access path.
4. Define first install scope.
5. Confirm success definition.
6. Explain pay-when-it-works terms.
7. Ask for approval and access checklist.

Talk track:

"The goal is not to build a big platform. It is to prove one thing: when someone submits this form, can your sales team open a useful buyer profile before follow-up?"

### Sales Conversation Framework

#### Initial Contact

- lead with the form gap.
- reference their actual form if possible.
- avoid pitching software.
- ask permission to send teardown.

#### Needs Discovery

- understand current follow-up.
- quantify lead value.
- identify manual research.
- identify CRM handoff weakness.

#### Solution Presentation

- show before/after.
- show CRM note.
- explain one-form install.
- show unknowns.
- frame AI as prep layer, not rep replacement.

#### Objection Handling

Common objections:

- "We do not get enough inbound."
- "HubSpot can already do this."
- "Can we build it in Clay?"
- "Will AI hallucinate?"
- "Will this reduce form conversion?"
- "We are too early."
- "I do not want a chatbot."
- "What if enrichment is wrong?"

Response patterns:

- agree first.
- narrow the scope.
- show proof.
- separate data from workflow.
- use pay-when-it-works for trust.

#### Close

Closing question:

"Is there any reason not to test this on one form and only start billing once profiles are landing?"

Next step:

- access checklist.
- target form.
- CRM admin path.
- test submission.

| Bottleneck | Diagnosis | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Low traffic | not enough daily distribution | increase founder outbound and partner asks |
| Low teardown requests | weak CTA or broad audience | sharpen "send form URL" and target only form-heavy B2B |
| Low call bookings | teardown not specific enough | improve Loom specificity and install recommendation |
| Low install approvals | trust/implementation risk | use pay-when-it-works and simpler one-form scope |
| Slow activation | access friction | use checklist and note-only first sync |
| Low paid conversion | profiles not useful | tune questions, summary, enrichment, and CRM output |

## 11. Personalization Strategy

### Behavioral Personalization

If prospect attends webinar:

- send teardown CTA.
- mention the live demo moment.

If prospect downloads audit kit:

- ask for their audit score.
- offer free teardown.

If prospect submits form URL:

- personalize around their actual form.
- deliver teardown quickly.

If prospect views pricing/offer:

- send install scope CTA.

If prospect stalls after call:

- ask which blocker applies: access, accuracy, budget, timing, priority.

### Segment Personalization

Founder-led:

- emphasize founder time, calling blind, no need to hire RevOps.

RevOps/GTM:

- emphasize CRM cleanliness, field mapping, routing, data confidence.

Partner:

- emphasize client add-on, easy implementation, referral revenue.

Drift/Qualified replacement:

- emphasize conversational front door replacement without enterprise rollout.

## Strategic Guardrails

- The funnel's job is not to maximize email subscribers. It is to get qualified form URLs.
- The teardown is the bridge between curiosity and trust.
- Do not sell the install before showing the gap.
- Do not over-automate the funnel before the manual motion converts.
- Keep the first install narrow: one form, one CRM, one profile workflow.
- Measure profile usefulness before promising revenue outcomes.
- Use pay-when-it-works to reduce trust risk, not to lower price.

The best version of this funnel makes the buyer think:

"They looked at my actual form, showed me what sales is missing, and offered a safe way to fix it."
