## Executive Summary

The first 90 days of content should not try to make Deal Threads famous. It should make the target buyer feel one sharp thing:

> Our contact form is giving sales a lead, but not a buyer profile.

The content system should create problem awareness, teach the buyer-profile framework, show before/after proof, drive webinar registrations, and convert interested operators into free Dead Form Teardowns.

The content calendar is built around one commercial motion:

1. Teach the "dead contact form" problem.
2. Show what a buyer profile should contain.
3. Demonstrate Deal Threads with real/simulated form teardowns.
4. Invite people to the AI enrichment webinar.
5. Convert them into free teardowns.
6. Convert qualified teardowns into install calls.

Content should be practical, founder-led, concrete, and proof-on-screen. Avoid generic AI automation content. The category should feel like a boring, valuable revenue workflow, not a shiny chatbot.

## 1. Content Strategy Framework

### Core Content Pillars

#### Pillar 1: The Dead Contact Form Problem

Purpose: create problem awareness.

Core idea:

Most forms capture contact info but fail to prepare sales for a better first conversation.

Repeatable angles:

- Your form got the lead. Your rep still got homework.
- A serious buyer and a tire-kicker look identical in most CRMs.
- The cost is not the form. The cost is the 20 minutes after the form.
- If the first callback starts with basic research questions, the form did not do enough.

Primary CTA:

Request a free Dead Form Teardown.

#### Pillar 2: Buyer Profile Education

Purpose: teach the desired future state.

Core idea:

Every inbound lead should become a buyer profile before sales follows up.

Buyer profile fields to teach repeatedly:

- business need.
- urgency/timeline.
- budget or buying stage.
- role and authority.
- company size and industry.
- tech stack and CRM.
- funding, hiring, or growth signals.
- ICP fit.
- likely objections or watchouts.
- recommended opener.

Primary CTA:

Download or complete the Dead Form Audit.

#### Pillar 3: Teardown-Led Proof

Purpose: replace case studies before case studies exist.

Core idea:

Show a real or simulated form fill, then show the enriched buyer dossier.

Recurring format:

1. current form.
2. raw lead.
3. missing context.
4. AI-built buyer profile.
5. what the rep can do differently.
6. what remains unknown.

Primary CTA:

Send me your form URL.

#### Pillar 4: RevOps-Lite For Founder-Led B2B

Purpose: make the solution feel built for the first buyer.

Core idea:

Small B2B teams can have grown-up inbound follow-up without hiring RevOps or buying enterprise tools.

Repeatable angles:

- Founder-led inbound should not mean founder-built research on every lead.
- You do not need a giant platform to stop calling leads blind.
- The first RevOps workflow to automate is the form-to-CRM handoff.
- Install one workflow before hiring another person.

Primary CTA:

Join the AI enrichment webinar.

#### Pillar 5: Trust, Accuracy, And AI Guardrails

Purpose: neutralize fear of AI in CRM.

Core idea:

AI enrichment only works if it marks uncertainty honestly.

Recurring positions:

- Unknown fields should stay unknown.
- Visitor-provided facts, enriched facts, and AI inferences must be separate.
- CRM fields should not be overwritten casually.
- Start with notes before complex automation.
- AI should prepare reps, not impersonate them.

Primary CTA:

Watch the demo or webinar.

#### Pillar 6: Product Education And Offer Conversion

Purpose: turn interest into pipeline.

Core idea:

Deal Threads is a managed workflow, not a generic tool.

Repeatable angles:

- free Dead Form Teardown.
- USD 500 Dead Form Audit.
- Founder Install.
- Growth Managed.
- pay-when-it-works proof-client terms.
- one form, one CRM, one buyer-profile workflow.

Primary CTA:

Book an install scoping call after teardown.

### Content Mix Formula

Recommended first-90-day mix:

| Type | Percent | Purpose |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Educational | 40% | Teach the dead form problem and buyer-profile framework. |
| Inspirational/proof | 20% | Show before/after teardowns, wins, and practical transformations. |
| Promotional/conversion | 20% | Drive webinar registrations, teardown requests, audits, and install calls. |
| Trust/behind-the-scenes | 15% | Build in public, show product decisions, address AI accuracy. |
| Community/engagement | 5% | Ask questions, polls, comment prompts, feedback loops. |

### Voice And Tone Guidelines

Brand personality:

- practical.
- direct.
- founder-to-founder.
- skeptical of hype.
- precise about what is known and unknown.
- generous with useful teardown insight.

Language to use:

- dead contact form.
- buyer profile.
- rep-ready dossier.
- form-to-CRM handoff.
- stop calling leads blind.
- unknown stays unknown.
- before the callback.
- founder-led inbound.
- one form, one CRM, one workflow.

Language to avoid:

- autonomous AI SDR.
- replaces your reps.
- guaranteed revenue.
- magic chatbot.
- always identifies everyone.
- set it and forget it.
- growth hack.

Quality standards:

- Every post should contain one useful idea.
- Every teardown should show the gap, not just claim it.
- Every AI claim should include an accuracy guardrail.
- Every promotional post should point to a concrete next step.
- Never imply the system knows facts it cannot verify.

## 5. Weekly Production Workflow

### Monday Planning

- Review previous week's metrics.
- Pick one long-form topic.
- Pick two teardown examples.
- Pick one product/trust topic.
- Schedule LinkedIn posts.
- Identify 10 target accounts to engage.

### Content Creation Workflow

1. Research and ideation.
2. Draft content brief.
3. Create long-form asset.
4. Extract 3-5 LinkedIn posts.
5. Record 1-2 short videos.
6. Create visual/screenshot.
7. Schedule posts.
8. Add CTA and tracking.
9. Review performance after 48 hours.

### Quality Control Checklist

Before publishing:

- Does this teach one clear idea?
- Does it speak to founder-led or RevOps-light B2B?
- Does it avoid generic AI hype?
- Does it point to a concrete next step?
- Are factual claims sourced or phrased as experience/hypothesis?
- Are AI accuracy limits stated where needed?
- Is the CTA aligned with the funnel stage?
- Can this be repurposed into email or sales collateral?

## 11. Strategic Guardrails

- Do not make the content too broad. The enemy is the dead B2B contact form.
- Do not over-teach AI. Teach the business workflow.
- Do not post generic founder motivation unless it ties back to inbound, CRM, or buyer profiles.
- Do not sell the install before the teardown.
- Do not chase vanity metrics at the expense of form URLs submitted.
- Do not promise revenue lifts before client proof.
- Do not hide the fact that the first version is a managed install.

The content works when a founder or GTM operator says:

"Our form does exactly this. Can you look at ours?"
