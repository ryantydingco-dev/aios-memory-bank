Who you're calling: agency owners and service business founders with money stuck in their AR aging report. They're the ones chasing invoices between client work, which means nobody's chasing them.

The one sentence to remember: **Stop chasing invoices. Stay the good guy.**

## Goal ladder (in order)

1. **Win:** they agree to the free AR audit. That means they send you their aging export from QuickBooks, Xero, or FreshBooks. Confirm their email on the call.
2. **Big win:** they want to talk more, book a 15 minute call.
3. **Only if they ask:** the Setup is $2,000 one time, live in about a week, recover 3x the fee in 30 days or pay nothing.
4. **Floor:** learn the objection, log it, hang up polite.

You're not closing $2,000 on a cold call. The free audit is the product today. One export, two minutes of their time, and you come back with a dollar figure. Everything funnels to that.

## Opener

Parallel dialer means a half second lag after pickup. They've already said hello, maybe twice. Say their name immediately, no "can you hear me," no apology.

> "Hey {Name}, it's Ryan. I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call. Want to give me 30 seconds, or do you want to hang up on me?"

Pause. Most laugh or say "go ahead." Then:

> "Quick question first. Off the top of your head, do you know how much is sitting past due in your aging report right now?"

Almost nobody does. Whatever they say, you're set up:

> "That's normal, and that's kind of the problem. We pulled that report at one creative agency and found $650,000 in unpaid invoices. Half of it collected inside 30 days, just from follow-up that didn't quit.
>
> I do free AR audits. You send me the aging export from QuickBooks, takes two minutes to pull, and I send back how much is recoverable and exactly where your follow-up broke. Want one?"

Stop talking. Silence does the closing.

**Why the question opener works:** it's not a pitch, it's a number they should know and don't. The gap between "I should know that" and "I have no idea" is the whole sale.

## Vertical fork: trades, roofing, restoration

About a third of your TOP150 connects will be trades owners, not agency founders. Same skeleton, different vocabulary. They don't say "aging report," they say "jobs I finished and haven't been paid for." Their debtors are GCs, property managers, and insurance carriers, and the amounts are bigger.

Opener question swap:

> "Quick question first. Off the top of your head, how much do people owe you right now on jobs you've already finished?"

Pitch swap:

> "That's normal. Commercial and insurance work pays slow, and nobody on your crew is sending the third reminder, because everyone's on a roof. We set up follow-up that starts the day the invoice is due, stays polite, in your company's name, and doesn't stop until it's paid. I do free audits: you send the open-invoice report from QuickBooks, I send back what's recoverable. Want one?"

Language swaps to remember mid-call:

| Agency version | Trades version |
|---|---|
| aging report | open invoices on finished jobs |
| clients | GCs, property managers, adjusters |
| your bookkeeper | your office manager |
| brand voice | in your company's name, professional |

The objections below all still work. "It'll hurt the relationship" becomes "I can't piss off the GC": same answer, the system stays polite and you stay the good guy, which protects the relationship better than the angry call you'd eventually make at day 90.

## If they engage

Discovery questions, in order of usefulness:

> "Who actually chases your unpaid invoices today?"

The answer is almost always "me" or "my bookkeeper, sort of." Either way:

> "Right. And when's the last time anyone sent a third follow-up on the same invoice? That's the one that gets paid, and it's the one that never gets written."

Second question if it's flowing:

> "What happens when a client hits 45 days past due? Honestly."

Most owners admit they do nothing because it feels awkward. That's your moment:

> "That awkwardness is the leak. The invoices were never the problem. The follow-up was."

## Objection handling

**The universal pattern: agree, reframe in one sentence, drop back to the smallest ask.** Never argue. Nobody can win an argument against a free audit.

### "Not interested."
> "Fair enough. One question before I go: is that because clients actually pay you on time, or because chasing them is nobody's job?"

If they pay on time: "That's rare, congrats. Mind if I send the audit info anyway? Worst case it confirms you're the exception."
If nobody's job: "That's every agency I talk to. The audit takes you two minutes and puts a dollar figure on it. Worth a look?"

### "We don't have an AR problem."
> "You might not. Most owners who say that haven't opened the aging report in a quarter. The audit is free and the worst outcome is I confirm you're right. Want the number either way?"

### "My bookkeeper handles that."
> "Keep them, this isn't their job being replaced. Bookkeepers record and reconcile, they don't send the awkward third reminder or escalate at day 14. Ask them when the last follow-up actually went out. Or skip the awkward conversation and let the audit show you."

### "Just send me an email."
> "Will do. It'll be two lines: how to pull your aging export, and what I send back. If the recoverable number comes back small, you never hear from me again. What's the best address?"

That's the exact outcome you wanted. Take the win.

### "Is this a cold call?"
> "Yep, one hundred percent. You can hang up, or give me 30 seconds and I'll tell you why I called {Agency} specifically."

### "I'm busy / bad time."
> "All good, I'll be 15 seconds. Free AR audit: you send one export, I send back how much unpaid money is recoverable. One agency found $650K. Want the email instructions? You don't even have to stay on the line."

### "I don't want some system nagging my clients. It'll hurt the relationship."
> "That's exactly why this exists. The reminders go out in your brand voice, polite, and they start the day the invoice is due, not after it's embarrassing. Clients pay systems faster than people, and there's no resentment because it's never personal. You stay the good guy. The system plays bad cop, gently."

This is the most important objection. If you only rehearse one, rehearse this.

### "QuickBooks already sends reminders."
> "It does, one generic nudge that everyone ignores. No escalation at 14 days, no flag when an invoice stalls and needs a phone call, no weekly digest of who still owes you. The QuickBooks reminder is a sticky note. This doesn't stop until the invoice is paid."

### "I can just do this myself."
> "You can, and you've been able to this whole time. When's the last follow-up you actually sent? That's not a discipline problem, it's a priority problem. Client work always wins, and it should. That's why this runs without you."

### "Sounds like a collections agency."
> "Opposite end of the spectrum. Collections takes 30 to 50 percent and torches the relationship. This is polite follow-up in your own voice, starting at day one, so invoices never get old enough to need collections."

### "I'm not sending you my financials."
> "Totally fair, and you wouldn't be. The aging export is invoice numbers, amounts, and days overdue. No bank info, no client conversations, nothing sensitive. Happy to sign an NDA first if that makes it easier."

### "How much does it cost?"
> "The audit is free, no strings. If the number's big enough to act on, the setup is $2,000 one time, live in about a week, wired into your billing tool. And it's guaranteed: recover three times the fee in 30 days or you pay nothing. But today I'm only asking for the export."

### "$2,000 is a lot."
> "Compare it to the aging report, not the bank balance. The agency I mentioned recovered $325K in 30 days. And the guarantee means the math has to work before I keep a dollar: 3x the fee back in 30 days or full refund."

### "We're too small for this."
> "Small is worse, honestly. At your size every late invoice hits payroll, and you're the only one who'd ever chase it. The audit's free, so the only question is whether the number matters to you."

### "Who is this / how'd you get my number?"
> "Ryan Tydingco, DealThreads. I research agencies with the profile where AR usually leaks, {Agency} fit, and here we are. That's the whole story."

## Voicemail (under 15 seconds)

> "Hey {Name}, Ryan from DealThreads. We found $650,000 in unpaid invoices at one creative agency, half collected in 30 days. I do free AR audits, takes one export from QuickBooks. I'll email you the details, subject line 'Free AR audit for {Agency}'. No need to call back."

Then actually send that email the same day. Voicemail plus email roughly doubles reply rates versus either alone. Record it once in Salesfinity and use voicemail drop so you're not saying it 40 times.

## Salesfinity dialer notes

- **Connect lag is real.** Lead with their name the instant you connect. Energy up from word one.
- **Don't pre-read between calls.** You can't deep-prep when multiple lines dial at once. The question opener works on any agency because nobody knows their aging number.
- **Call in 45 to 60 minute blocks.** Disposition with the quick buttons mid-block, do real logging after.
- **Best windows:** 8:30 to 10am and 4 to 5:30pm in the prospect's time zone. Owners answer outside meeting hours.
- **Tone:** slower than feels natural, downward inflection on the opener (statement, not plea), and a real pause after every ask.

| Booked a call | Calendar invite immediately |
| Soft no / pays on time | Nurture, follow up in 30 days |
| Hard no | Log the objection, move on |
| Voicemail dropped | Send the matching email today |
| No answer | Retry in 2 days, different window |

## After every block (10 minutes)

1. Send every promised email while the call is still warm. The export instructions email is one line; there's no excuse to batch it for tomorrow.
2. Log connects and objections. Whichever objection comes up most this week is the one to sharpen next.
