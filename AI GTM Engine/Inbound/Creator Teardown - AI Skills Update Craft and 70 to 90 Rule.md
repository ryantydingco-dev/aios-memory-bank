# Creator Teardown - AI Skills Update, Craft, and the 70 to 90 Rule

Video: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4TXaAnittHs

## Why This Video Matters For Ryan
This is a follow-up from a creator whose video went viral because he gave away real AI video skills. This video works because it deepens trust instead of just repeating the first hit.

Core lesson:

Giving away value gets attention. Going deeper after attention builds authority.

## Main Content Principle
AI gets people to 70%. Craft gets them to 90%.

The creator’s strongest line/idea:
- A pile of beautiful generations sitting on a timeline is not a film.
- The last 20% is editing, pacing, sound, color, structure, and taste.
- Anyone can get to 70 with tools. The 90 is still earned.

Ryan adaptation:
- AI can get a business workflow to 70%.
- The final 20% is judgment, context, trust, messaging, QA, and human taste.
- The point is not to remove the operator. The point is to make the operator dramatically more leveraged.

## Structure

### 1. Opens with momentum + humility
He references growth from 40 to 10,000 subscribers, but frames it with surprise and gratitude rather than guru energy.

### 2. Reassures the audience
He says the video is sponsored by no one and explains his ethics around sponsorships. This builds trust after a viral moment.

### 3. Gives a quick spectacle/demo
He shows cinematic AI characters and clips first, creating proof and curiosity.

### 4. Reveals the behind-the-scenes workflow
He explains how he creates AI characters inside his own footage:
- shoot empty/usable frame
- build character/outfit sheet
- script through Claude
- generate scene
- upscale
- mask/place in editing timeline

### 5. Teaches specific details
Examples:
- gray background beats white background for character references
- white backgrounds create bounce/shininess/plastic faces
- volumetric depth makes scenes feel photographed
- reference images must be uploaded in order
- camera operator shakiness helps realism
- 720p + Topaz can be enough

### 6. Ends with philosophy
AI is a brush, not the artist. Keep the human seam. Use AI to build worlds, not deceive people.

## Visual Style Observations
- Moody red/purple studio lighting
- Wide-angle lens close to creator, giving intimate/energetic feel
- AI characters composited into the room
- Jump cuts between creator, AI clips, editing timeline, generated portraits, prompt screens
- Dark cinematic aesthetic throughout
- Strong contrast between human creator chaos and polished AI output
- Visual proof appears constantly: timeline, UI, character sheets, prompt output, final scenes

## Humor Style
- AI characters interrupt him / roast him
- Self-aware jokes about being new, delirious, filming at 4am
- “Sponsored by no one” bit
- “Plastic no good” simple funny phrasing
- “Mama didn’t raise no AI slop” visual joke
- Honest imperfection: not pretending every output is amazing

## Why It Builds Authority
He does not just give inspiration. He gives operational detail.

High-authority moves:
- specific mistakes he made
- exact fixes
- technical reasons behind fixes
- workflow sequence
- proof screenshots
- philosophy/ethics
- free skills/assets

## Ryan Adaptation
Ryan should use this style for AI systems/business workflow content:

### The Ryan 70 to 90 Rule
AI can get the workflow to 70%:
- summarize leads
- draft openers
- score signals
- organize notes
- create tasks
- draft content

Human/operator craft gets it to 90%:
- taste
- judgment
- buyer context
- what not to say
- follow-up nuance
- trust
- positioning
- QA

Example line:
"AI gets you to 70. Taste, context, and actually knowing the business gets you to 90. The last 10 should probably stay human so we don’t accidentally build a haunted spreadsheet with WiFi."

## What To Steal
- follow-up content that deepens value after a viral hit
- specific tactical lessons, not vague motivation
- show mistakes and fixes
- use characters/visual bits to make the lesson entertaining
- combine tactical detail with a bigger philosophy

## What Not To Copy
- heavy AI film production unless Ryan actually wants that lane
- too much chaos if clarity suffers
- long videos without clear chapters

## New Content System Rule
When Ryan shares a workflow, include at least one mistake/fix detail.

Pattern:
`I used to do X. It seemed right. It created Y problem. Now I do Z because...`

This creates trust because it shows lived experience, not borrowed guru wisdom.
