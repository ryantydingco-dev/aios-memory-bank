# Ryan Higgsfield Prompt Kit — First Visual Bits

## How To Use
Use Banana Pro Director first for still image references/character sheets. Use Cinema Worldbuilder Pro second for motion scenes.

These are Ryan-adapted starter concepts, not final production prompts. Run them through the uploaded Joey skills before generating in Higgsfield.

---

## 1. Spreadsheet Goblin

### Lesson
A job title is not a lead. A buying signal is.

### Still Image Concept
A small slightly absurd but photoreal goblin sits in a dim cave made of glowing spreadsheets and monitor light. Around him are floating CSV rows, LinkedIn profile cards, and unqualified lead names. He is guarding one giant cursed spreadsheet labeled visually by context only, no readable text. Premium cinematic lighting, funny but polished, not horror.

### Motion Scene
The goblin proudly throws a giant pile of random lead cards toward an exhausted sales rep. The rep looks overwhelmed. A clean glowing signal map appears in the background as contrast. Camera slowly pushes in with subtle handheld operator movement. Mood is funny chaotic business metaphor.

## 2. Overconfident AI Intern

### Lesson
AI gets you to 70%; human context gets you to 90%.

### Still Image Concept
A very confident young office intern with a tiny homemade CEO badge stands in a modern office, proudly holding a stack of cold emails. Behind him, a monitor shows obviously broken placeholders like blurred bracket shapes, not readable text. The expression is proud but clueless. Photoreal, premium creator aesthetic, funny but not mean.

### Motion Scene
The intern slides a stack of papers across a desk with way too much confidence. Ryan/operator character glances at them, notices obvious mistakes, and calmly adds a red QA checklist. The intern deflates slightly but remains optimistic. Subtle comedic timing.

### CTA Asset
Free checklist: Human Approval Gate for AI Workflows.

## 3. CRM Gremlin

### Lesson
If it is not logged in HubSpot, it did not happen.

### Still Image Concept
A tiny gremlin hiding inside a CRM dashboard cave, stuffing warm reply envelopes under a messy pile of task cards. The room glows orange and blue from computer screens. Funny, cinematic, slightly chaotic, no readable brand logos.

### Motion Scene
The gremlin quietly drags a warm reply under a mountain of overdue tasks. A spotlight turns on. The gremlin freezes. A clean daily sales dashboard slides into view as the chaos gets organized.

## 4. Cursed Borrower Docs

### Lesson
Oloxa turns messy borrower documents into lender-ready packages.

### Still Image Concept
A commercial finance broker sits at a desk opening a glowing folder full of chaotic PDF-like papers. File names appear as visual nonsense shapes, not readable text. Papers float around the room like a cursed escape room. The broker looks tired but amused. Cinematic office lighting, realistic, funny, professional.

### Motion Scene
The broker opens the folder and papers burst out. The chaos freezes, then neatly sorts itself into organized lender-ready stacks. Camera moves from chaos to clean package. Keep it metaphorical, not magical-product-claim heavy.

Free checklist: Borrower Doc Intake Audit.

## 5. Tool Duct Tape Monster

### Lesson
Most businesses do not need more tools. They need one workflow mapped clearly.

### Still Image Concept
A friendly Frankenstein-like business monster made of laptops, app windows, sticky notes, calendar blocks, email bubbles, and duct tape. A tired operator tries to hold it together. The monster looks goofy, not scary. Premium cinematic workspace aesthetic.

### Motion Scene
The duct-tape tool monster wobbles forward holding too many apps. The operator stops, draws one clean workflow map on a glass board, and the monster calms down into an organized workstation.

## Production Notes
- Avoid readable third-party logos.
- Avoid creating deceptive real-person likenesses.
- Keep scenes metaphorical.
- Use humor to get attention, then deliver real value.
- Always pair visuals with proof/template/checklist.
