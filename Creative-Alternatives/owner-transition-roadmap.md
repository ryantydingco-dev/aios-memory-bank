# Owner Transition Roadmap — written 2026-08-06

## Why this document exists
CA is a succession play. Ryan and Maclaine take it over. That makes the objective
**a business that runs without Kenny**, not just a bigger commission number for Ryan.

**The insight that makes everything else obvious: Kenny is the bottleneck on revenue.**
Every hour Kenny is on the critical path is simultaneously (a) an hour he can't stop
working, (b) a deal moving slower than it should, and (c) knowledge that isn't
transferring to the next owners.

So "grow the business," "make Kenny work less," and "make it inheritable" are not three
projects competing for time. They are the same project.

**And it is the valuation argument.** Owner-dependence is the biggest discount applied to
any small business and the main reason handoffs fail. A shop that runs because one person
holds 27 years of pricing and customer history in his head is fragile, hard to value, and
hard to take over. Every process moved out of Kenny's head raises what Ryan and Maclaine
actually inherit.

## Sequence — by (revenue impact × ease)

### 1. RYAN LEARNS TO QUOTE ← the single highest-leverage move
This one thing removes Kenny from the critical path on **every deal**, lets Ryan close
without waiting on anyone, and is the core skill of actually owning a print shop.
It is also the skill Ryan most conspicuously lacks today.

**How:**
- Shadow Kenny on the next 10 quotes. Not "watch" — write each one yourself first, then
  have him correct it. The corrections are the entire lesson.
- Capture the logic as you go into `pillars/1-operations/pricing/`: blank costs by
  supplier, decoration cost by method (screen/embroidery/DTG) and quantity break,
  markup rules, when to discount, rush charges, freight.
- Goal: Ryan quotes anything under $5K without asking. Kenny reviews only the big ones.
- **Start with KMF**, which is sitting unquoted right now. Real deal, real lesson.

### 2. USE THE ART SYSTEM THAT ALREADY EXISTS
The `ca-production-art` skill produces the full artist deliverable — outlined vector PDF at
imprint size with PMS callouts, art-locked mockup, standard proof sheet. It was built to
replace the $75-100/job outside artist and it is not being used.

Every job run through it is cash saved, an art round-trip removed from Kenny's day, and a
faster proof to the customer. **Adopt it on the very next job that needs art.**

### 3. INSTALL THE SHEETS AUTOMATION THAT'S ALREADY WRITTEN
`pillars/1-operations/sheets-auto-close/auto_close.gs` is finished and uninstalled.
Roughly an hour of work: Viking first, then Diamond. Removes daily manual status chasing.

### 4. CAPTURE CUSTOMER HISTORY AS A BYPRODUCT OF THE CALLS
The call sheet's blank "what they bought" column is the whole knowledge-transfer problem in
miniature. Don't just fill it for these 20 — **fill it permanently.** Every reactivation
call, log what they bought, who the contact is, what went wrong, what they'll need next
season. In a year that file is the customer knowledge that currently exists only in
Kenny's memory, and it is exactly what makes the business inheritable.

### 5. AP AND VENDOR RELATIONSHIPS
~$44K past due to Sanmar is a real risk to terms and fulfillment. Visibility is already in
the daily brief. Ryan should understand the vendor relationships even if Kenny keeps
running them for now — supplier terms are a core asset of a shop like this.

## What this means for the near-term plan
Nothing in `full-time-plan-2026-08-06.md` gets cancelled. Reactivation calls and the live
deals are still the right first move — they generate cash AND they are how Ryan learns the
customers, the products, and the pricing. The revenue work IS the apprenticeship.

**What changes is the scoring.** Time spent on systems that reduce Kenny's hours is no
longer "overhead that pays no commission." It is the point. Two hours installing the sheets
script or building the pricing file counts as real progress even in a week with no orders.

**The severance clock is still real** (cliff ~2026-10-06). Long-term thinking does not pay
an October bill. Keep the fastest-cash lanes moving while the systems get built. That
tension is managed, not resolved.

---

## Scoreboard for this phase
Not just Ryan-originated revenue. Track both:
1. **Revenue originated** (the cash lane, still the survival metric)
2. **Kenny hours removed** — quotes Ryan wrote unaided, art jobs run in-house instead of
   sent out, manual sheet updates eliminated, customer records captured

A week with two orders and no systems built is not obviously better than a week with one
order and quoting learned. Score both.
