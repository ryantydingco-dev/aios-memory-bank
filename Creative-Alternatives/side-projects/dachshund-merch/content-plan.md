# Content engine

**Rule #1: entertainment first, product second.** The shirt is a prop in a dog video, never the subject. Accounts that sell in every video die in the algorithm.

## Two content engines, one account

1. **The dogs** — the show. Daily-life humor, dachshund-behavior truths, senior/puppy moments, dog-meets-world.
2. **The print shop** — the differentiator. Squeegee/heat-press ASMR, design reveals, "you comment it, we print it," behind-the-scenes of a 27-year-old family print shop making ridiculous dog shirts. No other dachshund brand can film this.

Format mix that's working in the niche right now (from R&D): daily routines, humor captions about dachshund stubbornness/burrowing/zoomies, senior-dog tenderness, owner-POV skits.

## Recurring formats (build these as series)

- **"We own a print shop and our dogs abuse this power"** — dog "demands" a shirt, shop prints it, dog wears it. Core franchise.
- **Comment-to-print** — pin a video asking for design ideas; print the top comment weekly and film the reveal. Engagement flywheel + free design R&D.
- **Print ASMR** — 15–30s satisfying press/peel shots with the design as the payoff.
- **Doxie truths** — text-on-screen behavior jokes over dog footage (cheapest to produce, post on low-energy days).
- **Matching-set reveals** — owner + dog in the same print. The shareable one.
- **Build-in-public** — "day 1 of launching a merch brand from our family print shop." Feeds Pillar 4 directly.

## Cadence

- **TikTok:** 4–5/week minimum, 60 days before judging anything. Volume beats polish.
- **Instagram:** repost Reels + Stories for community; IG is where the buyers age-skew slightly older (Bean Goods' audience lives here).
- **Batch filming:** one weekend session = 2 weeks of dog footage. Print-shop content films itself during real print runs.
