## Reverse-engineered models

### 1. Alex Hormozi / Acquisition.com

**Observed success:** Acquisition.com says Alex and Leila Hormozi's portfolio grew to over **$250M+ in annual revenue** in four years.

**Why it works:**
- Gives away deep business education for free.
- Uses clear frameworks and simple mental models.
- Every piece of content points back to a bigger business ecosystem.
- Strong lead magnets and workshops capture demand.

**What to steal:**
- Give away the exact workflow, prompt, checklist, and lesson.
- Make every video useful even if the viewer never buys.
- Use direct, high-clarity CTAs.

**Ryan implementation:**
- "Here is the exact AI workflow I used inside CA."
- "Comment `AIOS` and I will send the prompt/workflow."
- Build the Small Business AI Agent Playbook.
- Do not sell implementation yet; capture audience and trust first.

**CA implementation:**
- Turn buyer education into simple assets: "How branded stores work," "What to order for camp season," "How to avoid merch headaches."

Source: https://www.acquisition.com/

### 2. Codie Sanchez / Contrarian Thinking

**Observed success:** Contrarian Thinking positions Codie around owning and improving "boring" Main Street businesses. A 2025 Houston Chronicle profile reported about **90 employees, 11M followers**, and **14,129 program/event participants**.

**Why it works:**
- Owns a clear category: boring businesses as wealth vehicles.
- Makes unsexy businesses feel aspirational.
- Combines education, media, events, and paid programs.
- The content has a worldview, not just tactics.

**What to steal:**
- Make the unsexy business the star.
- Own a contrarian thesis.
- Build a movement around practical ownership, not internet hype.

**Ryan implementation:**
- Category: "AI for Main Street businesses."
- Thesis: the next big AI opportunity is not another software startup; it is applying AI to overlooked real businesses.
- Recurring line: "The best AI opportunity might be inside the business already sitting in front of you."

**CA implementation:**
- Make CA feel like a real Main Street success story, not just a supplier.
- Show how relationships and systems can coexist.

Sources:

- https://www.houstonchronicle.com/business/article/codie-sanchez-finance-influencer-21119012.php

### 3. Nate Herk / AI Automation

**Observed success:** Nate Herk's site claims **800K+ YouTube subscribers**, **400K+ community members**, **3,500+ paid members**, and a positioning around AI agents, automations, and income.

**Why it works:**

- Community monetization captures learners.
- Builds around what people are already searching for: AI agents, n8n, Claude Code, automation.

**What to steal:**

**Ryan implementation:**

## Patterns across winners

1. **They own a category.**
   - Hormozi: scaling offers/businesses.
   - Codie: boring businesses / Main Street ownership.
   - Nate: AI automation education.
   - 4imprint: direct promo-products convenience.
   - Ryan should own: AI transformation for real Main Street businesses.

2. **They make invisible work visible.**
   - UpFlip shows the business mechanics.
   - Nate shows the workflow.
   - CA content should show proofing, quoting, fulfillment, and AI assistance.

3. **They give away useful assets.**
   - Roadmaps, templates, newsletters, tutorials, case studies.
   - Ryan should give away workflows and prompts.
   - CA should give away mockups, buyer guides, and seasonal merch checklists.

4. **They use numbers as hooks.**
   - But numbers must be verified.
   - Use `[CONFIRM]` until Ryan/Kenny/Maclaine approve.

5. **They build a media-to-business loop.**
   - Content is not just awareness; it routes into lead magnets, workshops, calls, products, stores, or services.
   - Ryan's loop: video -> prompt/playbook -> email list -> optional monetization.
   - CA's loop: content -> mockup CTA -> branded-store conversation -> revenue.

## What not to copy

- Do not copy generic AI hype creators with no proof.
- Do not copy pure promo-product catalog content; CA cannot win by being a smaller 4imprint.
- Do not make Kenny/Maclaine perform for the camera.
- Do not show private operational data for a better hook.
- Do not build Ryan's channel around selling an AI agency right now. The stronger story is building one real business.

## Ryan's content formats to run

### Format 1 - The Build

**Template:** "I built [AI system] for my girlfriend's dad's $3M business."

Examples:
- "I built an AI daily brief for my girlfriend's dad's business."
- "I built an AI agent that finds dormant customers."
- "I built an AI proof assistant for a promo-products company."

### Format 2 - The Result

**Template:** "AI turned [manual pain] into [result]."

Examples:
- "8 hours in QuickBooks turned into X minutes." `[CONFIRM result]`
- "AI found $[REAL] in dormant customer opportunities." `[CONFIRM result]`
- "We sent 100 mockups and got X replies." `[CONFIRM result]`

### Format 3 - The Lesson

**Template:** "What I learned putting AI into a real business."

Examples:
- "The first thing you should automate is not what you think."
- "AI cannot replace relationship trust. It can protect it."
- "The difference between an AI chatbot and an AI employee."

### Format 4 - The Growth Experiment

**Template:** "I tested [growth play] for CA. Here is what happened."

Examples:
- "I tested AI store mockups as cold email openers."
- "I tested dormant customer reactivation."
- "I tested a branded-store offer for squash clubs."

### Format 5 - The Buyer Proof

**Template:** "Here is how CA makes [specific buyer outcome] easier."

Examples:
- "How a camp can launch a branded store without inventory."
- "What a squash club should sell before tournament season."
- "How to pick branded merch people actually use."
