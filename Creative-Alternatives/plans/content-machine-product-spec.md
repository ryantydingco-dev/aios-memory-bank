# Product Spec — "The Local Legend Content Engine"

> Ryan's sellable, productized version of the CA brand content machine
> (`pillars/3-online-presence/ca-brand-content-machine.md`). Build rule in force: every asset
> created for CA is saved twice — CA version + {{brand}} template — so the product assembles
> itself as the CA build ships. CA is the living case study; destiNY is proof #1 (product
> content), CA becomes proof #2 (a real B2B brand from zero).

## The one-liner
**"Your business is great and invisible. We build you a daily content machine — real faces,
real work, AI-powered production — so you look as good online as you are in person. You film
~90 minutes a week; we do everything else."**

## Who buys it
Family-run / owner-operated B2B businesses doing $1M-$20M that win on relationships and have
zero or dead social presence: print shops, contractors, manufacturers, med/dental practices,
law firms, insurance agencies, auto shops. (Deliberately the same verticals as CA's swag
campaigns — one research motion, two offers.) The buyer's felt pain: "our competitors look
huge online and we look like we don't exist."

## Why it wins (the wedge)
Same anti-big-machine insight as CA's own positioning: these businesses' unfair advantage is
that they're REAL — faces, hands, shop floors, a founder with stories. Big competitors can't
copy that. The product packages authenticity with AI-scale production: the humans stay real
on camera, the AI does the mockups/editing/assembly/scheduling invisibly.

## What's in the box (the modules — each maps to a CA-built asset)
1. **Brand voice + positioning workshop** → the anti-big-machine voice doc ({{brand}} version)
2. **Greenfield launch kit** → handle claiming, bios, profile assets, pinned intro, GBP claim
3. **The 7-format content library** → adapted to their craft (their "Mockup Magic" equivalent =
   whatever their visual money-shot is: the weld, the smile reveal, the before/after)
4. **The production engine** → product/mockup generation line + assemble pipeline + caption/hook
   templates + the weekly 3-hour filming system (shot lists so the owner films fast)
5. **2-week buffer + daily posting cadence** across TikTok/IG/Shorts + native LinkedIn
6. **Lead capture wiring** → comment-CTA mechanics, DM-to-quote flow, attribution (how-did-you-
   hear + UTMs), monthly value-metrics report (DMs/quotes/site clicks — never vanity)

## Offer structure & pricing (AAA pricing frameworks; market-typical ranges — validate on first sales)
- **Setup/launch (one-time):** $2,500-$5,000 — brand voice, launch kit, format library, engine
  setup, first 2-week buffer built.
- **Monthly engine (retainer):** $1,500-$3,500/mo — 20-30 posts/mo produced + queued from the
  client's weekly ~90-min footage drop, monthly metrics report, quarterly format refresh.
  (Cost-plus check: heavy AI leverage keeps delivery hours low → healthy 3-5x margin per AAA's
  rule. Value check: one reactivated customer or new account typically covers a month.)
- **Upsells that already exist in Ryan's stack:** the 20-ads sprint (paid creative), the AI
  assessment → AIOS build (consulting), outbound engine setup. Content retainer is the
  low-friction entry that surfaces the bigger builds — the same audit→retainer flywheel.

## Sales motion (when the time comes — NOT yet)
Gate: **don't sell hard until CA shows 60-90 days of real results** (followers are vanity;
use real numbers: DMs, quote requests, a closed order sourced from social). Then:
1. The case study IS the pitch: "we took a 27-year print shop from zero to a daily brand —
   here's the before/after and what it produced."
2. Wedge: a free "content teardown + 3 sample posts for YOUR business" (the mockup-wedge
   pattern applied to this offer — show, don't pitch).
3. Channels: Ryan's Transformation Diary audience (inbound), the existing swag-campaign
   verticals (they already know CA), local business networks.

## Build discipline (so the product assembles itself)
- Template-twin rule on every asset (CA + {{brand}}).
- Keep a running CHANGELOG of what works/flops on CA's channels — the playbook's "what we
  learned" section is half the product's value.
- Document time-per-week honestly — "90 minutes of your time" must be TRUE by the time it's sold.
