## Comedic positioning

**"The family shop that's seen every swag crime of the last 27 years — and finally started filming."**

Rules of funny for a business page:
1. **A face carries the funny.** Faceless brand humor dies. Kenny (deadpan veteran) is the franchise; Ryan (the AI guy) is the straight man; Maclaine is the voice of reason. Cast them.
2. Punch at: broken pens, tote-bag avalanches, ghosting vendors, 144-minimums, "processing" status pages. Never at: customers, recipients, employees, specific competitors by name (roast "your vendor," not "4imprint" — the audience fills the name in themselves).
3. Side with the RECIPIENT (the open angle nobody owns): the person drowning in lanyards is the hero of our content.
4. Self-deprecating is allowed and priceless ("we printed some of these crimes ourselves in 2003").
5. Law/financial buyers are watching: keep it clean, keep it kind, no politics, nothing a compliance officer couldn't share.
6. Every third-ish post pays it off with proof (mockup speed, proof page, store) — funny earns attention, proof converts it.

## Recurring series (each = infinite episodes)

1. **🚔 SWAG CRIMES** — crime-scene treatment of terrible promo. Caution tape, evidence bags, case numbers. "Case #047: the lightning-bolt pen that didn't work at TWO trade shows." Source material: the corpus + viewers submitting their worst swag (UGC engine: "report a swag crime").
2. **👴 KENNY RATES** — Kenny deadpan-rates swag 1-10. Drawstring bag: "2. It's a parachute for disappointment." Stress ball: "1. The stress comes back." Well-done Yeti: "9. This one survives the drawer." Also: Kenny Rates trends, Kenny reacts to AI.
3. **🎬 POV: YOUR VENDOR** — dramatized pain corpus. Ghosting, "processing since June," the $75-per-revision loop, "we regret to inform you your order was never… made." Each ends with the quiet flex (proof page tap, 47-minute mockup).
4. **⏱ MOCKUP SPEEDRUNS** — gamer framing: "Law firm replied 9:14 AM" → timer overlay → 10:01, three mockups. "New PB." Infinite series, one per vertical; doubles as the literal offer demo.
5. **📦 27 YEARS OF PRINTING** — weird true archive stories. "Things we have actually put logos on." (Mine Kenny + the QuickBooks/paper archive — Engine-1 synergy.)
6. **🧵 THE SWAG CLOSET FILES** — spreadsheet chaos, size avalanches, "73 unsold shirts in a closet" reenactments → "we run the store, you cash the check."

## How it feeds the machine

- Friday block: episode first (record kit), then 20 minutes of skits (2-3 takes each, phone, no polish — polish kills the charm).
- The corpus is the writers' room: every strong quote = a skit premise or one-liner. It refreshes as we re-mine quarterly.
- Success metrics (brand page): profile visits from cold-email prospects (ask "where'd you find us" on calls), mockup-CTA clicks, saves/shares. Follower count is vanity; the page's job is to make CA feel alive, human, and safe to buy from.
