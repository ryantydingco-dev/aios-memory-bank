# Content Growth Game Plan - Creative Alternatives + Ryan

> Created 2026-06-29. Purpose: turn the real AI transformation work at Creative Alternatives into revenue, trust, and Ryan's personal brand without distracting from the business.

## Core thesis

Ryan is not becoming a generic "AI tips" creator. The strongest story is:

> I am modernizing my girlfriend's dad's 27-year promotional-products business with AI, one real system at a time, and showing business owners exactly what works.

Creative Alternatives is the proof vehicle. Ryan is the operator. Kenny and Maclaine are the human stakes. The audience follows because the work is real: real workflows, real customers, real money, real mistakes.

## Two engines, one flywheel

### Engine 1 - Ryan's personal brand

**Audience:** small-business owners, operators, AI-curious founders, automation builders.

**Goal:** build authority, trust, optionality, and an audience Ryan can monetize later through products, sponsorships, consulting/selective implementation, or software.

**Primary channels:**
- YouTube long-form
- YouTube Shorts, TikTok, Instagram Reels
- LinkedIn

**Content promise:** "Watch me use AI to transform a real $3M family business. Steal the workflows."

### Engine 2 - Creative Alternatives marketing

**Audience:** camps, clubs, schools, teams, squash/racquet clubs, member-based organizations, and past customers.

**Goal:** generate revenue for CA through reactivation, referrals, branded-store leads, and credibility.

**Primary channels:**
- CA LinkedIn
- CA Instagram/Facebook
- Website/Google Business Profile proof assets
- Sales assets reused inside SmartLead and follow-up sequences

**Content promise:** "We make branded gear and stores easy. You get the product, store, and revenue without the headaches."

### Flywheel

1. Claude runs GTM: lead pull, enrichment, copy, SmartLead, dialer, outreach.
2. Codex turns the work into content: hooks, scripts, shot lists, posts, shorts, case studies, sales assets.
3. Ryan records the work using Oakley Meta glasses, Sony ZV-E10, MacBook screen capture, and Rode audio.
4. Published content creates trust, proof, and attention.
5. Proof goes back into outbound: better reply angles, follow-up assets, case studies, founder credibility.
6. GTM replies, wins, and failures become the next videos.

## Content pillars

### 1. AI Transformation Diary

The flagship build-in-public series.

Examples:
- "I built an AI employee for my girlfriend's dad's business."
- "I automated the 8-hour QuickBooks task."
- "I gave a 27-year business an AI daily brief."
- "What AI could and could not automate this week."

Use for: Ryan YouTube, Shorts, LinkedIn.

### 2. Revenue Experiments

The growth story. This is where Claude's GTM work becomes content.

Examples:

- "We sent AI-generated store mockups to 100 camps. Here is what happened."
- "I found dormant customers in QuickBooks and wrote the win-back campaign."
- "The cold email that got replies for a promo-products business."

Use for: Ryan YouTube, Shorts, LinkedIn, sales enablement.

### 3. Old-School Business, New-School Systems

The emotional hook: Kenny's experience plus Ryan's AI systems.

Examples:
- "He built this business on relationships. I am trying not to break that."
- "What 27 years of customer trust teaches you about automation."
- "The rule: no AI touches customers, vendors, or money without approval."
- "Why I am building inside a family business instead of starting another agency."

Use for: Ryan personal brand, founder story, trust building.

### 4. CA Product Proof

Buyer-facing content for Creative Alternatives.

Examples:
- Product spotlights: apparel, bags, awards, lanyards, towels, trophies, locker plates.
- "What we would build for a summer camp merch store."
- "3 branded products every squash club should have this season."
- Behind the scenes: proof, production, packing, delivery, finished orders.
- Customer/store walkthroughs when approved.

Use for: CA social, website proof, outbound assets.

### 5. Operator Lessons

The reusable frameworks Ryan learns while building.

Examples:
- "AI agents are not chatbots. They are workflows with judgment."
- "The 5 AI tools a small business actually needs."
- "How I decide what to automate first."
- "What I would never automate in a relationship-driven business."

Use for: Ryan YouTube, LinkedIn, newsletter/lead magnet later.

## Weekly cadence

### Minimum viable cadence

- 1 YouTube long-form per week.
- 5 Shorts per week, cut from real work.
- 2 Ryan LinkedIn posts per week.
- 3 CA social posts per week.
- 1 sales asset per week created from the content, such as a case study, proof screenshot, Loom, or one-page offer asset.

### Stretch cadence

- 2 YouTube long-form per week.
- Daily Shorts.
- 3 to 5 Ryan LinkedIn posts per week.
- 4 to 5 CA posts per week.
- 1 weekly email/newsletter once the list exists.

## Capture system

### Oakley Meta glasses

Use for natural POV footage:
- Walking into the office.
- Looking over order boards, product samples, packaging, equipment, notes.
- "I am about to ask Maclaine how this actually works."
- Behind-the-scenes b-roll of the messy, real business.

Rules:
- Get permission before recording Kenny, Maclaine, customers, vendors, or sensitive work.
- Do not capture private customer data, invoices, bank details, or supplier pricing.
- Treat Oakley footage as texture and story, not the main teaching asset.

### Sony ZV-E10

Use for polished footage:
- Talking-head intros and conclusions.
- Kenny/Maclaine interviews.
- Weekly recap videos.
- Sales/brand assets that need to look credible.

### MacBook Pro

Use for proof:
- Screen-record the AI workflow.
- Show anonymized Claude/Codex prompts.
- Show dashboards, scripts, SmartLead setup, QuickBooks analysis, and before/after outputs.

### Rode microphones

Use for every intentional recording:
- Talking head.
- Interview.
- Screen demo voiceover.
- Two-person audit or recap.

## Weekly production rhythm

### Monday - Pick and package

- Choose the week's business work worth filming.
- Define the content angle before doing the work.
- Output: title options, thumbnail concept, hook, shot list, CTA, and publish date.

### Tuesday/Wednesday - Build and capture

- Do the actual CA work.
- Record screen, Oakley POV, and short reflections before/after.
- Save raw notes and results in the workspace.

### Thursday - Script and edit map

- Turn the footage into:
  - 1 long-form outline/script
  - 5 Shorts
  - 2 LinkedIn posts
  - 3 CA buyer-facing posts if relevant
  - 1 sales asset for outbound/follow-up

### Friday - Publish and distribute

- Publish the flagship video or post.
- Schedule Shorts.
- Add the best proof asset to SmartLead follow-ups or the CA site/social queue.
- Log metrics after 24 hours and 7 days.
