# The CA Show — World Bible (v1)

> The creative universe for Creative Alternatives' channels. Built from GenHQ craft (story
> spine, consistent characters, locked-frame compositing) + the anti-slop doctrine in
> `../ca-brand-content-machine.md`. This file is the source of truth for tone, cast, and the
> rules of the world — every episode packet references it.

## The premise (the whole show in two sentences)
A 27-year-old family print shop finally joins the internet — dragged there by the new guy and
his AI. The AI can preview anything instantly; only the humans can actually make it.

That tension IS the brand thesis: **AI previews. Real people deliver.** Every episode proves
it again in a new way.

## The two channels (LOCKED 2026-07-02 — Dealthread scrapped, all-in on CA)
1. **Ryan's channel (PRIMARY — YouTube long-form, weekly).** The build diary: "I'm rebuilding
   my girlfriend's family's 27-year-old print shop with AI." Documentary, not tutorial — real
   stakes, real numbers, real family. The Intern appears as Ryan's ACTUAL AI assistant: it
   presents the builds (mockups, campaigns, lookbooks) that Ryan's real AI stack genuinely
   produced. Credibility engine for everything CA sells. Format spec:
   `longform-build-diary.md` (this folder).
2. **CA's channels (short-form daily).** The 15-30s silent-comedy episodes + the 7 formats in
   `../ca-brand-content-machine.md`, aimed at shop customers. Long-form episodes get clipped
   into these — one filming effort feeds both.

**The honesty covenant (what makes this uncopyable):** everything The Intern "does" on the
show was actually done by Ryan's real AI systems. Show results, show the reply screenshots,
show the revenue. Never a fabricated demo. The show is basically true — that's the moat.

## The cast

### Real Ryan (the lead, Phase 1)
The new guy with the robots. Confident about the tech, slightly outnumbered by reality.
Films on ZV-E10 / iPhone / Meta glasses. Speaks to camera like he's letting you in on
something. Never explains the joke.

### "The Intern" — AI-Ryan (the co-star, openly AI)
- **What it is:** an AI clone Ryan "trained on himself" to help run the shop. This is show
  LORE — it explains why the avatar looks like Ryan and why it exists at all.
- **Personality:** instant, eager, hyper-literal, zero understanding of the physical world.
  It can render 500 hoodies in a second; it cannot lift a single box. It is never mean and
  never wrong about digital things — it's wrong about REALITY, which is the joke.
- **The permanent outfit (consistency anchor + brand):** navy CA quarter-zip, every episode,
  forever. (Same trick that kept the destiNY print consistent — a fixed reference outfit is
  what makes a character re-generatable, per GenHQ's consistent-character method. And the
  avatar literally wears the product.)
- **The tell (anti-slop rule):** The Intern is ALWAYS visibly AI — it appears with a soft
  glitch/materialize effect, and it never exists in a shot that pretends to be real. The
  audience is always in on it. We never pass AI off as human. That's the line between "fun
  AI character" and slop.
- **Voice (REVISED 2026-07-02, split by channel):**
  - **CA short-form:** NO VOICE — gestures, renders, text bubbles. Silent-comedy energy;
    dodges AI-voice slop and is funnier at 20 seconds.
  - **Ryan's long-form diary:** SPEAKS, with Ryan's own cloned voice (ElevenLabs). The lore
    justifies it — it's trained on Ryan, so it sounds like Ryan. Needed because the Intern
    co-hosts technical segments (the Joey/Mira handoff pattern). Slightly too-perfect
    delivery = part of the tell, alongside the glitch.

### Kenny (Phase 2 — seeded before he appears)
The legend. Gets referenced BEFORE he's ever on camera ("...that sounds like a Kenny
problem") so his first appearance is a payoff, not an introduction. His skepticism is the bit.

### Maclaine (Phase 2)
The operator. The one who actually runs everything and knows where everything is. Straight
woman to both Ryan's tech optimism and Kenny's analog stubbornness.

## Running gags (recurring = compounding)
1. **"That sounds like a Kenny problem."** — The Intern's answer to anything physical.
2. **The handoff** — objects passed between the real world and the AI frame (the signature
   visual trick; something goes in blank, comes back printed, or vice versa).
3. **The Intern's instant mockups** — any org mentioned = kit rendered before the sentence ends.
4. **Kenny's AOL email / the fax machine** — validate the real details with Kenny first
   (Friday), then never let it go.
5. **"We're real people"** said while standing next to an obvious AI clone. Self-aware, once
   per every few episodes, never more.

## Tone rules (print and tape to the wall)
- Deadpan > hype. Nobody in this universe is excited; things are simply true.
- 15-30 seconds. One joke per video. Land it and get out.
- Hook inside 1-3 seconds (motion or claim), payoff in the last 3.
- Never explain the technology. Never say "we used AI to..." in-video — the world just works
  this way. (The HOW content is Ryan's personal channel — different audience, per the
  two-engine plan.)
- No trends unless they'd be funny to a 55-year-old shop owner too.
- Real products, real prints, real shop. The only fictional thing in the universe is the Intern.

## Episode structure (the story spine, GenHQ Short Film path)
Every episode = 3 beats: **Setup (real) → Escalation (AI does something instant/impossible)
→ Grounding (reality wins, warmly).** The brand always lands on the human side. The Intern
is amazing; the family is irreplaceable. That order is the positioning.
