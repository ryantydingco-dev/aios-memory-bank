# LinkedIn Content Engine — Ryan, Kenny, and Maclaine

> **Governing LinkedIn operating document.** The goal is a durable weekly habit tied to real business events and sales proof, not a daily content quota. Nothing here authorizes publishing.

## Purpose

Creative Alternatives has three credible perspectives that should not sound interchangeable:

- Ryan explains the transformation and the operating decisions.
- Kenny shares the judgment earned over 27 years.
- Maclaine makes the buyer and operator experience concrete.

The company page can amplify proof, but the people carry trust. Each post must begin with a real event, artifact, decision, or lesson from the business.

## Voice cards

### Ryan — the operator building in public

**Purpose:** attract operators and AI-curious business owners, document decisions, and make the modernization credible.

**Voice:** specific, analytical, candid, energetic without pretending to be an “AI guru.”

**Best source events:**

- A workflow mapped or simplified.
- A metric changed or contradicted a plan.
- An automation failed, was stopped, or saved time.
- A hard tradeoff between speed, trust, and scale.
- A customer-safe artifact that shows the work.

**Structure:** artifact or number → what it revealed → decision → principle.

**Avoid:** generic AI tips, invented certainty, customer acquisition claims without attribution, making Kenny look obsolete.

**Sustainable cadence:** two posts per week. One may be a short build note. A third post is allowed only when the week produces a genuinely strong event.

### Kenny — founder judgment

**Purpose:** transfer trust and operating judgment, humanize CA, and capture knowledge the systems need.

**Voice:** plainspoken, experienced, relationship-first, dryly funny when natural. Ryan may draft, but it must sound like something Kenny would actually say.

**Best source events:**

- Why he chose a product, vendor, margin, or timeline.
- A customer promise he protects.
- A strange order or near-miss and the lesson.
- What 27 years taught him that a dashboard misses.
- A reaction to a proposed process change.

**Structure:** “Here is what happened” → “here is what experience noticed” → “here is the rule.”

**Avoid:** technical AI language, forced creator voice, customer names without approval, turning him into a mascot.

**Sustainable cadence:** one captured story or quote per week; publish no more than one post per week. If Kenny does not want to operate a personal LinkedIn profile, publish an approved founder-note format through the CA page or Ryan/Maclaine with clear attribution.

### Maclaine — the buyer and operator translator

**Purpose:** help buyers understand how a good merchandise partner removes work, while showing the practical reality of running the business.

**Voice:** warm, observant, organized, customer-first, lightly personal. She knows the books and the follow-up burden; the post should feel helpful, not “sales content.”

**Best source events:**

- A request that arrived incomplete and how better intake helps.
- A deadline saved by proactive communication.
- A proof, reorder, invoice, or vendor handoff lesson.
- What a buyer actually needs to provide for a fast quote.
- A customer-safe before/after in coordination or response time.

**Structure:** buyer situation → friction → what good handling looks like → practical takeaway.

**Avoid:** account-specific collections detail, passive-aggressive vendor/customer stories, copying Ryan's systems voice.

**Sustainable cadence:** one post per week, plus meaningful comments from her real perspective when she wants to participate.

## Content-to-sales boundaries

Ryan's audience and CA's buyers overlap only partly. Label every idea:

- `operator-learning` — primarily Ryan's audience.
- `founder-judgment` — trust and story.
- `buyer-education` — helps a merch buyer make a good decision.
- `proof` — real CA artifact or outcome.

A post may warm an account, but it does not count as a sales touch unless a person directly contacts or messages the prospect. Do not manufacture engagement or use a personal post as cover for an unrecorded cold DM.

## The event-driven workflow

### Step 1: capture during real work, under five minutes

Add a row to `operating-system/trackers/content/event-capture.csv` when one of these occurs:

- A quote, order, proof, vendor, invoice, or campaign reveals a lesson.
- Kenny makes an override and explains why.
- A metric changes a decision.
- A customer-safe artifact is produced.
- Something breaks and the fix is instructive.

Record the fact, evidence location, suggested voice, permission state, and why a buyer/operator should care.

### Step 2: weekly editorial review, 30 minutes

Ryan reviews captured events with these rules:

1. Facts and artifacts first.
2. Match the event to the person with the most authentic right to tell it.
3. Skip a voice if there is no honest event.
4. Choose one primary audience and purpose.
5. Mark every permission needed.

Add approved ideas to `operating-system/trackers/content/editorial-board.csv`.

### Step 3: draft in voice, 45–60 minutes total

Draft the minimum viable weekly set:

- Ryan: two posts.
- Kenny: one founder note or approved quote.
- Maclaine: one operator/buyer post.

One event may produce more than one post only when the perspectives are materially different. Do not rewrite the same story three times.

### Step 4: proof and privacy review, 15 minutes

Before approval:

- Verify every number and date.
- Confirm whether a logo, customer, screenshot, quote, or employee can be shown.
- Remove invoice numbers, contact details, supplier pricing, private margins, and bank/payment data.
- Mark generated mockups as mockups/previews.
- Confirm the named person approves their own words.

### Step 5: publish separately

Publishing is an external action and remains manual/approved. This workspace stores the queue and learnings; it does not publish.

### Step 6: learn, 10 minutes Friday

Record:

- Inbound DMs or quote requests.
- Prospects who mention a post.
- Useful comments from real buyers/operators.
- Which hooks produced qualified conversation.
- Human time spent.

Views and follower counts are diagnostic, not the business outcome.

## Weekly shape

| Day | Human work | Output |
|---|---|---|
| During work | Capture notable event, 1–3 minutes | Event row + proof location |
| Monday | 30-minute editorial review | Four or fewer approved ideas |
| Tuesday | Draft Ryan post 1 and Maclaine post | Drafts only |
| Thursday | Draft Ryan post 2 and Kenny founder note | Drafts only |
| Friday | Approval and learning review | Ready queue + scorecard |

Target human time: **90–120 minutes per week**, excluding optional filming. If the workflow exceeds two hours for two consecutive weeks, reduce output before adding automation.

## Proof hierarchy

Use the strongest safe proof available:

1. Verified customer/order outcome with explicit permission.
2. Redacted real artifact.
3. Verified aggregate business metric approved for public use.
4. Internal process before/after with measured time or exception count.
5. Kenny/Maclaine judgment tied to a real event.
6. Clearly labeled sample or mockup.

Do not use a sample as a case study or imply a draft automation is live.

## Reusable post frames

### Ryan

```text
[Artifact/number that changed the plan]

We expected [old assumption].
The real business showed [observed fact].

So we changed [one decision].

The lesson: [operator principle].
```

### Kenny

```text
A customer once asked us to [real situation].

The obvious answer was [option].
I chose [decision] because [relationship/product judgment].

After 27 years, my rule is: [plainspoken rule].
```

### Maclaine

```text
The fastest quote usually starts before we price anything.

When a request includes [three useful inputs], we can [buyer benefit].
When it doesn't, the delay is usually [honest friction].

If you're ordering branded merchandise, send [practical checklist].
```

## Measurement

Track weekly in `operating-system/trackers/content/weekly-scorecard.csv`.

Primary:

- Content-assisted replies, inbound DMs, quote requests, and qualified conversations.
- Proof assets cleared for future sales use.
- Human minutes per approved post.

Secondary:

- Useful comments and profile views from target roles.
- Saves/shares when the post is educational.
- Ryan audience growth, kept separate from CA buyer outcomes.

## First 30 days

- Week 1: confirm profile/account reality and public boundaries; capture one event per person.
- Week 2: draft one post per person plus one second Ryan post; no publication implied.
- Week 3: build one approved week of drafts and measure the human time.
- Week 4: keep only the formats that produced qualified conversation or felt sustainable.

The execution tasks are in `plans/first-30-days-unified-operating-plan.md`.
