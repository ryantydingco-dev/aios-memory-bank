## Hero

# Build the smallest company store that solves a real problem.

A company store can support employee apparel, onboarding, recognition, events, clients, or team ordering. It can also become another platform to manage. Use this guide to define the job and operating model before reviewing products or software.

## Name the primary job

Choose one first:

- Employee self-purchase
- Company-funded employee gear
- Onboarding
- Recognition or milestones
- Event and conference ordering
- Client or partner gifts
- Department or location reorders
- Field-team or staff apparel

If several jobs matter, rank them. They may need separate permissions, budgets, products, or delivery paths.

## Define the audience

- Employees
- Managers or department buyers
- New hires
- Clients and partners
- Event attendees
- Field teams
- Franchisees, locations, or approved groups

Confirm who may access the program and who pays.

## Store-fit questions

### Demand

- How many potential buyers or recipients exist?
- Is demand optional, required, or company-funded?
- Is ordering continuous or tied to windows?
- Which products repeat?

### Assortment

- What belongs in the evergreen core?
- Which products are seasonal or event-specific?
- Who approves brand, price, and product changes?
- How many choices can the audience usefully navigate?

### Fulfillment

- One office, multiple locations, venue, or individual addresses?
- Is inventory held, produced by window, or handled another way?
- Who manages packing, inserts, and special labels?
- Who handles undeliverable shipments?

### Support

- Who answers buyer questions?
- Who handles damage, returns, or size issues?
- Who owns product substitutions?
- What response expectations are practical?

### Data and access

- What employee or recipient data is required?
- Who provides and may access it?
- Are integrations actually required?
- What security or procurement review applies?

## Capability fit

### A managed service may fit when

- The company wants direct human help
- Product and artwork judgment matter
- The program is right-sized and understandable
- Bulk, gifting, events, and store needs overlap
- A focused assortment is more useful than a global catalog

### An enterprise platform may fit when

- Global warehouses or currencies are required
- Complex procurement or integrations are mandatory
- Many permission levels and budgets are needed
- Deep software administration is expected
- Service-level and security requirements exceed the approved CA model

CA should not imply enterprise capabilities it does not support.

## Pilot before expansion

A useful pilot can begin with one audience, one moment, a focused assortment, defined ordering period, and named owners.

Measure:

- Qualified usage or demand
- Product and size issues
- Support questions
- Delivery exceptions
- Reorders
- Internal workload
- Revenue or cost only when the accounting model is clear
