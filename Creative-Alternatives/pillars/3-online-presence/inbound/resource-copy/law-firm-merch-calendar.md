## Hero

# Map the firm's merchandise before every request becomes urgent.

Law-firm merchandise usually arrives through separate recruiting, marketing, events, office, client, and employee requests. A shared calendar turns those requests into one reviewable program without forcing every moment into one product assortment.

## Build the annual moment map

List dates and owners for:

- Summer associate recruiting and welcome
- Law-school and lateral recruiting events
- Attorney and staff onboarding
- Firm retreats and practice meetings
- Conferences, sponsorships, and client events
- Client and referral-source gifting
- Employee recognition and milestones
- Office openings or relocations
- Community, pro bono, and charitable events
- Holiday and year-end programs

## Assign an audience to each moment

- Candidates
- Summer associates
- New attorneys and staff
- Clients and referral sources
- Partners and internal teams
- Conference attendees
- Speakers, sponsors, or hosts
- Community participants

Audience clarity matters more than adding the firm logo to a generic list.

## Quarterly planning rhythm

### Quarter 1 review

- Confirm recruiting and summer-program dates
- Review onboarding cadence
- Identify spring conferences and sponsorships
- Assign retreat and client-event owners
- Refresh brand and approval requirements

### Quarter 2 review

- Lock summer associate and retreat needs
- Review event delivery locations
- Capture feedback on welcome merchandise
- Identify fall conference and recognition needs

### Quarter 3 review

- Plan year-end client and employee programs
- Confirm recipient data and shipping model
- Review office and practice-group requests
- Identify products requiring more approval time

### Quarter 4 review

- Complete approved year-end programs
- Record delivery and recipient issues
- Review unused inventory and repeat demand
- Draft next year's moment map while feedback is current

The quarter labels are a review rhythm, not fixed production deadlines.

## Brief for every request

Capture:

- Business moment
- Audience and recipient count
- Event or in-hand date
- Product purpose
- Budget guidance
- Brand standards and logo files
- Required approvers
- One location, multiple offices, venue, or individual shipping
- Packaging, note, or insert requirements
- Recipient-data owner

## Brand and approval control

- Use current approved marks
- Identify practice, event, sponsor, or client logos
- Confirm color and placement rules
- Name the final approver
- Set the date when changes stop
- Avoid production based on an email attachment that has not been authorized

## Gifting questions

- Is the gift for a relationship, milestone, event, or broad program?
- Should branding be visible, subtle, or limited to packaging?
- Are food, alcohol, wellness, or preference constraints involved?
- Does the firm hold recipient addresses?
- Who handles undeliverable packages and support?
- Are price, tax, policy, or ethics reviews required internally?

CA does not provide legal or ethics advice; the firm owns its policy review.
