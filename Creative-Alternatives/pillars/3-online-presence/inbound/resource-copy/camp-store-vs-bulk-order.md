## Hero

# Camp store or bulk order? Start with who is ordering.

The right answer may be bulk, store, or both. The decision depends on audience, demand certainty, distribution, timing, economics, and who will own questions after launch.

## The short version

### Choose bulk when

- The camp knows the quantity and size mix
- The products are required for staff or campers
- One coordinated delivery matters
- The camp controls distribution
- The date and final count are known

### Consider a store when

- Parents, alumni, or another approved audience should order directly
- Demand and sizes are uncertain
- A defined ordering window fits the calendar
- The expected volume supports the production model
- Store ownership and customer support are clear

### Use both when

Staff and operational items are predictable, while optional family or alumni merchandise works better through direct ordering.

## Decision table

| Question | Bulk order leans better | Store leans better |
|---|---|---|
| Who orders? | Camp buyer | Approved individuals |
| Quantity certainty | Known | Demand-driven |
| Distribution | Camp distributes | Supported direct shipping or pickup |
| Product need | Required or coordinated | Optional purchase |
| Timing | One production/delivery plan | Defined order window or approved program |
| Questions | Camp manages internally | Store support model is defined |
| Assortment | Tighter operational set | Focused buyer-facing collection |
| Reorders | Camp initiates | Program rules determine availability |

## Questions for a bulk order

- Is the quantity final or estimated?
- Who provides sizes?
- Are all products delivered together?
- How should staff, sessions, cabins, or events be separated?
- Who receives and counts cartons?
- What happens when a size runs short?
- Which products should be easy to reorder?

## Questions for a managed store

- Who may access the store?
- Is ordering always open or time-limited?
- Which products belong in the first collection?
- How are payment, production, shipping, pickup, and support handled?
- Who promotes the store?
- What expected demand supports the program?
- How are returns, damaged items, and buyer questions handled?
- Are revenue or fundraising terms involved? `[CONFIRM before public claim]`

## Common mistakes

### Too many products at launch

A large catalog creates choices, not necessarily demand. Start with a coherent core tied to staff, campers, parents, or alumni.

### Optional products inside a required bulk order

The camp can end up holding sizes or items that people did not choose.

### Required staff gear left to individual ordering

Coordinated arrival, sizing, and compliance may be harder when every person orders independently.

### Store terms decided after promotion

Products, ordering window, production, shipping, support, pricing, and ownership should be confirmed before anyone is invited to buy.

### No restock rule

Define how a fast-moving size or essential item will be reviewed. Availability may change between production runs.

## A practical hybrid model

One possible structure:

1. Bulk staff apparel and required camper items
2. Parent/alumni store with a focused optional assortment
3. Separate special-event or reunion order
4. Human review for urgent in-season restocks
5. Post-season demand review before repeating the collection

This is an example, not a universal operating promise. The approved model depends on CA's confirmed store and fulfillment terms.

## Before choosing

Bring these inputs:

- Audience by ordering path
- Opening and in-hand dates
- Estimated quantity or buyer count
- Current products and last season's demand
- Logo and artwork status
- Distribution and shipping requirements
- Internal owner
- Budget or price guidance
