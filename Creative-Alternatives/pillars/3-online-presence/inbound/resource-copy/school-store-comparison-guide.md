## Hero

# Human-managed or self-serve? Choose the school-store workload first.

Both models can work. The useful question is not which platform has the longest feature list; it is which responsibilities the school wants to own and which need an experienced merchandise partner.

## Two broad models

### Self-serve store

The school or group chooses a platform, builds the assortment, manages brand decisions, promotes the store, and works within the platform's product, production, and support rules.

### Human-managed program

A merchandise partner helps narrow products, review artwork, plan the ordering model, surface production constraints, and manage the approved program.

Specific responsibilities vary. Confirm them before launch.

## Comparison table

| Decision | Self-serve leans stronger when | Human-managed leans stronger when |
|---|---|---|
| Product choice | The team knows what to list | The team wants curated guidance |
| Artwork | Files and placement are simple | Marks, approvals, or decoration need review |
| Workload | An internal owner can manage setup | The group wants a person coordinating details |
| Assortment | Standard platform products are enough | Staff, teams, events, or unusual items matter |
| Production | Platform rules fit the need | Timing and methods need human judgment |
| Support | Platform support is acceptable | The school wants a direct relationship |
| Bulk needs | Separate vendor/process is fine | Bulk and store should work together |

## Questions for any provider

- Is setup priced separately?
- Is the store always open or time-limited?
- How are products produced?
- Does the school hold inventory?
- Who handles payment?
- Who handles customer questions?
- How do shipping and pickup work?
- How are returns, damage, or incorrect items handled?
- Who approves logos and artwork?
- What happens when a product becomes unavailable?
- Are fundraising or revenue terms involved? `[CONFIRM before promotion]`
- Can staff, teams, or events use a separate bulk path?

## When bulk still matters

A store does not automatically replace:

- Staff apparel with a required size list
- Team or event delivery on one date
- Orientation or admissions kits
- Sponsor or tournament merchandise
- Products distributed by the school
- Unusual pieces outside the store platform

A mixed model may be cleaner than forcing everything through one storefront.

## Proof and privacy questions

- Who may use the school logo?
- Are student photographs involved?
- Who can approve public examples?
- Where do buyer and recipient details live?
- Who can access uploaded artwork?
- What consent is separate from an order response?

## Red flags

- "Free" without clear operating and financial terms
- No named owner for buyer support
- No logo approval process
- Too many products before demand is understood
- No distinction between required and optional merchandise
- A launch date promised before artwork and product review
