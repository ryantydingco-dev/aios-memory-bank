## Hero

# A camp merchandise calendar built backward from opening day.

The easiest time to improve next season is while this season's sizing, ordering, product, and deadline lessons are still visible.

Use this calendar to assign decisions early. Replace each relative window with the dates that fit your camp, suppliers, approval process, and delivery requirements.

## First, define the dates that cannot move

Write down:

- Staff arrival
- Camper opening day by session
- Orientation and training
- Parent or alumni ordering windows
- Reunions and special events
- Photo, video, and registration deadlines that need merchandise
- Last acceptable delivery date for each order
- Person authorized to approve artwork and quantities

The in-hand date, not the order date, controls the plan.

## Five to seven months before opening

### Review the previous season

- What sold or disappeared first?
- Which sizes ran short?
- What was overordered?
- Which staff pieces were actually worn?
- Which items created questions or returns?
- Which products were difficult to reorder?
- Did parent ordering create unnecessary work?
- Which deadline felt too close?

### Choose the operating model

Decide which needs belong in:

- A known-quantity bulk order
- A parent or alumni store
- A staff-only order
- A special-event order
- A planned in-season restock

Do not make one ordering model solve every audience.

## Three to five months before opening

### Build the assortment

Organize products by job:

- Staff identity and everyday wear
- Camper essentials
- Parent and alumni merchandise
- Opening-day or session pieces
- Recognition, awards, and cabin items
- Weather, waterfront, travel, or activity needs

Choose a focused core before adding novelty products.

### Confirm artwork and ownership

- Collect current logo files
- Identify old or retired marks
- Confirm colors and decoration expectations
- Decide whether sessions, cabins, dates, or names change
- Name the final approver
- Identify who owns store questions and promotion

## Eight to twelve weeks before the in-hand date

### Review product and proof details

For every item confirm:

- Product, color, and size range
- Decoration location and method
- Quantity or ordering window
- Price and approved budget
- Inventory or substitute risk
- Packaging and distribution
- Shipping destination and receiving contact
- Proof approval owner

This is a planning window, not a production guarantee. The confirmed project schedule governs.

## Four to eight weeks before the in-hand date

### Lock approvals and logistics

- Approve artwork and quantities
- Confirm final delivery addresses
- Confirm receiving hours and contacts
- Document substitutions before they become urgent
- Separate staff, camper, parent, and event cartons if required
- Schedule the first reorder review

If an input is still moving, surface it rather than assuming production will absorb the delay.

## Opening month and in-season

### Track what actually happens

Keep a simple note of:

- Fast-moving products and sizes
- Staff requests
- Camper or family questions
- Damaged or missing pieces
- Restock demand
- Products that did not earn their space
- Useful photos, with permission

For a rush need, call for a feasibility review. Product availability, artwork, production, and shipping still need to support the date.

## Within two weeks after the season

### Run a 30-minute merchandise review

Record:

1. Keep
2. Change
3. Stop
4. Test next year
5. Reorder before inventory changes

Capture approved customer feedback and photographs while the program is still easy to remember.
