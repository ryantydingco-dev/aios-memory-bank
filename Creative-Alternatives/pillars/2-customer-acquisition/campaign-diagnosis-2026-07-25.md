## Verdict: it is NOT a list-freshness problem and NOT a deliverability problem
- **Deliverability is healthy.** Bounces 0.0-0.9% (industry safe is <3%). Opens 42-69%.
  Mail is landing. Nothing to fix in the infrastructure.
- **It IS a vertical-selection problem.** Camps convert **17x better than Law National**
  on the same infrastructure, same sender, same wedge.

## Why camps and clubs win
Merch is **operationally required** there. Every camper gets a shirt; every team needs
uniforms; it's a budgeted annual line item with a known buyer (the camp director).
In law/financial/construction, swag is an afterthought with a diffuse owner. The actual
replies say it out loud: "our Marketing department already has a vendor," "those decisions
are made out of our corporate office," "not an area our shareholders have shown interest in."

## Contributing factors (secondary, but real)
1. **Season.** Camps ran in MARCH, when camps buy for summer. Law National ran in JULY,
   when firms are on vacation. 72 of 101 replies in the July campaigns were auto/OOO.
2. **Role staleness in professional services.** A large share of those auto-replies were
   "retired," "no longer with the firm," "moved on." Law admin rosters churn hard.
3. **Warmth.** CA's book is FULL of camps (Coniston, Sloane, Shibley, Awosting, Becket...).
   The camps list was adjacent to real customers. Sell where you have proof.

## The reallocation math (observed rates, not assumed)
- Continuing on law: 3,000 more leads → ~2 interested.
- Same 3,000 leads spent on camps at the observed 1-per-95: **~31 interested.**
Same inboxes, same effort, same wedge. That is the entire finding.
