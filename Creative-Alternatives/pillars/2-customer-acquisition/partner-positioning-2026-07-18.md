## The repositioning in one line

**Stop pitching as a merch vendor looking for referrals. Start recruiting as the
merch operation behind a community the partner already respects — opening a
partner lane for the first time.**

A vendor asking for referrals is a dime a dozen and reads as "please send me
business." An established operator with visible gravity in a niche, inviting a
partner into a program, reads as opportunity. CA has the second story and has
been telling the first.

## The credibility asset (what makes the second story true)

CA is not "a promo company." The book proves community dominance, most of all in
squash: USSRA/US Squash is the **#1 customer**, plus NY Squash, Open Squash (two
sites), StreetSquash, Heights Casino, World Masters, the Junior Open, HS/Middle
School Nationals, the British Open on-site stores — **the squash ecosystem alone
is ~$300K+/yr and CA is at every major event.** Same pattern at ~100+ camps and
clubs across the Northeast, 27 years deep.

## The lane Ryan actually described: Pro Shop Partners (NEW — add as lane 4)

**Who:** pro shops and shops-within-clubs — squash/tennis/racquet clubs, golf pro
shops, gyms with retail corners. Usually run by a teaching pro or club GM who
**hates the inventory**: buy-ins, sizes that don't sell, boxes in the office,
margin tied up in stock.

**The offer (Store Engine, pro-shop framing):** *"Keep your shop. Lose the
inventory."* CA runs a branded online store (and/or event pop-up supply) for the
club — no buy-in, no stock risk, no fulfillment — and the shop/pro keeps a share
of every sale. For physical shops: CA as the merch supply line behind the counter
instead of the pro fronting inventory cash.

**Why they're the perfect partner shape:** they already sell branded goods to a
member community, they already own the trust, and their #1 pain is exactly the
thing CA absorbs. And each pro is a network node — teaching pros move clubs, know
other pros, run junior programs and tournaments. One pro = many clubs over time.

**Who the outreach actually targets:** the *person*, not the club — head
pros/directors of squash & racquets, club GMs, golf head professionals, tournament
directors. They carry their book with them.

## Beachhead sequencing: squash out, not spread thin

1. **Squash first.** CA's gravity is provable there; reply rates follow familiarity
   (squash clubs already hit 4.6–5.2% cold *as customers* — as community peers with
   a partner offer it should beat that). Warm intros through the existing ecosystem
   beat cold entirely: `[KENNY]` who at US Squash / NY Squash / Open Squash would
   introduce the program to club pros?
2. **Racquet adjacency second.** Tennis, padel (exploding, all new clubs need
   everything), pickleball — same buyer psychology, CA's squash proof transfers
   ("we do this for squash's national events" lands at a padel club).
3. **Golf pro shops third.** Bigger retail culture, existing vendor relationships —
   harder, so earn it with racquet proof first.

## The copy architecture (applies to ALL partner lanes, including the built 3)

Every partner email rebuilds around three beats:

1. **Peer-proof opener** — community gravity, not services:
   *"We've handled the merch for [the national squash championships / 100+
   Northeast camps] for years"* — not *"we're a full-service promotional products
   company."* The existing launch-75 openers personalize on THEIR work (good, keep)
   but the sender identity paragraph must carry CA's gravity.
2. **The trade, framed as their upside:** you keep the relationship/shop/client;
   we absorb the part you lose money or sleep on (inventory / production /
   fulfillment / sourcing). One sentence. The economics doc's terms back it.
3. **Audition CTA, not partnership CTA:** never "want to be a partner?" (big,
   vague, commitment-flavored). Always the one-brief audition: *"send us one real
   request — an event, a restock, a client ask — and see how it runs."* The proof
   assets (sample brief, timeline, budget guide) are the attachments that make
   this concrete.

**Scarcity, honestly:** the pilot is genuinely capped (a handful of partners per
lane while CA proves the ops). Say that — "we're taking N founding partners in
[metro/sport] this year" — only with a real N Kenny stands behind. `[KENNY: N?]`
