## The Decision

Creative Alternatives should stop presenting itself primarily as a place that can put a logo on any product. That is a useful capability, but it is also the same promise made by thousands of catalogs, online printers, and local distributors.

The stronger market position is:

> **Creative Alternatives is the branded-merchandise operating partner for organizations with recurring people moments.**

Buyer-facing line:

> **Branded moments, handled.**

Supporting promise:

> **Show us the moment. We will plan, source, produce, and deliver the merch.**

The "moment" can be a camp season, tournament, new-hire class, trade show, grand opening, rebrand, safety milestone, client gift, school year, fundraiser, or quarterly employee program. This changes the sale from "do you need pens?" to "what important moment is coming up, and who is responsible for making it happen?"

The catalog remains the fulfillment layer. It should not be the positioning.

## Why This Position Fits The Business

### Internal evidence

- Creative Alternatives' 2025-2026 QuickBooks data contains about **$3.03M in recorded revenue** across 364 customers.
- After obvious classification corrections, **camps represent about 41% of recorded revenue and squash/racquet organizations about 19%**. Together they represent roughly 60%.
- **Apparel tops represent about 46% of named product revenue**. Tees alone represent about 30%.
- There are **118 2025 customers representing $306k in 2025 sales that had not yet reordered in 2026** as of the June analysis. This is a prioritized reorder list, not confirmed churn.
- The current site supports the service story: 27 years, 75,000+ orders, 2,700+ customers, 24-48 hour proofs, boutique orders through national rollouts, tight-deadline capability, and direct access to Kenny.
- The customer testimonials repeatedly praise responsiveness, deadline reliability, creative sourcing, personal attention, and the ability to handle many unrelated branded items through one relationship.

### Market evidence

- PPAI estimates that U.S. promotional-products distributor sales reached **$27.1B in 2025**, but also reports margin pressure from tariffs, freight, and client budgets. Competing only on product markup is therefore vulnerable.
- ASI identifies education, healthcare, construction, manufacturing/distribution, and financial/insurance among the largest North American buyer markets. Education alone represented an estimated **$3.6B in 2024 promo sales**.
- PPAI's 2026 consumer research found that usefulness and quality are leading reasons people keep branded merchandise. The practical implication is simple: sell fewer "random things" and more products designed around how the recipient will actually use them.
- Current industry demand is concentrated in apparel, drinkware, and bags/travel. That aligns with Creative Alternatives' existing product mix and supplier network.

### Strategic implication

Creative Alternatives already wins when the buyer values judgment, responsiveness, flexibility, and accountability. It should package those advantages into repeatable programs instead of forcing every buyer to invent an order from a catalog.

## Positioning Architecture

### Master brand position

**Category:** Managed branded merchandise

**Position:** The responsive, founder-led merchandise partner that handles complex branded moments from idea to delivery.

**Promise:** Branded moments, handled.

**Proof:** 27 years; 75,000+ orders; 2,700+ customers; 24-48 hour proofs; runs from 50 to 50,000 items; access to broad wholesale sourcing and established decoration partners. `[CONFIRM any claim before publication]`

**Personality:** Resourceful, direct, calm under deadline, high-touch, and allergic to making the buyer chase five vendors.

### Three offer families

1. **The Season and Community Engine**
   For camps, clubs, schools, teams, associations, and communities whose people want to wear the identity. Includes bulk seasonal orders, preorders, fundraising, and revenue-share stores.

2. **Swag, Handled**
   For companies buying for employees, events, clients, locations, and internal programs. Includes campaign packages and managed annual programs.

3. **Rollout and Replenishment**
   For multi-location, uniform, new-hire, rebrand, and recurring programs where consistency and reorder simplicity matter more than novelty.

### The capability stack

Creative Alternatives should explain its operating role as four connected layers:

1. **Wholesale sourcing:** SanMar, S&S Activewear, alphabroder, and specialty promotional-product suppliers provide broad access to blanks, retail-style apparel, hard goods, and unusual products. `[CONFIRM current approved supplier roster]`
2. **Decoration and production:** Diamond Graphics and Viking Solutions are core print/decoration partners. Their exact screen-printing, embroidery, transfer, kitting, rush, and specialty capabilities need a written matrix. `[CONFIRM]`
3. **Creative Alternatives ownership:** Product curation, concepting, art/proofs, quoting, vendor coordination, quality control, timeline management, customer communication, and delivery accountability.
4. **Program layer:** Stores, reorder calendars, inventory visibility, kitting, address collection, split shipping, and reporting. Some exist today; others must be built or fulfilled through a partner before they are sold as standard.

This is why Creative Alternatives is more than a reseller adding markup. The wholesalers provide product access and the print shops provide decoration capacity; Creative Alternatives turns those components into one accountable customer outcome.

### Message hierarchy

Use messages in this order:

1. **The moment or business outcome:** "Your fall conference kit arrives ready for the booth."
2. **The removed headache:** "One brief, one proof flow, one accountable person."
3. **The proof:** "27 years, 75,000+ orders, proofs in 24-48 hours."
4. **The products:** "Apparel, bags, drinkware, signage, gifts, awards, and the odd thing nobody else can find."
5. **The technology:** Modern systems and AI can support speed behind the scenes, but should not be the primary customer promise.

### What not to lead with

- "We sell promotional products."
- "Choose from thousands of items."
- "We can print anything."
- "We are AI-powered."
- "We are the cheapest."

Those claims describe capability, not a compelling reason to switch vendors.

## One Offer Architecture For Every Package

### The attraction offer

Give away thinking and visualization, not expensive production:

- Personalized concept board showing the prospect's logo on the right products for their moment.
- Three-option budget and quality plan.
- Delivery timeline working backward from the event or launch date.
- Swag-spend teardown: review the last order and identify one quality, speed, or process improvement.
- Digital store preview for member-based organizations.

Physical samples should be reserved for qualified opportunities, paid pilots, or deposits credited to an order.

### The three-tier menu

1. **Essentials:** Products, decoration, proof, and delivery to one location.
2. **Managed Campaign:** Essentials plus curation, project management, kitting, personalization, and split shipping.
3. **Program:** Managed Campaign plus annual calendar, store/reorder workflow, inventory or replenishment, reporting, and recurring planning.

Always present Program first as the anchor, then Managed Campaign, then Essentials. This makes the middle option easier to choose and gives the buyer a clear path to expand.

### Bonuses that add value without destroying margin

- Free logo cleanup or digitizing above an order threshold.
- Free digital concept board.
- Launch email and social copy for stores or fundraisers.
- Event carton labels and packing map.
- Reorder calendar setup.
- One additional design direction.
- Locked artwork and decoration specs for future consistency.

### Guarantees Creative Alternatives can responsibly consider

- Proof sent within an agreed window after a complete brief.
- Approved-date delivery promise only when the customer meets art, quantity, and payment cutoffs.
- Reprint or remedy for confirmed decoration defects.
- Paid pilot credited toward rollout.
- No inventory commitment for a preorder or approved store model.

Do not guarantee event results, store sales, employee satisfaction, or delivery dates that depend on unconfirmed supplier capacity.

### Honest urgency and scarcity

- Production cutoff tied to the actual event date.
- Seasonal order window tied to camp or school opening.
- Limited number of new managed programs accepted while the workflow is being piloted.
- Supplier inventory reservation deadline.
- Founding-program terms for a defined pilot cohort, only if Kenny approves the economics.

## The Money Model

Acquisition.com's public `$100M Money Models` materials group monetization into attraction offers, upsells, downsells, and continuity. Creative Alternatives can apply that structure without discounting the core product.

### Model 1: Customer-financed acquisition

The first paid order should ideally cover the acquisition cost and sales effort. Until retention is proven for a new segment:

```text
Maximum test CAC <= first-order gross profit dollars
```

Once repeat behavior is measured, the allowable CAC can be based on expected 12-month gross profit, not revenue.

### Model 2: Offer stack

```text
Attraction: personalized digital concept + three-tier plan
Core sale: bulk order or managed campaign
Upsell: premium product, extra SKU, personalization, kitting, rush, split shipping
Downsell: fewer SKUs, standard products, single-location delivery, preorder, phased rollout
Continuity: reorder calendar, company store, per-hire program, quarterly plan, annual contract
Referral: customer or partner introduction after a successful delivery
```

### Model 3: Price around gross profit, not only markup

Ryan stated that Creative Alternatives commonly applies a **30-40% markup** `[CONFIRM exact method and which costs it covers]`. Markup is not the same as gross margin:

| Landed variable cost | Markup | Sell price | Gross profit | Gross margin |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $10,000 | 30% | $13,000 | $3,000 | 23.1% |
| $10,000 | 40% | $14,000 | $4,000 | 28.6% |

If the target is a 35% gross margin, the required markup on fully loaded variable cost is about 53.8%.

Use this formula in the quote system:

```text
Minimum sell price = total variable cost / (1 - target gross margin)
```

Total variable cost should include blanks, decoration, inbound freight, spoilage allowance, kitting labor, packaging, outsourced art, transaction fees, and any other order-specific cost. Tax and outbound shipping should follow a consistent policy.

### Model 4: Add service and logistics profit deliberately

Bulk product margin remains important, but the buyer also values project ownership. Protect profit through clearly priced or bundled:

- Creative and complex sourcing
- Rush management
- Personalization
- Kitting and assembly
- Storage
- Pick and pack
- Address collection
- Split shipping
- Inventory reporting
- Annual planning

The customer is not merely paying for the blank item. They are paying to make a consequential moment happen without failure.

### Model 5: Continuity before novelty

The most valuable new offer is often the second order built into the first:

- Camp order includes an in-season reorder reserve and next-season planning date.
- Crew rollout includes new-hire and replacement ordering.
- Event kit includes the next three event dates.
- Client-gift campaign includes a year-round referral-gift menu.
- Rebrand includes a replenishment store.
- New-hire pilot includes a per-hire workflow.
