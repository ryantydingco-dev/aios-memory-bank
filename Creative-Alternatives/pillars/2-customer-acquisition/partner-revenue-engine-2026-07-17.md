# Creative Alternatives Partner Revenue Engine

> Created 2026-07-17. This converts partnerships from sponsorship/directory research into a measurable revenue channel. Human approval is required for every commercial term, referral payout, customer introduction, and public claim.

## Decision

Creative Alternatives will build a **partner-sourced revenue motion** alongside Smartlead, Sendr, Salesfinity, inbound, reactivation, and reorders.

The goal is not a giant affiliate program. The goal is to recruit a small number of **trigger-adjacent partners** who encounter merchandise demand before CA does and can produce repeat opportunities.

The core partner promise:

> **You keep the relationship. Creative Alternatives handles the merchandise work.**

CA can provide product curation, quoting, proofs, production coordination, stores, gifting, events, and delivery only where those capabilities are operationally verified.

## Why this can work

The best partners already hear statements such as:

- “We need giveaways for this event.”
- “Can you source new-hire kits?”
- “Our client wants apparel.”
- “We need a company store.”
- “Can you handle sponsor gifts?”
- “We need this delivered before the conference.”

CA should partner with people who hear the need while planning the event, brand, website, hiring program, facility, association calendar, or campaign.

One productive partner can introduce many buyers. This is a better multiplier than acquiring every account independently.

## Priority partner lanes

### Tier 1 — Pilot now

#### 1. Branding, marketing, and creative agencies

**Why they are close to the trigger:** They create brands, campaigns, launches, events, and client experiences but frequently do not want sourcing, production, freight, and fulfillment work.

**Offer:** **White-Label Merch Desk**

- Agency keeps client ownership.
- CA creates curated product directions and production-ready quotes.
- Agency chooses transparent referral or wholesale/reseller mode.
- CA does not market around the agency to its client.
- One named CA contact owns execution.
- Co-branded or white-label presentation option after process validation.

**Best incentive:** Wholesale/reseller spread or a share of collected gross profit on referred work.

**First proof asset:** An agency-facing sample brief showing how a launch/event request becomes a curated shortlist, proof, and delivery plan.

#### 2. Event planners, conference producers, venues, and retreat planners

**Why they are close to the trigger:** They know the date, audience, venue, sponsors, budget, and delivery constraints before most vendors.

**Offer:** **Planner Merch Concierge**

- Date feasibility review.
- Three-option product direction by audience and budget.
- Sponsor/staff/attendee merchandise planning.
- Venue and in-hands-date coordination.
- Planner remains the strategic owner.
- Emergency escalation path for qualified projects.

**Best incentive:** Collected gross-profit share, planner credit, or reciprocal preferred-vendor status.

**First proof asset:** Event Merchandise Timeline plus a sample attendee/staff/sponsor plan.

#### 3. HR consultants, recruiters, PEOs, fractional People leaders, and benefits brokers

**Why they are close to the trigger:** They see hiring, onboarding, retention, recognition, office openings, mergers, and employee events.

**Offer:** **Employee Moment Merch Partner**

- New-hire kits.
- Employee recognition and milestone programs.
- Holiday/seasonal gifting.
- Remote-team shipping where supported.
- Company-store consultation.

**Best incentive:** Referral gross-profit share, client benefit/credit, or co-branded workshop/content.

**First proof asset:** New-Hire Kit Budget Guide and a simple good/better/best program brief.

### Tier 2 — Add after one Tier-1 model converts

#### 4. Website designers and ecommerce consultants

Trigger: client needs a company store, uniforms, merchandise, launch kits, or physical brand assets.

Offer: **Physical Brand Extension Partner** — CA handles the merchandise/store layer that follows the website and brand work.

#### 5. Printers, sign shops, embroidery shops, packaging companies, and photographers

Trigger: a buyer asks for a capability they do not offer or a project outside their equipment/fulfillment fit.

Offer: **No-Dead-End Referral Network** — each business keeps its core work and hands off noncompetitive requests under a written no-poach rule.

#### 6. Trade-show booth builders, AV firms, exhibit houses, hotels, venues, and destination-management companies

Trigger: exhibitor or organizer is already buying around a fixed event date.

Offer: **Booth-to-Swag Bundle** — coordinated branded staff apparel, giveaways, attendee items, sponsor gifts, and venue delivery.

#### 7. Association managers, software companies, consultants, and insurance providers serving concentrated niches

Examples: camp-management software, racquet organizations, legal administrators, school/club networks.

Offer: **Member Merch Benefit** — educational resource, office hours, preferred consultation, and an attributable member path. Avoid buying logo-only sponsorships.

#### 8. Commercial real-estate brokers, office designers, coworking operators, and movers

Trigger: new office, relocation, expansion, opening event, or team reset.

Offer: **New Space Launch Kit** — employee apparel, office-opening gifts, signage-adjacent merchandise, client invitations, and launch-event items.

## Incentive menu

Commercial incentives must be based on **collected gross profit**, not top-line revenue, unless finance approves a specific exception. CA’s margins vary materially by vertical, and a revenue-percentage promise can make lower-margin jobs unattractive.

### Model A — Qualified introduction

Best for consultants and occasional referrers.

- Partner makes a permissioned introduction.
- CA owns discovery, quote, and fulfillment.
- Partner receives an approved share of **collected first-order gross profit** after customer payment and final job-cost reconciliation.
- No payout on tax, shipping pass-through, refunds, credits, or unpaid invoices.
- Payout window, cap, and eligibility require written approval.

### Model B — Recurring account share

Best for partners who create repeat programs.

- Lower share than Model A.
- Applies to collected gross profit from attributable orders for a defined time window.
- Renewed only if the partner remains meaningfully involved or continues sourcing opportunities.
- Account ownership and excluded pre-existing CA customers are documented before the first introduction.

### Model C — White-label/reseller

Best for agencies, event planners, printers, and consultants who want to own the client commercial relationship.

- CA provides an approved net/wholesale quote.
- Partner sets its own client price and retains the spread.
- Partner may own billing only after credit, tax, collections, and cancellation responsibility are defined.
- CA’s production floor and margin floor remain protected.

### Model D — Partner credit instead of cash

Best for partners who also purchase merchandise.

- Partner earns an approved CA merchandise credit after a referred order is paid.
- Credit can fund the partner’s own event, team gear, or client gifting.
- The perceived value can exceed CA’s economic cost, but finance must record the true cost and expiration.

### Model E — Client benefit

Best for trusted advisors who prefer not to accept cash.

- The referred client receives an approved benefit: sample credit, design/art credit, shipping allowance, upgraded item, or program consultation.
- Never promise “free” without defining qualification and cost.
- This lets the partner look valuable without creating an ethical/compliance concern.

### Model F — Co-marketing and lead exchange

Best for partners with meaningful audiences.

- Co-authored resource, webinar, office hours, event clinic, newsletter, or case study.
- Each side uses consented attribution and its own follow-up.
- No raw list sharing or purchased member data.
- Each campaign has a unique landing page/UTM and explicit lead ownership.

### Model G — Milestone bonus

Best for high-potential partners.

- Base incentive plus a one-time approved bonus after a defined number of **paid, profitable** referred orders or gross-profit threshold.
- Avoid volume bonuses based only on introductions, leads, or low-margin revenue.

## Creative partner hooks

### 1. “Bring us the weird request”

Give agencies and planners a direct channel for unusual, hard-to-source, or deadline-sensitive requests. CA becomes the partner that prevents them from saying no.

### 2. Partner-only deadline desk

A private intake route for verified partners. It collects in-hands date, ZIP, quantity, artwork status, and flexibility. A human confirms feasibility. Do not market a guaranteed rush SLA until operations proves one.

### 3. Three-options-on-demand

For qualified briefs, provide good/better/best directions with the tradeoffs explained. The partner can present useful choices without searching a catalog.

### 4. Physical-brand add-on

Bundle CA into branding and website projects as the physical implementation layer: launch kits, uniforms, event merchandise, store assortment, and client gifts.

### 5. Partner sample library

Create a small rotating sample kit that selected partners can use in client meetings. Track every kit, product, recipient, and resulting opportunity. Samples are sales tools, not unconditional giveaways.

### 6. Co-branded buyer clinics

Run 20-minute virtual sessions:

- Event Merch Office Hours
- New-Hire Kit Clinic
- Company Store Fit Review
- Holiday Gift Planning Session
- Brand-to-Merch Review

The partner provides the trusted audience; CA provides useful operational expertise.

### 7. No-poach promise

A plain-language written rule:

- CA will not solicit unrelated services to the partner’s client.
- CA will identify the partner as relationship owner where applicable.
- CA will document direct and referred opportunities before quoting.
- Exceptions for existing CA relationships are disclosed before work begins.

### 8. Saved-brand concierge

For repeat partner/client programs, preserve approved artwork, decoration details, product specs, shipping rules, and prior-order context so the next order starts from a known state.

### 9. Referral-funded partner merch

Allow an approved partner to accumulate credits toward its own conference booth, staff apparel, or client gifting. This turns successful introductions into visible CA proof inside the partner’s own audience.

### 10. “Second opinion” quote review

Partners can send an existing merchandise quote for an apples-to-apples review of product, decoration, setup, freight, deadline, and fulfillment assumptions. CA does not promise to beat price; it helps the partner identify risk and alternatives.

## Partner qualification score

Score 0–2 for each dimension:

1. **Trigger proximity:** How often does the partner encounter an active merch need?
2. **Account multiplier:** Can one partner produce multiple opportunities per year?
3. **Trust:** Does the buyer rely on the partner’s recommendation?
4. **Noncompetition:** Is CA complementary rather than threatening?
5. **Economics:** Are likely orders and margins attractive?
6. **Operational fit:** Can CA fulfill the common requests reliably?
7. **Access:** Is there a reachable owner/leader and a credible warm or researched approach?
8. **Proof potential:** Can the partnership create case studies, content, links, or events?

Interpretation:

- **13–16:** Tier A — founder-led recruitment and custom terms.
- **9–12:** Tier B — standard pilot offer.
- **5–8:** Nurture/co-marketing only.
- **0–4:** Do not pursue.

## 30-day pilot

### Pilot lanes

Run only three lanes initially:

1. Branding/marketing agencies
2. Event/retreat/conference planners
3. HR/recruiting/People consultants

### Target set

- Research 15 qualified targets per lane.
- Select the best 8 per lane for personalized outreach.
- Seek 5 fit conversations per lane.
- Activate no more than 2–3 partners per lane initially.

These are activity targets, not revenue forecasts.

### Week 1 — package the program

- Confirm which CA services can be promised.
- Approve incentive models and margin floors with Kenny/finance.
- Create one-page partner overview.
- Create the partner brief/intake.
- Create no-poach and lead-registration terms.
- Build one sample asset for each lane.
- Configure HubSpot partner properties and pipeline attribution.

### Week 2 — recruit the first cohort

- Build 45-target research pool.
- Score all targets.
- Personalized Smartlead email to the top 24.
- Sendr connection/proof touch to high-value targets.
- Salesfinity calls only to engaged or warm partner prospects.
- Ask current customers, suppliers, and friends for warm introductions.

### Week 3 — run fit conversations

Ask:

- When do clients ask you for merchandise?
- What do you do today?
- What goes wrong?
- Do you want to remain client-facing or hand the opportunity off?
- Which incentive is acceptable in your business?
- What would make you comfortable referring one live project?
- What must CA never do with your customer?

The success criterion is not “signed partnership.” It is one permissioned live opportunity or a scheduled co-marketing action.

### Week 4 — activate and review

- Register first opportunities.
- Deliver shortlists/quotes quickly.
- Record response, quote, margin, customer outcome, and partner experience.
- Collect feedback from partner and buyer.
- Keep, revise, or stop each lane based on profitable opportunities and operational burden.

## Using the existing outreach stack

### Smartlead — partner recruitment and education

Use a separate partner campaign and sender identity. Do not mix partner recruitment with end-buyer campaigns.

Sequence concept:

1. Specific overlap observed.
2. Concrete “you keep the client; we handle merch” model.
3. Useful lane-specific asset or example.
4. Ask for a 15-minute fit conversation or one live project test.

### Sendr — trust and demonstration

Use partner-specific pages, not prospect merch mockups by default:

- Agency page: white-label process and sample deliverable.
- Planner page: date-safe event workflow.
- HR page: employee-moment program examples.

### Salesfinity — engaged partner follow-up

Call after a meaningful email open/click, reply, mutual connection, introduction, or event interaction. The goal is a fit conversation and one pilot opportunity—not a hard partnership close.

### HubSpot — source of partnership truth

Track:

- Partner organization and type
- Partner owner
- Status
- Incentive model
- Lead-registration date
- Introduced company/contact
- Existing CA relationship check
- Opportunity/deal ID
- Quote value
- Estimated and reconciled gross profit
- Payout/credit status
- Revenue and gross profit by partner
- Last activity and next action

QuickBooks remains the money truth. Incentives are payable only after reconciliation and collection.

## Partner terms that must be explicit

- What constitutes a qualified introduction
- How attribution is established
- Existing-customer and duplicate-lead handling
- Customer ownership and communication
- Non-solicitation/no-poach behavior
- Pricing visibility
- White-label versus disclosed-CA delivery
- Who contracts, invoices, collects, and bears credit risk
- Returns, cancellations, errors, and chargebacks
- When incentives are earned and paid
- Taxes and required reporting
- Termination and survival of existing orders
- Data privacy and consent
- No exclusivity unless economically justified and approved

Use counsel/accounting review before scaling recurring payouts or formal reseller structures.

## Scoreboard

Track weekly by lane and partner:

- Targets researched
- Fit score
- Outreach sent
- Positive replies
- Fit conversations
- Partners activated
- Live opportunities introduced
- Quotes
- Paid orders
- Revenue
- Reconciled gross profit
- Incentives paid/credited
- Net gross profit after incentive
- Repeat opportunities
- Days from introduction to quote
- Partner satisfaction/issues

The North Star is **net collected gross profit from partner-sourced orders**, not partner count.

## First decisions

1. Approve the three pilot lanes.
2. Choose which incentive models CA will pilot.
3. Define the margin floor and payout cap.
4. Decide disclosed CA versus white-label rules.
5. Name the internal owner and response expectation.
6. Confirm whether partners may receive cash, credits, client benefits, or a choice.
7. Select the first 24 outreach targets after scoring.
