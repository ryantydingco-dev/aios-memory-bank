## The offer in one sentence

**We build your [camp / club / box / academy] a branded store your people actually want to buy from — we design it, stock it, ship it, and run support. You earn a cut of every sale and never touch inventory, money, or a single box.**

Internal program name: **The Store Engine.** Buyer-facing name: **"Your Done-For-You Team Store"** (swap "Team" for Camp / Club / Box / Studio per segment).

---

## Why this is a home run (the value equation)

Hormozi's equation: **(Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice).** A home-run offer maxes the top and crushes the bottom. Here's how this one scores — and the lever that makes it unfair:

| Lever | Most promo pitches | The Store Engine |
|-------|-------------------|------------------|
| **Dream outcome** | "We'll print your shirts" (a chore) | Your members proudly rep your brand everywhere + a hands-off revenue stream — without becoming a merch company |
| **Perceived likelihood** | "Trust us, it'll look good" | **They see their actual store before they say yes** (AI mockup of their logo on real products) + 27 yrs / 75,000 orders of proof |
| **Time delay** | Weeks of back-and-forth | Live in ~7 days from "go." Mockup in their inbox in touch #1 |
| **Effort & sacrifice** | They manage art, orders, inventory, shipping, complaints | **They do nothing.** No upfront cost. No inventory. No risk. We run all of it |

The unfair lever is the bottom-right two boxes: **near-zero effort and near-zero risk on their side.** They are not buying a service that costs money and time — they're being handed a finished asset that pays *them*. That is what turns a 3% reply rate into a 10%+ one.

## The value stack (everything they get)

Lead with the whole stack — it makes the "you pay nothing" reveal land harder.

1. **A custom-designed branded store** — their logo, their colors, a curated product line picked for their audience. Built free.
2. **The full CA catalog** — apparel, hoodies, hats, drinkware, bags, lanyards, the works. "We print anything on everything."
3. **In-house art team** — CA's designers do every mockup and proof. 24–48 hr proof turnaround (their signature speed).
4. **Done-for-you fulfillment** — production, packing, shipping, returns, customer service. All CA.
5. **Zero inventory, zero upfront cost, zero risk** — nothing is pre-bought; items are made on demand.
6. **Revenue share on every order** — the org earns **10–12%** `[CONFIRM exact]` of sales, paid out on a set cadence `[CONFIRM cadence]`.
7. **A launch kit** — ready-made announcement email + social posts so their first drop actually sells (this is what makes the rev-share real instead of theoretical).
8. **Seasonal drop planning** — we line up new designs to their calendar (camp opening, season start, anniversary, championship, holiday) so the store keeps earning.

> Stack-slip rule for outreach: **never list all 8 in a cold email.** Lead with the mockup + the "you earn, you do nothing" headline. The full stack is for the call/demo. Cold copy sells the click; the stack sells the close.

## The wedge: the AI store mockup (touch #1, every time)

This is the single biggest reply-rate lever (`plans/ai-growth-plays.md` Play #1) and the reason the offer beats every other promo distributor in the inbox.

**The move:** before the first email or DM, generate a real mockup of *their* store — their logo on a hoodie, a tee, a tumbler — and lead with the image. Show, don't pitch.

- **Email:** embed the mockup image. Subject + one line + "want to see the rest?"
- **LinkedIn (Sendr video/voice DM):** record a 20-second screen-share walking through *their* mockup. This is why Sendr matters — the video plays in-conversation.
- **Call (Salesfinity):** "I actually mocked up what a store for [Camp] could look like — want me to send it over?" → gets the email opened.

Production line and tooling for mockups at scale are in `outbound-gtm-playbook.md` → "Mockup production line."

## What this offer is NOT (keep motions clean)

- It is **not** the core promo-distribution business (Offer 1, the $3.2M word-of-mouth engine). That grows by retention/reorder/referral, not cold outbound — see `context/offer.md`.
- It is **not** a corporate/SaaS play. The data is clear: corporate (0.3%) and SaaS (1.1%) flop; camps (10.1%) and clubs (4.6%) win. The offer is tuned for member-based, identity-driven, parent/owner-funded orgs.
- The web store is the **front door**, not the whole house — every store customer is a future core-distribution account.
