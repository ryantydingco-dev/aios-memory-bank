## What the evidence says

### Creative Alternatives evidence

- Camps produced a **10.1% SmartLead reply rate** across 665 leads; squash clubs
  produced **5.2%** across 173. K–12 produced **0.3%** across 2,223 and no interested
  replies. Lead with camps and squash proof; treat schools as a measured call test, not
  a proven channel.
- Winning CA campaigns used a concrete seasonal reason and named peer proof. Generic
  “organizations like yours” language underperformed.
- The free mockup/store-preview wedge has not yet been isolated in a controlled CA call
  test. Use it as the next-step hypothesis and measure it; do not call it proven.
- Safe proof today: family-run since 1999, 2,700+ organizations served, 25,000+ ledger
  orders, digital proof in 24–48 hours, and deep camp/squash experience. Customer names
  require Kenny's approval before cold use.

Sources: `smartlead-campaign-analysis.md`, `context/business-info.md`, and
`proof-statement-bank.md`.

### External call and buyer research

- Gong's analysis of 300M+ cold calls reports an **11.18% success rate** for a tailored
  permission opener versus **2.15%** for “Did I catch you at a bad time?” Stating the
  reason for the call correlated with a **2.1x** higher success rate.
- Gong's top five objections—Not Interested, Product Fit, No Budget, Not My
  Responsibility, and Hang Up—represent **74%** of objections. Its median successful
  cold call lasted **4.8 minutes** and normally passed at least two objections.
- In Gong's analysis of 67,149 sales calls, stronger objection handlers paused after an
  objection five times longer than weaker performers.
- PPAI's 2024 end-buyer research says product selection is influenced by quality (85%),
  price (78%), supplier reliability (51%), and uniqueness (44%); 57% consider distributor
  support/customer service very important. PPAI's 2025 distributor research says buyers
  under budget pressure most often reduce quantity (60%) or product tier (51.1%), while
  only 11.1% cut customization or supporting services.

## The operating posture

The call is not trying to close an order. It is trying to earn one of four next steps:

1. Permission to send a relevant mockup or store preview.
2. A specific buying brief for Maclaine to quote.
3. The correct buyer and a warm transfer/introduction.
4. A dated callback tied to a real buying window.

Do not “overcome” a true mismatch. Diagnose it quickly. Persist through a reflexive
brush-off once or twice; respect a clear no immediately.

## The core cold-call script

### 1. Tailored permission opener

> “Hi [First], Ryan Tydingco with Creative Alternatives. I know I'm calling you out of
> the blue. Can I take 25 seconds to tell you why I picked [Organization], and then you
> can tell me if it's irrelevant?”

If yes, continue. If they say “make it quick,” that is permission—do not repeat the
intro.

Do not use “How have you been?” with a stranger. Gong has reported strong historical
results for it, but it can imply familiarity that does not exist. CA's trust posture is
more important than a borrowed pattern interrupt.

### 2. Reason + proof + relevance question

> “I saw [specific dated trigger/buying moment]. We've been family-run since 1999 and
> do a lot of work with [camps / racquet organizations / schools / organizations with
> events]. We handle the design, production, and fulfillment so the organization isn't
> stuck managing [inventory / individual orders / product sourcing]. How are you
> handling [specific merch moment] today?”

This section must use the research agent's `reason_to_call`. Never substitute “I thought
you might need swag.”

### 3. Listen and qualify

Use their answer, then ask no more than two of these:

- “What are you currently doing for that?”
- “What works well with the current setup?”
- “Where does it become a headache?”
- “Who besides you is involved?”
- “When would you need it in hand or live?”
- “Is the goal fundraising, member access, an event, staff gear, or something else?”

Do not interrogate. Reflect their words before the next question.

### 4. Make one relevant recommendation

**Store motion:**

> “Based on that, the relevant piece is a curated [camp/club/school] store. Families or
> members order directly; we handle design, production, individual shipping, and
> support. You don't hold inventory, and the organization receives a share of sales.”

**Bulk/event/gifting motion:**

> “Based on that, the relevant piece is giving you a tight shortlist built around the
> audience, quantity, and in-hands date—not another giant catalog. We handle the art,
> sourcing, production, and delivery.”

### 5. Close for the smallest visual next step

> “Rather than asking you to picture it, I can put your logo on a few relevant options
> and send the preview for you to react to. No commitment. Worth seeing?”

If yes:

> “Great. What's the best email, and is [verified logo/source] the current mark? What
> matters most: [one relevant choice A] or [choice B]?”

Confirm the next action and timing. Never promise price, margin, inventory, or delivery
without Maclaine's confirmation.

## Lane modules

Insert one module into the core script. Do not stack several.

| Lane | Reason/proof module | Relevance question |
|---|---|---|
| Camps | “I saw [2027 registration / reunion / fall-family event]. Seven of our 15 largest 2025 customers were camps, so we know the seasonal rush.” | “What can families buy once camp ends today?” |
| Squash/racquet | “I saw [league / tournament / member event]. We've worked in the squash community for decades and much of that book grew by referral.” | “How are members and teams getting club gear now?” |
| School/PTA/booster | “I saw [season / fundraiser / spirit event]. We build stores so families order directly instead of volunteers collecting sizes and boxes.” | “Who owns spirit wear, and where does the admin fall today?” |
| Corporate gifting | “I saw [hiring / anniversary / Q4 event]. We help narrow the choices and handle art, sourcing, and delivery.” | “Is the priority employees, clients, or an event audience?” |
| Trade show/event | “I saw you're exhibiting at [event] on [date]. I'm calling before the normal production crunch.” | “Are booth giveaways and staff apparel already locked?” |

Named customer proof may replace the generalized proof only after Kenny approves that
name for outbound.

## The objection system: PAUSE

Use this on every objection, including one not listed below:

1. **Pause.** One full beat. Do not step on the buyer.
2. **Acknowledge.** “That's fair,” “Makes sense,” or “Thanks for telling me.”
3. **Understand.** Ask one short either/or or clarification question.
4. **Solve only the stated issue.** One or two sentences, no feature dump.
5. **Earn a micro-next-step.** A mockup, correct owner, dated callback, or clean exit.

Universal reset for an unexpected objection:

> “That's fair. When you say [their exact words], is the main issue [likely meaning A]
> or [likely meaning B]?”

If their answer reveals a true mismatch, disqualify graciously. Never rebut the same
objection more than twice.

## Objection library

### Brush-offs and interruption objections

| Prospect says | Ryan says | Desired outcome |
|---|---|---|
| “Not interested.” | “Totally fair. Is that because branded merchandise isn't a priority, or because you already have it covered?” If they repeat no: “Understood—I'll let you go.” | Diagnose once or exit |
| “I'm busy.” | “Understood. Let me give you the 15-second reason now; if it sounds relevant, we'll choose a better time.” | Earn 15 seconds or schedule |
| “Call me later.” | “Happy to. So I don't call blindly again, is this something that comes up around [verified season/event], or not really?” | Dated callback or disqualify |
| “Just email me.” | “Absolutely. To make it useful, should I send the store/no-inventory idea or options for [event/use case]?” Then confirm when to follow up. | Relevant email + follow-up |
| “Is this a cold call?” | “It is. I researched [Organization] because of [trigger]. Give me 20 seconds, and you can decide whether I missed.” | Honest permission |
| “Where did you get my number?” | “From [truthful source]. I was trying to reach the person responsible for [use case]. If you'd rather I not call again, I'll mark that now.” | Transparency or suppression |
| “Take me off your list / don't call again.” | “Absolutely. I'll mark that now. Sorry for the interruption.” End the call and suppress immediately. | Compliance; no recovery attempt |
| “I thought you were someone else.” | “Sorry about that. I'm Ryan with Creative Alternatives. Since I have you, may I take 15 seconds to explain why I called?” | Reset once |
| Silence / skepticism | “I know it was unexpected. The short version is [one-sentence reason]. Is that completely off base?” | Invite correction |
| Hang-up | Do not immediately redial. Log it. Use the approved future cadence only if no DNC signal exists. | Protect reputation |

### Ownership and process objections

| Prospect says | Ryan says | Desired outcome |
|---|---|---|
| “Not my responsibility.” | “Thanks—that helps. Who owns [spirit wear / member gear / events / gifting], and is there a good way to reach them?” | Correct buyer |
| “You need to speak with the board/committee.” | “Makes sense. What do they need to see to decide—a visual, economics, or fulfillment details? I can make you something easy to forward.” | Forwardable preview + process |
| “We need approval/procurement.” | “Understood. What is the first approval step, and what vendor information is required before anyone evaluates ideas?” | Map process |
| “We're under contract.” | “Makes sense. When does that arrangement renew, and are there gaps or overflow work it doesn't cover?” | Renewal date or secondary need |
| “Talk to our current vendor.” | “Happy to work through the right process. Are they the decision-maker, or should your team first approve the concept and use case?” | Clarify authority |
| Gatekeeper asks why | “I'm trying to reach whoever owns [specific program]. I noticed [trigger] and have one relevant idea—not a general catalog pitch.” | Name/transfer |

### Existing solution objections

| Prospect says | Ryan says | Desired outcome |
|---|---|---|
| “We already have a vendor.” | “That makes sense, and I'm not asking you to replace a good partner. What do they do especially well, and is there anything they don't cover—stores, individual fulfillment, rushes, or creative options?” | Find a real gap or exit |
| “We already have an online store.” | “Good—you've already validated the model. Is it producing the participation you want, and who handles new designs, promotion, returns, and support?” | Diagnose performance/admin gap |
| “We do it in-house.” | “Understood. Which part do you want to keep in-house, and where—if anywhere—does volume or fulfillment strain the team?” | Overflow opportunity or exit |
| “We use Printful/another POD platform.” | “Makes sense. Is your team satisfied with product quality, design help, member experience, and what the organization earns? If yes, I won't manufacture a problem.” | Honest comparison |
| “Our local printer handles it.” | “That's valuable, especially when they are reliable. Is there any work outside their sweet spot—individual shipping, specialty items, or larger seasonal drops?” | Complement, not attack |
| “We tried merch before and it didn't sell.” | “That's useful context. Was the problem the products, the designs, price, or that nobody launched it? I wouldn't recommend repeating the same setup.” | Identify failure cause |

### Money objections

| Prospect says | Ryan says | Desired outcome |
|---|---|---|
| “We have no budget.” — store | “Understood. The store model doesn't require the organization to pre-buy inventory; families or members order directly. Is the real issue budget, or not wanting another program to manage?” | Clarify budget vs bandwidth |
| “We have no budget.” — bulk | “Makes sense. Is there no approved budget yet, or has it already been allocated elsewhere?” | Timing/budget process |
| “You're too expensive.” | “Thanks for saying it. Is the concern unit price, total spend, or what you're getting for it?” Do not defend a number; route pricing to Maclaine. | Diagnose price objection |
| “Can you beat this price?” | “We can compare it accurately once we match product, decoration, quantity, freight, and deadline. Maclaine owns that review—I won't invent a number on the phone.” | Apples-to-apples quote handoff |
| “What's the catch?” | “For the store model, CA earns its margin when an order is produced, and the organization receives a share. There is no upfront store or inventory purchase. Maclaine confirms the exact commercial terms.” | Transparent economics |
| “How much do we earn?” | “It depends on the approved product and store structure. Maclaine will confirm the exact amount; I don't want to make up a payout.” | Qualified handoff |
| “Send pricing.” | “Happy to route that. To price something real, I need product/use case, quantity, decoration, and in-hands date. Which of those do you already know?” | Complete quote brief |

Do not discount reflexively. PPAI data indicates buyers under budget pressure more often
reduce quantity or product tier than supporting service. Preserve the outcome and offer
tradeoffs only after Maclaine approves them.

### Need, timing, and demand objections

| Prospect says | Ryan says | Desired outcome |
|---|---|---|
| “We don't need anything.” | “Understood. Does that mean this season is already covered, or merchandise simply isn't part of your program?” | Timing or disqualify |
| “It's too early.” | “Fair. What month do you normally choose products or open the store? I'll use your timing, not mine.” | Buying-window callback |
| “It's too late.” | “Understood. What's the in-hands date? I won't promise feasibility, but Maclaine can tell us quickly whether there is a responsible option.” | Feasibility handoff |
| “Our people won't buy.” | “That may be right. What happened the last time—wrong products, wrong price, or weak promotion? A preview can test the creative, but I won't promise demand.” | Learn or exit |
| “Our audience is too small.” | “Possibly. How many likely buyers are there and how often does the need recur? If the economics don't fit, I'll say so.” | Qualify honestly |
| “We don't want to promote another thing.” | “That's fair. The store only works if there is at least one channel to launch it. If you don't want to share it once, we shouldn't build it.” | Confirm minimum commitment |
| “We can't share our email list.” | “You don't need to give us the list. Your team can share the store link through the channels you already control.” | Remove data concern |
| “We only buy once a year.” | “That's useful. When is the decision made—not the delivery date—so I can be relevant at the right time?” | Decision-date callback |

| Prospect says | Ryan says | Desired outcome |
|---|---|---|

| “How do I know you're legitimate?” | “Fair question. We're family-run since 1999 and have served 2,700+ organizations. I'll send our site and the specific preview; you can verify us before taking another step.” | Credibility follow-up |

## Voicemail

Leave one short voicemail on the first attempt only unless live data proves another
cadence works better:

> “Hi [First], Ryan Tydingco with Creative Alternatives. I noticed [specific trigger]
> and had one idea for [specific merch/store outcome]. I'll send a short note with the
> context so you can decide if it's relevant. I'm at [number].”

Gong reports voicemail lowered later connect rate in its dataset but increased email
reply rate from 2.73% to 5.87%. Because CA uses both phone and email, test one voicemail
plus a same-day email against no voicemail; do not assume the result will transfer.

## Call disposition and learning loop

After every live conversation, record:

- lane and trigger type;
- research tier;
- buyer title;
- first objection, second objection, and verbatim wording;
- response used;
- outcome: wrong person, no fit, callback, send info, mockup/store preview, quote brief,
  meeting, DNC;
- dated next action.

Use these normalized objection codes:

`not_interested`, `product_fit`, `no_budget`, `wrong_person`, `busy`, `send_email`,
`existing_vendor`, `in_house`, `contract`, `timing`, `price`, `no_demand`, `bandwidth`,
`approval`, `quality_risk`, `fulfillment_risk`, `compliance`, `dnc`, `other`.

After the first 100 live conversations:

1. Rank objections by frequency and qualified-next-step conversion.
2. Compare outcomes by script opener, lane, trigger, and research tier.
3. Keep one control script.
4. Change only one element for the next 100 conversations.

CA's own call recordings and dispositions then outrank every external benchmark in this
document.

## Non-negotiables

- No fake familiarity, fake referrals, or invented research.
- No arguing with a clear no.
- No pricing, payout, margin, inventory, or delivery promise without Maclaine.
- No unapproved customer names.
- No vague “eco-friendly” claims; provide verified documentation.
- A DNC request is acknowledged, suppressed, and ended immediately.
- Every yes receives the promised next step on time.
