# CA Cold-Call Cheat Sheet

Full evidence and objection library: `cold-call-playbook.md`

## Open

> “Hi [First], Ryan Tydingco with Creative Alternatives. I know I'm calling you out of
> the blue. Can I take 25 seconds to tell you why I picked [Organization], and then you
> can tell me if it's irrelevant?”

## Reason

> “I saw [specific trigger]. We've been family-run since 1999 and do a lot of work with
> [peer type]. We handle design, production, and fulfillment so you aren't stuck with
> [relevant headache]. How are you handling [specific buying moment] today?”

## Qualify

Pick no more than two:

- “What are you doing today?”
- “What works well?”
- “Where is the headache?”
- “Who else is involved?”
- “When does it need to be live or in hand?”

## Close

> “Rather than asking you to picture it, I can put your logo on a few relevant options
> and send the preview. No commitment. Worth seeing?”

## PAUSE on objections

**Pause → Acknowledge → Understand → Solve one issue → Earn one next step**

Universal reset:

> “That's fair. When you say [their words], is the main issue [A] or [B]?”

## Top objection responses

**Not interested**

> “Totally fair. Is that because merch isn't a priority, or because you already have it
> covered?”

**Busy**

> “Understood. Let me give you the 15-second reason now; if it's relevant, we'll choose
> a better time.”

**Email me**

> “Absolutely. Should I send the store/no-inventory idea or options for [event/use
> case]?”

**Already have a vendor**

> “Makes sense—I'm not asking you to replace a good partner. Is there anything they
> don't cover: stores, individual fulfillment, rushes, or creative options?”

**No budget**

> “Understood. Is there no approved budget yet, or has it already been allocated?”

For a store: “The organization doesn't pre-buy inventory. Is the real issue budget, or
not wanting another program to manage?”

**Too expensive**

> “Thanks for saying it. Is the concern unit price, total spend, or what you're getting
> for it?”

Never invent or defend pricing. Route it to Maclaine.

**Wrong person**

> “Thanks—that helps. Who owns [specific program], and what's the best way to reach
> them?”

**We already have a store**

> “Good—you've validated the model. Is it producing the participation you want, and who
> owns designs, promotion, returns, and support?”

**Our people won't buy**

> “That may be right. What happened last time—products, design, price, or promotion? I
> wouldn't recommend repeating the same setup.”

**Call later**

> “Happy to. When do you normally make the decision—not the delivery date?”

**DNC**

> “Absolutely. I'll mark that now. Sorry for the interruption.”

End the call and suppress immediately.

## Never do these

- Argue with a clear no.
- Use an unapproved customer name.
- Promise price, payout, stock, or delivery.
- Say “just checking in” or “I thought you might need swag.”
- Ask five discovery questions.
- Finish a yes without a dated next action.
