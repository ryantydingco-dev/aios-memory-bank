# Industry Expansion — a home-run offer for any industry

> CA can put any logo on anything (SanMar / S&S / alphabroder). The goal: break beyond camps/clubs into multiple industries — *without* repeating the corporate flop (0.3–1.1% reply) that came from pitching the wrong offer. The fix: **match the offer to why the industry buys.**

## The core reframe
"Sell to anybody" is a **capability**, not a targeting strategy. Different industries buy promo products for completely different reasons, and the home-run offer has to map to that reason. Get the *why* right and the same business wins in corporate that it wins in camps — with a different offer.

## Two offer archetypes (the wedge is shared)

**Archetype A — The Store Engine** *(proven — `home-run-offer.md`)*
- **Who:** identity-driven, member-based orgs — camps, clubs, gyms, schools (spirit), churches, alumni, teams.
- **Why they buy:** members *want* to wear the brand. Demand already exists.
- **The offer:** free branded web store, CA runs everything, **the org earns 10–12% rev-share, zero risk.** They make money by pointing their people at it.
- **Why it's a home run:** turns merch from a chore into a revenue stream they don't manage.

**Archetype B — "Swag, Handled" (the done-for-you procurement offer)** *(new — for corporate)*
- **Who:** corporate, tech, healthcare, professional firms, industrial, finance.
- **Why they buy:** NOT to resell to members — they buy for **themselves**: employee onboarding kits, event/conference gear, client gifts, company stores, uniforms.
- **Their real pain:** juggling multiple vendors, slow turnarounds, managing art/inventory, cheap junk, nobody owns it internally.
- **The offer:** *"Your company swag, handled. Onboarding kits, event gear, client gifts — designed, sourced, warehoused, shipped, one point of contact, 24–48hr proofs. Stop juggling vendors and chasing orders."*
- **Why it's a home run:** for a busy ops/HR/marketing person, the dream outcome is *"I never think about swag again and it always looks great."* Near-zero effort, one vendor, fast. This is the offer corporate was *never* pitched — the store offer doesn't fit them, this does.

**The shared wedge (universal across every industry):** the **AI mockup first touch** — send them their logo on the *right products for their job* (a camp store hoodie; a tech-company onboarding kit; a law firm's client-gift set) *before* they reply. Show, don't pitch. This is the door-opener in every industry.

## The repeatable method — build a home-run offer for ANY industry
For a new target industry, answer these six. The answers write the offer.

1. **The Job** — why does this industry buy promo products? (member identity · onboarding · events/conferences · client gifts · uniforms · fundraising · spirit) → *this picks the archetype.*
2. **The Pain** — how do they get it today and what sucks about it? → *this is what the offer removes.*
3. **The Archetype** — Store Engine (they earn) or Swag Handled (they're relieved)? Some get a hybrid.
4. **The Proof** — which CA customers can we name in/near this industry? → *collapses "will this work?"* (CA already has: camps, squash, schools, **healthcare — Thermo Fisher, Trinity Health, Holyoke Medical**, **finance — Farm Credit East**, **industrial — Joseph Freedman, machine/tool shops**).
5. **The Wedge** — the mockup, rendered on *this industry's* products (not always a hoodie — an onboarding kit, a conference setup, a gift box).
6. **The CTA** — "want to see the full mockup?" / 15-minute call.

## The seasonality play (this is the key timing insight)
Camps — CA's best segment — are **bought out for the summer right now.** That's not a problem, it's an opening: **summer is the window to build and prove the corporate "Swag Handled" motion** while camps are dormant. Then by Nov/Dec, when camp outreach reopens, CA has *two* proven motions running instead of one. Don't let the off-season sit idle; use it to build the second engine.

## The data discipline (so expansion doesn't become spraying)
- Corporate/SaaS flopped at 0.3–1.1% on the **store offer** — that's evidence the *store offer* doesn't fit them, NOT that the industry is dead. The Swag Handled offer is **untested** there.
- So every new industry is a **test, not a faith bet:** right offer (matched archetype) + named proof + the mockup wedge → a small batch → measure reply → scale only what clears the bar.
- Camps/squash stay the proven core. Expansion is *additive*, funded by the core, not a pivot away from it.

## Recommended first move
**Build and test the "Swag Handled" offer on corporate/professional firms — now, during camp off-season.** Reasons: it's year-round (doesn't waste summer), CA already has nameable proof (Thermo Fisher, Trinity Health, Farm Credit), it's the biggest untapped surface, and it's the offer corporate was never actually pitched. Run it as a real test (right offer + mockup + named proof + small batch), then scale if it clears ~4–5% reply.
