# Episode 1 — "The Audit"

The first build-in-public episode: Ryan runs the operations audit on Kenny's business, on camera, while Claude feeds the questions. The episode that sets up the whole series.

> **Packaging status: LOCKED (2026-06-23).** Muse: Chuck the Contractor — *How I'm Using AI to Run My Business* (19.4K subs / 144K views / 7:1). Full title / thumbnail / intro in the **Packaging — LOCKED** section at the bottom. The shooting structure below is ready to record.

---

## Format

**Hybrid.** Ryan to camera for the scripted spine (hook, intro, reflections, CTA) + a live, semi-improvised interview with Kenny (and/or Maclaine) for the body. Claude feeds interview questions in real time from `pillars/1-operations/ops-audit-interview-kit.md`. The answers are unscripted — that's the point.

**Runtime target:** 8–14 min. **Shoot order:** film the interview first (it's the substance), then record the hook/intro/reflections once you know what you actually found.

## The structure (beat sheet)

### 1. COLD OPEN / HOOK — 0:00–0:15
Punchy, to camera. Deliver the promise in the first sentence. (Locked — use the first two lines of intro V1 in the **Packaging — LOCKED** section as the cold open.)

Provisional options:
- "My girlfriend's dad has run a $3 million business for 25 years — on word of mouth, with almost no technology. I'm rebuilding the whole thing with AI, and I'm filming all of it. Today I find out exactly where his business is bleeding time."
- "This company does $3 million a year. No website traffic, no marketing, no automation — and it still nets close to $700,000. I'm going to modernize it with AI from the inside, starting now."
- "I asked my girlfriend's dad to let me run his 25-year-old business through AI. He said yes. Episode one: the audit."

*Cut to a fast montage here if you have B-roll (product, the office, Kenny working).*

### 2. INTRO / SETUP — 0:15–1:45
Who you are, the business, the mission, and what THIS episode is. Draft (follows the intro rules — trim to taste):

> "Here's the setup. Kenny built Creative Alternatives over 25 years selling promotional products — the branded shirts, mugs, and gear companies hand out. He did it all on relationships and word of mouth: no marketing, no real systems, most of it living in his head. It works, but it runs entirely on his time. My job over the next few months is to modernize it with AI — make it more efficient, bring in new customers, give it a real presence online — and document every step. Today we start where every transformation has to: the audit. I'm going to sit Kenny down, map exactly how his business runs, find the parts eating his week, and pick the first thing to automate. If you appreciate me making this type of content, let me know by gently tapping that like button. Let's get to work."

### 3. SEGMENT A — "Meet the business" — ~1:00
Quick context so the viewer cares. A few Kenny soundbites or your VO over B-roll: what CA sells, how long, the word-of-mouth thing, the "$3M with no website" hook restated. Keep it tight — don't front-load history.

### 4. SEGMENT B — THE AUDIT (the body) — the bulk of the episode
The live interview. Claude feeds questions; you ask them. Edit to the *interesting* beats only.

**Muse-informed frame (see `../muse-teardown-chuck.md`):** the muse won on one idea — *every AI transformation starts with finding where the work and data actually live.* Chuck **told** it; we **show** it. Open the audit on that exact question and let Kenny's real business answer on camera.

The arc the viewer follows:
1. **"Where does the work actually live?"** How a job shows up (phone / referral) and where it's tracked (Kenny's head / paper / scattered tools). The concrete, on-camera version of the muse's "it's all about data" point.
2. **Walk one real order** — the messy middle. Watch Kenny copy between spreadsheets, chase proofs, re-type orders. Let the inefficiency speak.
3. **The pain question** — "What's the most annoying thing you do over and over?" The emotional center.
4. **The drop-the-ball moment** — "What slips through the cracks?" (missed reorders = money on the floor).

**Respect the 80/20 (also the operator's code):** say out loud that we're *augmenting* Kenny, not replacing him — AI takes the busywork, Kenny keeps the judgment and the relationships. This is the human-in-the-loop point that made the muse land, and it keeps Kenny on-side.

**Land Segment B on the first automation candidate** — the muse's "small wins" (auto-drafting follow-ups from email/calls, inbox/quote triage, flagging missed reorders). That candidate is the THE DECISION beat and the Episode 2 build.

*Capture reaction shots of yourself listening — you'll need them for the edit.*

### 5. SEGMENT C — "What I found" — ~1:30
Back to camera, after the interview. Your honest read: the 2–3 biggest time leaks, anything that surprised you, and the ranked list of what could be automated. Use real specifics from the interview — `[FILL IN: top time leaks once the audit is done]`.

### 6. THE DECISION — ~0:45
Pick the first automation to build. Name it, say why (hours saved × ease), and set the stakes: `[FILL IN: the chosen automation]`. This is the cliffhanger.

### 7. CTA + OUTRO + EP2 TEASE — 0:30
- Subscribe line: follow the build — "I'm doing this whole transformation in public, win or lose."
- Tease: "Next episode, I build [the first automation] — and we find out if it actually saves Kenny `[FILL IN: X]` hours a week."
- Sign-off (rotate; don't reuse next episode): "Let's get into it." / "Here we go." / "Alright, let's do this."

## Shot list / B-roll to grab while you're there
- Kenny working — at the computer, on the phone, looking at product.
- The "system" — screen recordings of the actual spreadsheets/inbox (blur customer data per filming boundaries).
- Product / samples — anything visual and branded.
- The two of you talking — wide + over-the-shoulder.
- Your reaction shots (listening, reacting to a painful answer).
- Exterior / establishing shot of the office.

## Guardrails (from the operator's code)
- Settle filming boundaries with Kenny BEFORE rolling (`interview-kit` Section 10). Blur customer names, exact numbers, supplier names if he wants.
- No manufactured drama. The real inefficiency is enough.
- Don't make Kenny look foolish — he built a $3M business. The story is "smart operator, outdated tools," not "old guy doesn't get tech."
