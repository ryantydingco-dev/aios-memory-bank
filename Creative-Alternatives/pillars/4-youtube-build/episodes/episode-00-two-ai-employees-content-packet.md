## Video idea, sharpened

I am using two AI systems to modernize my girlfriend's dad's real $3M family business: Claude handles lead generation and outbound, while Codex turns the work into content, proof, and sales assets.

## Who it is for

Business owners, operators, and AI-curious builders who know AI is powerful but do not know how to plug it into a real business without creating chaos.

## Promise

By the end, viewers will understand the simple AI operating model Ryan is using at Creative Alternatives and how to copy the split in their own business:

1. One AI for revenue execution.
2. One AI for content and documentation.
3. One human approval layer for customers, vendors, money, and public claims.
4. One feedback loop where sales work becomes content, and content becomes sales proof.

## Why click today

Most AI content is abstract. This shows AI being applied inside a real, old-school, relationship-driven family business with real revenue, real customers, and real risk.

### Cold open

I do not think the best AI opportunity is starting another AI agency.

I think it is taking all this AI stuff and pointing it at a real business that already exists.

So this is the experiment.

My girlfriend's dad has run Creative Alternatives for 27 years. It is a promotional-products business. They print logos on shirts, bags, lanyards, trophies, store merch, basically anything an organization needs branded.

It does roughly $3M a year, it has served thousands of customers, and most of the growth has come from word of mouth.

But the business still has a lot of manual work. Lead gen, follow-up, content, documentation, customer reactivation, quoting, all the stuff that quietly eats time.

So I am giving the business two AI employees.

Claude is running the go-to-market engine.

Codex is becoming the content brain.

And I am going to show you exactly how that split works.

### Setup

Here is the important part: I am not trying to make AI replace Kenny or Maclaine.

That would be the fastest way to break the business.

Kenny has 27 years of relationships, supplier knowledge, customer judgment, and taste built into his head. Maclaine knows the books, the outreach, and the day-to-day systems.

The point is not to bulldoze that. The point is to build systems around it.

So the rule is simple:

AI can draft.

AI can research.

AI can organize.

AI can create first passes.

But anything that touches a customer, a vendor, money, or something public gets approved by a human.

That is the operating principle for this whole thing.

### The simple model

The way I am thinking about this business is pretty simple.

Every small business has two big problems.

First, it needs more revenue.

Second, it needs more leverage.

Creative Alternatives already has a real business. It has customers, proof, relationships, suppliers, products, repeat orders, and a brand that people trust.

What it does not have yet is a real growth engine or a content engine.

So I split the AI work into two employees.

### AI employee 1: Claude, the GTM engine

Claude's job is revenue execution.

Right now, Claude is helping with:

Pulling leads.

Enriching those leads.

Writing cold email copy.

Putting campaigns into SmartLead.

Putting leads into the dialer.

Helping with personalization.

And supporting the cold outreach system.

The business offer we are pushing is simple: Creative Alternatives can build a custom branded online store for a camp, club, school, team, or organization.

They get branded gear for their people.

They do not have to hold inventory.

Creative Alternatives handles design, fulfillment, and shipping.

And the organization can earn a revenue share.

That is the growth wedge.

So Claude is not just "writing emails." Claude is doing the repetitive GTM work that would otherwise take hours: finding the accounts, building the lists, personalizing the angles, and getting the outbound ready.

But Maclaine and I still approve what goes out.

AI drafts. Humans approve.

### AI employee 2: Codex, the content brain

Codex's job is different.

Codex is not pulling leads.

Codex is turning the work into content, documentation, and assets that compound.

So when Claude runs a campaign, Codex helps me turn that campaign into:

A YouTube video.

Five Shorts.

LinkedIn posts.

Creative Alternatives social posts.

A sales asset we can reuse in follow-up.

And an internal note so the system gets smarter next time.

That is the part most businesses miss.

They do the work once, then it disappears.

They have a sales call, a campaign, a customer win, a product shipment, a good explanation, and then it just lives in someone's memory.

I want every useful thing we do inside this business to become an asset.

If we build an AI workflow, it becomes a video.

If we run an outbound test, it becomes a case study.

If we learn an objection, it becomes sales copy.

If we find a workflow that saves time, it becomes an SOP.

That is Codex's job: make sure the work compounds.

### The flywheel

So the flywheel looks like this.

Claude creates revenue activity.

That means campaigns, leads, emails, calls, follow-ups, replies, and tests.

Codex turns that activity into content and proof.

That means scripts, posts, Shorts, case studies, sales assets, and internal documentation.

Then the content creates trust.

The proof makes the outbound better.

The replies create more lessons.

And those lessons feed the next campaign and the next video.

That is the whole operating system.

Sales work becomes content.

Content becomes trust.

Trust makes sales easier.

And the business gets smarter every week.

### What I am recording tomorrow / this week

The reason I am starting with this video is because I want you to understand the machine before I show the individual builds.

Next, I am going to document the real work:

The operations audit with Maclaine.

The QuickBooks reactivation play.

The outbound engine.

The AI store mockup wedge.

The quoting and order workflows.

And the daily brief that tells us what needs attention in the business.

Some of those will work.

Some of them will probably be messier than I expect.

But that is why this is interesting.

This is not me pretending AI magically fixes everything.

This is me trying to put AI into a real business without breaking the trust that made the business work in the first place.

### Lesson

If you run a business, here is the takeaway:

Do not start by asking, "What AI tool should I use?"

Start by asking, "What job should this AI employee have?"

One AI might handle lead research.

One might handle customer support drafts.

One might build reports.

One might turn your work into content.

But if the job is vague, the output will be vague.

Claude has a job.

Codex has a job.

I have a job.

And Kenny and Maclaine still own the judgment.

That is the model I am betting on.

No theory. No fake numbers. No pretending the messy parts are not messy.

Just real workflows, real campaigns, real lessons, and hopefully real revenue.
