# Channel Trailer / First Post (~75–90 sec)

The flag-planter. Records solo, today. Establishes the story so Episode 1 (the audit) lands on a primed channel. Also the source for your first 3–5 shorts.

**Title idea:** "I'm Rebuilding My Girlfriend's Dad's $3M Business With AI"
**Format:** straight to camera, one take is fine, energy up.

---

## Script

*(to camera)*

"My girlfriend's dad has run a $3 million business for 25 years — selling promotional products, the branded shirts and mugs companies hand out. And he's done all of it on word of mouth. No website that really sells. No marketing. No systems. Most of the business lives in his head.

I'm Ryan. I've spent the last few years learning how to use AI to run and grow businesses. So I made him a deal: let me come in and rebuild this thing with AI — make it more efficient, bring in a lot more customers, and put the whole company online.

He said yes. And I'm filming all of it.

Over the next year you'll watch a real, profitable, old-school business get pulled into the AI era — the wins, the awkward conversations, the automations that save hours, and the systems that bring in actual revenue. No theory. A real company, real money, on the line.

So if you run a business, work in one, or you just want to see what AI can actually do when you point it at the real world — follow along. Episode one drops this week: I sit down and find out exactly where this business is bleeding time and money.

Let's get to work."

---

## Delivery notes
- Numbers stay spoken as numerals ("$3 million," "25 years," "Episode one").
- Cut the first line tight — "$3 million business for 25 years" is the hook; lead with it.
- Look right down the lens for the "follow along" line.
- Grab 2–3 alt takes of the first sentence for shorts.

## Shorts to cut from this (Day 4)
1. "$3M business, no website, no systems" cold-open → the deal he made
2. "He said yes. And I'm filming all of it." (the premise, 15s)
3. The "what you'll watch this year" rundown (20s)
