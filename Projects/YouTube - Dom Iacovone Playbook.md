## Episode formula (every episode, no exceptions)

1. **Cold open** — pulled from the week's most dramatic beat (buyer reply, Kenny pushback, campaign flop). Tease it, don't resolve it.
2. **Meeting recaps to camera** — never film clients/Kenny convos live; walk out and give a 60-sec recap. Fast pacing, signals access, no privacy issues.
3. **One tension arc threaded in 3–4 beats** — e.g. "will the tradeshow campaign book a meeting by Friday?" Check in on it throughout the episode (his stock-trade subplot pattern).
4. **Teach at the shelf** — explain the mockup wedge AT the embroidery machine holding the hat; explain reactivation math walking the warehouse. Never desk talking-head for the teaching segment.
5. **Real numbers out loud** — SmartLead reply rates, reactivation counts, quotes won/lost, dollar figures. Sanitize client names, not numbers. Specificity IS the authority.
6. **Confess mistakes** — his best hooks are admitted failures ("this mistake cost us a lot of money"). Failed campaigns are episode gold, not embarrassments.
7. **Lifestyle B-plot as pacing** — Hyrox training, gym shifts, life beats braided between operator substance.
8. **Payoff the cold open at the end.**

Film the real work as it happens (phone on tripod during mockup builds / campaign launches). Marginal cost = the edit, not the footage. Off-camera "interviewer" questions (videographer role) can be simulated by self-prompted Q&A beats.

## Shorts machine

- Each long-form contains 5–10 clippable moments: Kenny one-liners, reply notifications, mockup reveals, shop-floor moments.
- Target: **3–5 Shorts/week** clipped from the weekly episode (his 2/day is a team; this is the solo-viable floor).
- Use Joey AI video skills / clipper tooling already in the vault.
- His Shorts outliers are personality + micro-moments (18–30s), not mini-lessons.

## Operating principles to steal for CA itself (not just content)

- **KPI translation by altitude**: Kenny/Maclaine get unit scoreboards (emails sent, replies, mockups delivered, reorders) — "remove the money talk after C-suite." Gamify + tie recognition to units, not revenue they don't control.
- **"Do less, but do better"**: a few big motions blown out (tradeshow signal, reactivation) > five parallel experiments.
- **2-second rule**: any task under ~2 min (intro email, reply, follow-up) done on the spot, never queued.
- **Single-threaded ownership**: one owner per function; nobody routes around them.
- **Hero-SKU thinking**: lead outbound with ONE hero offer per channel, long-tail lives elsewhere.
- **Channel-ladder logic** (his specialty→FDM→club): different offer/packaging per channel so channels don't cannibalize — maps to cold vs. warm/reactivation vs. event-planner motions.
