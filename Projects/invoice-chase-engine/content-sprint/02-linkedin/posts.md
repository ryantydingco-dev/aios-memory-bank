## Post 1 - QuickBooks and $650K

A 25+ year business had QuickBooks and still had $650K sitting in open invoices.

That sounds like "they need better accounting software."

They did not.

Kenny at Creative Alternatives already had QuickBooks.

Invoices were going out.

The leak was after the invoice.

He was still personally emailing clients one by one to chase payment, keep track of who replied, remember who promised to pay, and follow up when something slipped.

That is not really an accounting problem.

It is an operational follow-up problem wearing an accounting hat.

I connected QuickBooks to a Claude-powered follow-up workflow and helped create reminder sequences so invoice follow-up became more consistent.

Over 90 days, that helped recover $650K in open invoices.

Now I am testing whether that one-off workflow can become a repeatable AI service:

A $500 AR Leak Audit for businesses that use QuickBooks but still chase invoices manually.

Very unsexy.

Possibly useful.

My favorite category.

## Post 2 - The follow-up recovered the cash

QuickBooks tracked the invoices.

The follow-up system helped recover the cash.

That distinction matters.

In the Creative Alternatives workflow, the issue was not that invoices were disappearing into the void.

The issue was that the follow-up lived in Kenny's head and inbox.

Invoice sent.

Manual reminder.

Client replies.

"We will pay next week."

Someone has to remember that.

Someone has to check if it happened.

Someone has to know when to escalate, when to be polite, when to ask about a dispute, and when to send the owner a clean weekly report.

That is where invoices leak.

I helped connect QuickBooks to a Claude-powered follow-up system and created sequences that made the chase more consistent.

That workflow helped recover $650K in open invoices over 90 days.

The lesson for me:

AI services do not need to sound futuristic.

Sometimes they just need to make the boring thing happen on Tuesday.

## Post 3 - Boring AI businesses

I am becoming increasingly suspicious that the best AI businesses are boring.

Not "AI avatar that changes the future of everything."

More like:

"You already use QuickBooks, but your invoice follow-up still lives in your inbox."

That is the kind of problem I am testing right now.

I helped Kenny at Creative Alternatives recover $650K in open invoices over 90 days by connecting QuickBooks to a Claude-powered follow-up workflow and creating reminder sequences.

Now I am trying to package the first repeatable piece:

An AR Leak Audit.

The audit looks at 15 places invoices leak after they are sent:

- wrong billing contact
- no receipt confirmation
- no pre-due reminder
- no 3/7/14 day overdue sequence
- no promise-to-pay tracking
- no dispute category
- no weekly owner report

Nothing glamorous.

No guru pose required.

Just a workflow that already costs real businesses time, attention, and cash flow when it is inconsistent.

The experiment:

Can this become a repeatable AI service business?
