# Dealthreads Call Scripts — v3, 2026-07-26

Two tracks: HOT (hiring-signal lists, the Notes field has the exact role) and BENCH (owner office lines). One CTA philosophy: sell the 10-account niche proof, not a generic meeting. The sample is free, specific, and earns a 30-minute Pipeline Math session.

---

## TRACK 1 — HOT (they are hiring a BD/sales rep right now)

**Opener (permission + signal):**
"{First}? Ryan Tydingco. I'll be straight with you, this is a cold call, but it's about the {role from Notes} you're hiring for. Got 30 seconds or should I get lost?"

**The wedge (pick the angle by their tone):**

*Angle A, arm the hire:*
"Here's why I called. That seat's going to cost you 60, 70 grand plus six months of ramp, and most of that ramp is them building a list and getting ignored. I build the outbound system for staffing firms, the list, the email engine, the call queue, so whoever you hire walks in day one with a pipeline instead of a blank CRM."

*Angle B, question the seat:*
"Most owners hire that role to get more client conversations. The rep still needs a market, a list, a reason to call, and a follow-up system. I install that client-pipeline operating system for staffing firms. Worth two minutes?"

**Discovery (two questions max, then CTA):**
- "What's the plan for that rep's first 90 days, who builds their list?"
- "What desk are they feeding, what niche do you place in?"

**CTA (the sample list):**
"Do this with me. Give me the niche you recruit in. I'll show you 10 companies with a current reason to need talent and the exact buyer I'd contact. Free. If the rows are right, we spend 20 minutes on the pipeline math. What niche should I use?"

## TRACK 2 — BENCH (owner, cold, office line)

**Gatekeeper:**
"Hey, is {First} around? It's Ryan." (Nothing else. If asked what about: "It's about business development for {Company}, he'll know the context." — the email in-sequence IS the context.)

**Opener:**
"{First}? Ryan Tydingco. Cold call, I'll be quick. I run outbound for staffing firms, and {Company} came up when I was building a list of {niche} agencies. You the right person for how new client conversations happen there, or is that someone else?"

**If they're in your email sequence (most are), cross-reference it:**
"You might've seen an email from me this week about a sample list of hiring managers in {niche}. This is that guy."

**The pitch (one breath):**
"Owners tell me the same thing: the recruiting desk is full, so client development becomes whoever has spare time—which is nobody. I install the operating system that tells the team who to call, why now, and what to say, then makes sure every reply and follow-up gets handled. I'm not going to pitch you hard on a cold call. I'd rather show you."

**CTA (same lead magnet):**
"Give me the niche you recruit in. I'll show you 10 companies with a current reason to need talent and the exact buyer I'd contact. If the rows are right, we spend 20 minutes on the pipeline math. What niche should I use?"

## OBJECTIONS (both tracks)

**"We already do outbound / we have a BD person."**
"Perfect, then you know the hard part is the list and the timing. Let me send the sample anyway, if it beats what your person's working from, we talk. If not, free list."

**"Just send me an email."**
"Will do. So it's worth opening: what niche do you place in, and who's the ideal client?" (Micro-commitment. Log the answer in the disposition notes.)

**"How much is it?"**
"The Pipeline Proof is $2,000. We install the system in five business days and run it for 14 calendar days. If you continue, every dollar becomes part of the first $4,500 month—no extra setup fee. But I haven't earned that recommendation yet. Let me show you the 10 accounts first."

**"Why are we making the calls at $4,500?"**
"Because your team already understands talent and live conversations. We remove the research, prioritization, scripting, follow-up, and system work so their 30-minute block is spent talking to the right accounts. If nobody can work the queue, we use the Proof to measure the workload and scope managed calling separately."

**"Why not buy an AI SDR?"**
"You should if you only need software and already have somebody to choose the market, protect deliverability, coordinate calls, handle replies, enforce follow-up, and learn from pipeline. Dealthreads is the managed operating system and accountability layer, not another sending tool."

**"We grow off referrals."**
"Every good firm does, that's the sign you deliver. Referrals just don't scale on your schedule. The system's for the quarter when the referral well is quiet. Sample list anyway?"

**"Not interested."**
One probe: "Fair. Because outbound's not a priority, or because you've been burned by an agency before?" Then exit clean: "Respect it. I'll leave you alone. If a client-side slowdown ever hits, you know a guy."

**"Aren't you selling what you're doing to me right now?"**
"Caught me. This call is the product demo. Imagine it running for you all day."

(Both versions point at the email already in their inbox from the sequence. The voicemail's job is to make the email get opened, not to get a callback.)

## DISPOSITION RULES (feeds the call loop)

- Mark dispositions in Salesfinity on EVERY call, the engine reads them nightly.
- Positive or callback → log the niche they gave you in the notes verbatim. That's the sample-list order form.
- Wrong number → mark it, the engine suppresses it forever.
- A "send me the email" with a niche answer counts as a WIN, package it same day.

## THE OS LINE (both tracks — use when they ask "so what do you actually do?")

"Most outbound agencies sell you effort: a person, some tools, and a list. I install the Staffing Client Pipeline OS. Every morning your team gets the exact companies to call, why they may need talent now, and the opener to use. Email, calls, LinkedIn, replies, follow-ups, calendar activity, and CRM attribution run as one account thread. You own the machine; I operate the brain and execution."

If they push on "proprietary": "Built it myself, runs nowhere else. The tools underneath are rentable; the system that runs them isn't."

## HARD RULES
- Never promise meetings, placements, opportunities, or revenue. The promise is the installed system, agreed activity, response SLA, ownership, and receipts.
- Never describe the call as a generic demo. The next step is the 30-minute Pipeline Math session.
- Never present multiple plans. Recommend the $4,500 client-operated path when the buyer has calling capacity. If they do not, identify managed calling as a separately scoped option after the Proof.
- The Calendar Group: hang up if one slips through.
- 25 dials before you judge the script. Then tell me what's breaking and I'll rewrite the weak line.
