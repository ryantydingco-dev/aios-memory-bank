# Video 1 — Intro Video (working outline)
*Caleb's playbook: NOT a brag reel. Why you're making content now + 3–5 pivotal moments, each paired with ONE lesson FOR the audience. Close with what to expect + seed the contrarian belief. Bullet points on camera, never scripted lines. Target 15–25 min. Make it for ONE person (a specific skeptical SMB owner — picture Kenny two years ago). Say "you," never "you guys."*

## Packaging candidates (shop the wrapping-paper library; pick before filming)
- "Door-to-door vacuums to AI: why I'm betting everything on a 25-year-old family business"
- "I sell for my girlfriend's dad. Here's why that's the best job in AI."
- "15 years of cold outreach led me to a $3.2M promo business"
- Thumbnail: you + the CA shop floor or a stack of branded merch; real environment, no studio.

## Four C's open (first 60 seconds — get to a real learning fast, no montage)
- **Call-out:** "If you run a small business and every AI pitch you've heard smells like BS — this channel is for you."
- **Credibility (contextual):** "I've done cold outreach for 15 years — started selling vacuums door-to-door — and right now I'm using AI to rebuild a real 25-year-old, $3.2M business. Not a demo. A business with trucks and payroll."
- **Compass:** "I'll tell you the 4 moments that taught me how sales actually works, the lesson from each you can use this week, and what I'm going to document here every month."
- **Core learning up front:** "Here's the first one free: AI doesn't fix a sales problem — reps fix a sales problem. AI multiplies whoever's holding it." (brand statement seeded in minute one)

## Body — 4 pivotal moments (context → principle → story → tactic → example)
1. **Kirby doors.** Story: the hardest no's that exist. Lesson FOR THEM: rejection math — every funnel has a rate; find yours and plan backwards from it. Tactic: count your real conversion rate before you scale anything.
2. **SDR → senior SDR → growth AM in one year.** Story: how, concretely (volume + iteration, not talent). Lesson: speed of iteration beats intensity of effort. Tactic: change ONE variable per week in your outreach and log it.
3. **The pivot: going all-in on my girlfriend's dad's business.** Story: leaving the AI-consulting shiny objects to fix one real company (name the failed detours honestly — that's lore, not weakness). Lesson: one real business beats ten demos. Tactic: pick your ONE thing and give it 12 months.
4. **The stakes: I lose my day job in August.** Story: the honest pressure, and why the answer isn't a new hustle — it's making the math close on the thing that works. Lesson: money pressure makes you pivot exactly when you shouldn't. Tactic: write your tripwires BEFORE the pressure hits.

## Close
- What to expect: one deep video a month documenting the CA transformation — real dashboards, real reply rates, real failures. "I'll show you the whole playbook. Most of you should steal it and run it yourselves."
- Restate the brand statement. Introduce Kenny as a character ("you'll meet him — 25 years in this business, zero patience for hype. He's the audience I have to convince every day.").
- CTA (no selling): "Comment the one revenue problem you'd want me to run through the honest-math process."

## Production notes (Caleb's rules)
- 10+ hours pre-production minimum; his only dead shoot of year one was the zero-prep one.
- Film in a REAL environment (CA shop floor > any studio). Environments you love = sustainable.
- Bullets, not script; re-take any line addressed to a group instead of one person.
- Never past V3 on the edit. Notes only on: structure, hook, brand positioning. No motion-graphics rabbit holes — "the greatest retention hack in educational content is the audience learning."
- Cut the intro in the edit if a later line is stronger cold open (his course famously opens mid-thought).
- After the shoot: log 1–3 things to fix next time (iteration ritual).
