# X → YouTube Pipeline: "They post the claim. I run the test."
*Competitive intelligence system: mine the X build-in-public scene for signals, validate inside CA/Dealthreads, publish the receipts on YouTube. Caleb-compliant: we steal DEMAND and WRAPPING PAPER, never transcripts. "Stop being a reporter sharing the news; be the person making the news."*

## The core positioning move
The X AI scene is a claim machine: "this agent booked 40 meetings," "this workflow replaced my SDR," "$0 to $20k with this stack." Almost none of it is verified inside a real business. You own the verification layer. That turns their content into your raw material AND feeds your contrarian belief (receipts over demos) — every video is your brand statement in action.

## What we extract from X (the three legal takes)
1. **Demand signals** — which claims/tools/workflows are getting massive engagement = what your audience is curious (and skeptical) about. Their virality is your topic validation.
2. **Wrapping paper** — hooks, thread structures, screenshot formats that outperform. Goes straight into 03-wrapping-paper-library.md.
3. **Test candidates** — specific buildable claims we can actually run inside CA/Dealthreads and measure.

**Never extracted:** their teaching, their frameworks, their transcripts. (Caleb's 3-month cold-turkey rule applies — everything published comes from OUR observed results.)

## Weekly loop (fits inside the existing wrapping-paper ritual, ~60-90 min total)
1. **Scout (30 min, weekly):** scroll a curated X list of AI-builder accounts + outlier posts. Save only outliers (posts clearly above the author's normal engagement). Log each in the Claim Ledger below: claim, author, engagement, buildability, skepticism level.
2. **Select (10 min, monthly):** pick 1 claim per month that (a) your ICP would care about, (b) maps to a painful-problem row in 02-painful-problems.md, (c) is testable in CA/Dealthreads within 2-4 weeks without derailing revenue work.
3. **Test:** run it for real inside the business. Define the win metric BEFORE the test (honest-math style: what rate would make this claim true?). Log everything — screenshots, dashboards, costs, hours.
4. **Publish:** the monthly YouTube pillar = the test + verdict + your system. Waterfall the golden moments back to LinkedIn/X — and on X, the results post quote-tags the original claim's topic (never attacking the person; Caleb's contrarian-vs-callout rule is absolute: attack the CONCEPT, never the creator).

## The repeatable YouTube format this creates (a named show — worldbuilding)
Working title: **"The Receipts Test"** (or "Honest Math vs. the Internet").
Episode structure (Four C's + body template):
- Call-out: "X says [claim]. I run a real $3.2M business. Let's find out."
- Credibility: contextual line for THIS test (e.g., our observed cold-email rates).
- Compass: the claim → the setup → the data → the verdict → what you should actually do.
- Core learning in the first 60s: the single most surprising number from the test.
- Body: how we built it (your system visible) → what the data said → verdict: CONFIRMED / BUSTED / "true, but only if..." → the tactic viewers can apply this week.
- Verdicts must be fair, specific, and generous — the goal is trusted referee, not rage-baiter. When something works, say so loudly; confirmations build as much trust as busts.

## Guardrails
- **Never name/dunk on a specific person.** Test the claim category. If citing a viral post, do it respectfully or anonymize.
- **One test/month max.** The tests must ride on work CA needs anyway (dual-purpose or don't do it) — revenue work stays first.
- **No fabricated urgency in verdicts, no rage-bait thumbnails.** Trust gate on every episode: does this increase or decrease trust?
- **The 75/20/5 ratio still governs:** this show is the 20% niche-wide lane at most; the 75% deep lane stays the CA transformation documentation. If the show outperforms, great — but the brand is the transformation, not the reactions.
- Log every episode in the multiplier sheet with lever = "receipts-over-demos."
