# The Content Production System — built 2026-08-04 (layoff day)

The strategy was settled weeks ago: anti-hype, receipts on screen, document the
build instead of performing expertise. See `content-os.md` and
`creator-emulation-playbook.md` for the constitution and house title style.

This document is the **production layer** — the thing that makes recording happen
on a day when you don't feel like it. The bottleneck was never ideas. It was
friction between deciding to record and pressing the button.

---

## The rule that makes it survive the runway

**One hour a day, and it never touches the asks.** Content is the 1:45-2:45 block.
If a call runs long, content gets cut that day, not the call. A channel built on
skipped sales calls is a channel with no company behind it.

---

## Three formats. Never invent a fourth.

**1. THE SPRINT LOG (2x/week, 5-8 min, one take)**
The spine of the channel. Scoreboard on screen, then what happened, then what you
learned. Recorded at the desk, no setup beyond hitting record.
> Structure: the numbers → the one thing that happened → the one thing it taught
> → what's next. Four beats, no script.

**2. THE TEARDOWN (1x/week, 8-12 min, screen share)**
Open a real piece of the system and explain it while it runs. The signal table,
the reply router, the scoreboard, the LinkedIn queue. You are not teaching how to
build it — you are showing what it did this week.

**3. THE POST-MORTEM (whenever something fails, 4-6 min)**
The most valuable format you own and the one nobody else in this niche can make,
because everyone else is selling the dream. When something breaks, film it.

No research required. Every one of these already happened.

**When the list runs low, the changelog refills it.** Every week the machine does
something new or breaks something. That is the episode.

## The weekly rhythm

| Day | Content block |
|---|---|
| Mon | Sprint Log #1 (the week's numbers + the plan) |
| Tue | LinkedIn post from Monday's video |
| Wed | Teardown (screen share, one system) |
| Thu | LinkedIn post + cut 2 shorts from the week's footage |
| Fri | **Recording day.** Batch anything remaining. Sprint Log #2. |

**Batch on Friday.** Camera stays set up. Record two or three in a row while the
lighting and the mic are already right. Recording sessions that require setup do
not happen; sessions where the camera is already on do.

## The repurposing chain (one recording, five assets)

1. YouTube video (the original)
2. LinkedIn text post, the same story written out, posted the next morning
3. 2 shorts / reels cut from the strongest 45 seconds
4. The numbers become a standalone LinkedIn graphic or table
5. The transcript becomes the following week's newsletter section, if you start one

Do not film separately for each platform. Film once, cut five ways.

## Distribution priority for THIS business
1. **LinkedIn** — your buyers are literally on it, and you are already inviting
   200 staffing owners a month who will see your posts after they accept.
2. **YouTube** — the credibility asset. Where the tour video lives forever and
   where prospects land when they Google you.
3. Shorts/TikTok/IG — spillover only. Never the priority, never the reason to film.

## The setup (kill the friction permanently)

Sony ZV-E10 II, Sigma 16mm, Rode wireless. **Leave it assembled on the desk,
pointed at your chair, mic charged, SD card empty.** The single highest-leverage
change you can make to your content output is never having to set up.

Recording checklist, 30 seconds:
- Camera on, red light confirmed
- Mic on, receiver showing signal
- Scoreboard or the artifact open on screen
- One sentence of what this video is about, out loud, then go

## The metric for 90 days
**Videos published.** Not views, not subscribers, not watch time. Those measure
the algorithm's opinion of a channel that does not exist yet. Publish 25 videos
in 90 days and the rest is arithmetic you cannot rush.

Two tripwires:
- **If a week passes with zero recordings**, the block moved or the setup got
  packed away. Fix the physical thing, not the motivation.
- **If content ever displaces an ask**, content loses that day. Every time.

## Why this compounds (the honest version)

Content does not pay in 60 days. What it does:
- Raises close rates immediately, because buyers Google you before every call
- Makes podcast hosts say yes, because they can see you talk
- Makes the warm network intro easy, because there's something to point at
- At month 6-12, produces inbound that costs nothing per lead
- At year 2+, becomes the asset that makes everything else cheaper

The compounding is real, and it is entirely conditional on consistency. Twenty-five
mediocre videos published beats five great ones and eight months of silence, every
single time. The channel is not a bet on talent. It is a bet on showing up.
