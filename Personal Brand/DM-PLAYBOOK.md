# LinkedIn Conversation and DM Playbook

## Principle

The first message should feel like meeting someone for the first time. Its only job is to make a real conversation possible.

No pitch in the first message. Do not mention services, ask for a call, drop a calendar link, offer a mockup, or use a fake-curious question whose answer obviously leads to a pitch.

## Relationship stages

### Current connections workflow

An existing connection is not automatically warm. Before drafting, confirm the current role, check CA customer history, add Ryan's real relationship context, and identify one current signal. Never generate a pitch from a title match.

If Ryan does not remember how they connected, say that honestly instead of inventing familiarity:

> Hey [Name], realized we have been connected for a while but I do not think we have ever actually talked.
>
> [Ryan's real observation or question].

Do not add CA to the first message unless it naturally answers something they asked.

### 0. Context

Know why this person is relevant: their post, comment, shared experience, event, company change, or introduction. If there is no real context, do not invent one.

### 1. First hello

Use this structure:

> [Specific context or reason they caught Ryan's attention].
>
> [Brief human reaction or shared experience].
>
> [One easy, genuine question].

Examples:

Warm post engagement:

> Appreciate you jumping into the QuickBooks post. I am still figuring out which parts of these builds are useful to other operators versus just interesting to me.
>
> What kind of business are you working on?

Operator connection:

> Your point about updating an old business without breaking what already works hit home.
>
> I am in the middle of that exact thing at a 27-year shop. What system has been the hardest for you to modernize?

CA-adjacent context:

> Saw the post about your team event. Looked like a good one.
>
> I spend half my week around event and merch projects, so I always notice the details. What did your team enjoy most?

Cold connection request with thin context:

> I have been following more owners who are modernizing established businesses from the inside. Your work at [company] caught my eye. Would be glad to connect.

### 2. Conversation

Respond to what they actually said. Ask one follow-up at a time. Share a relevant sentence about Ryan's experience, but do not turn every answer back toward Ryan.

### 3. Useful exchange

When relevant, offer a small useful item without creating an obligation: a build note, checklist, prompt, example, vendor idea, or introduction. Ask permission before sending links or long material.

> I built something similar for our shop. Happy to send the outline if it would be useful. No pitch attached.

### 4. Permission to explore

Only after a two-way conversation reveals a real problem or buying signal:

Automation:

> That sounds close to the workflow I just built for us. Do you want to compare how yours works now with what we changed?

CA:

> If the merch side is still open, I am happy to share a few directions based on the event. Want me to take a look?

### 5. Call or offer

Only when they ask for help or explicitly accept the exploration. State the next step plainly. Do not manufacture urgency.

## Inbound handling

"Can you build this for us?"

> Potentially, yes. Before I pretend I know the answer, what is the workflow doing today and where does it keep breaking?

"What does Creative Alternatives do?"

> We handle branded merchandise and promotional products, especially when a team wants help turning a rough idea into something people will actually use. What are you planning?

"How much does it cost?"

Answer directly if the scope is clear. If it is not:

> It depends on the workflow and how much needs to connect. If you send me the current process in a few bullets, I can tell you whether it is a small build or something bigger.

## Follow-ups

- Do not send "just bumping this."
- Follow up only with new context, a promised item, or a natural continuation.
- One unanswered follow-up is enough for an ordinary connection. Leave the door open after that.

Example:

> Your comment came back to me when our A/R workflow broke again this morning. We fixed the login issue by removing the login entirely. Thought you might appreciate that ending.

## Assistant output format

When Ryan asks for DM help, return:

1. `Send now:` one copy-ready message.
2. `Why:` one sentence naming the context and relationship stage.
3. `If they reply:` the next goal, not a prewritten pitch tree.

Never claim a message was sent. Ryan sends, or explicitly confirms an individual browser action at the moment it is ready.
