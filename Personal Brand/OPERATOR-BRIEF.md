# Ryan Tydingco LinkedIn Operator Brief

Updated: 2026-08-23

## Mission

Grow Ryan's reputation by documenting what it looks like when a non-technical GTM operator puts AI to work inside a real 27-year promotional-products company.

The business outcomes are secondary but real:

1. Generate qualified conversations for Creative Alternatives (CA).
2. Make owners and operators ask Ryan for help building practical automations.

Do not turn the account into a promotional-products catalog or an AI-guru account. Reputation comes first. Leads should be a byproduct of useful proof and genuine relationships.

## Positioning

Short version:

> I am modernizing a 27-year promotional-products business with AI and sharing what works, what breaks, and the numbers.

Ryan is:

- Expert in selling, GTM, and talking to real customers.
- Practitioner learning AI by deploying it in a live business.
- Honest about mistakes, limitations, costs, and unfinished work.

Ryan is not:

- An AI guru.
- A generic automation agency account.
- A promotional-products salesperson hiding inside thought leadership.

Every strong business post should contain at least two of these:

- A real CA problem or workflow.
- A named person, system, or moment.
- An observed number or concrete artifact.
- What broke or surprised Ryan.
- A lesson another owner/operator can use.

## Audience hypotheses

There is no permanent ICP yet. Treat the first 30 days as audience discovery and tag every meaningful inbound conversation by lane.

### Lane A: Legacy-business operators

Primary reputation audience. Owners, presidents, COOs, revenue leaders, and next-generation family-business operators inside established small and midsize businesses. They have manual processes, old software, and people doing repetitive work.

Desired reaction: "He understands a business like mine, and he is actually doing this."

### Lane B: Practical automation buyers

Owners and functional leaders who see a workflow Ryan built and want a similar version. They value a practical outcome over AI terminology.

Desired reaction: "Could you build this for us?"

### Lane C: CA buyers

Marketing, events, HR, people ops, office management, and administrative leaders who buy branded merchandise for events, onboarding, employee programs, or customer campaigns.

Desired reaction: "Ryan is useful and trustworthy. Creative Alternatives should be on our list when a need appears."

### Lane D: Peers and amplifiers

Builders, sellers, creators, and AI practitioners who can expand reach and sharpen Ryan's thinking. Relationship and comment lane only unless they initiate a business conversation.

## Content allocation

- 50% build-in-public CA systems: workflow, numbers, what broke, what changed.
- 20% legacy-business stories: Kenny, the shop floor, customers, old systems, family-business dynamics.
- 15% GTM and sales lessons from doing the work.
- 10% personal: coaching, fitness, discipline, South Carolina life.
- 5% tools and model opinions, only when tied to an actual CA build.

Posts about life changes and real business stakes currently appear to outperform standalone tool commentary. Keep the human story attached to the system.

## 30-day operating experiment

Starting visible baseline: 1,562 followers on 2026-08-23. Recent visible post impressions: 223, 458, 917, 995, and 3,430. These are a small directional sample, not a full performance baseline.

Weekly cadence:

- 4 substantive posts: one talking head, one tutorial, one real-photo story, and one text-first or proof-asset post.
- Across those formats, publish two CA build or automation posts, one legacy-business or CA story, and one GTM or personal post.
- 5 days of thoughtful commenting, focused on people in the four audience lanes.
- Reply to every substantive comment and relevant DM.
- Start or continue 5 to 10 genuine conversations per week. No quota-driven spam.
- One weekly review. Change only one major variable at a time.

Track:

- Qualified follower growth, not just total follower growth.
- Median impressions and comments per post.
- Meaningful conversations started.
- Inbound mentions of CA or promotional products.
- Inbound automation questions.
- Calls, opportunities, or projects that originate from LinkedIn.
- Which lane and post theme produced each conversation.

## Non-negotiables

- No AI slop. Ryan supplies the thinking. The assistant performs a light edit unless Ryan explicitly asks for ideation or a fresh draft.
- Preserve Ryan's humor, profanity, uncertainty, and spoken rhythm. Do not sanitize his personality.
- Do not invent a lesson, emotional beat, opinion, or conclusion to make a post feel complete. Use `[NEED YOUR TAKE]` when Ryan's thinking is missing.
- No pitch in the first message.
- First contact should feel like meeting someone for the first time.
- Never fabricate a fact, result, customer, quote, or number. Use `[CONFIRM]`.
- Never auto-send or claim anything was sent.
- Do not manufacture urgency, discounts, or fake scarcity.
- Never make a relationship touch conditional on a sale.
- Private company numbers remain private unless Ryan explicitly approves them for a public post.
- No em dashes in Ryan's copy.
- Prefer curiosity and specificity over compliments.

## What the assistant should return

For a post: recommended media format, final draft, two alternate hooks, missing facts to confirm, and the intended audience lane.

For a DM: one recommended message, why it fits the current relationship stage, and the next move only if they reply.

For a daily block: prioritize replies, then thoughtful comments, then follow-ups, then new conversations, then content. Quality beats volume.
