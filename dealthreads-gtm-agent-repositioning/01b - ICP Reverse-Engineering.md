## The reframe that matters
Two different questions, usually collapsed into one:
- **Lens A — Who benefits MOST?** (where the engine delivers the most value → who *should* buy)
- **Lens B — Who's the LOWEST-HANGING FRUIT?** (easiest first sale for *Ryan specifically, today, with zero case studies*)

They are **not the same people.** The biggest beneficiary is often a slow, skeptical sale. The easy first sale is often a smaller-value client. **For pilot #1, Lens B wins** — because the whole job of the first 1-2 pilots is to manufacture proof, fast. Then proof unlocks the Lens-A whales.

---

## First principles — what has to be TRUE

**To BENEFIT (Lens A), they need ALL of:**
1. **High deal value** — one closed deal pays for 6-12 months of retainer (so a few conversations = ROI; cold reply rates are 1-5%, so low volume must still pay).
2. **Pipeline = referrals only** — no system today, so adding one is a step-change, not a marginal gain.
3. **Credible but bad at own outreach** — "cobbler's children": real proof/case studies, zero time to sell themselves.
4. **Buyers live on LinkedIn** — the wedge channel is where their market actually is.
5. **No RevOps/SDR** — nobody owns this internally; can't justify the $10-12k/mo hire.

**To be LOW-HANGING FRUIT (Lens B), they also need:**
6. **Ryan has trust or peer-credibility** with them (warm intro, shared community, or they've watched him build).
7. **Acute, recent, visible pain** — just lost a client, just posted "pipeline's slow," just tried/failed to hire an SDR. Pain *now*, not someday.
8. **No education tax** — they already believe AI-run outbound can work, so the call is about fit, not convincing.
9. **Fast, solo decision** — founder buys alone, no committee.

> The beachhead = the segment scoring high on BOTH. Everything else is "later."

## The two honest tensions (this is the real work)

**Tension 1 — Your warmest network is real-estate-flavored, but the offer is now GTM-general.**
Your highest-trust contacts (the gym circle: investors, agents, brokerage owners) are the *easiest* first sale by trust — but you just stripped real estate from the brand. Don't ignore them: a brokerage owner who needs **agent-recruiting pipeline** is still a GTM-pipeline problem the engine solves (recruiting = outbound to candidates/agents). You can serve a warm real-estate contact *as a GTM client* without the brand being real-estate. **But** don't let pilot #1 quietly drag you back into the real-estate identity. Use warm RE contacts for *proof and cash*, keep the *brand and case-study language* GTM-general.

**Tension 2 — AI-agency peers (candidate #1) are the lowest-hanging fruit AND the biggest adjacency trap.**
They believe in AI-run GTM (no education tax) and you have peer cred — gold. But split them:
- ✅ **GOOD fit:** AI-agency/service founders who sell a *delivery* service (automation builds, AI implementation, content, ops) and are too busy delivering to run their own outbound. They want it DONE.
- ❌ **BAD fit:** anyone who sells *outbound / lead-gen / appointment-setting itself* — they'll say "I'll just build this." They're competitors, not buyers.
The tell: **what do they sell?** If it's lead-gen, skip. If it's anything else, they're prime.

### ⚠️ THE TRAP that would sink this (read before sending anything)
The AAA community is full of people building **AI-automation / GTM / lead-gen agencies** — i.e., your **competitors or the "I'll just build it myself" crowd.** If you pitch them, you lose. The qualifying split:

**The one question that sorts them:** *"What do you actually sell, and do you have paying clients?"* Non-lead-gen + has clients = prime. Everything else = pass.
