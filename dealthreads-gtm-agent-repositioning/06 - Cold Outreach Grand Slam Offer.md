## The one move everything hangs on: GIVE pipeline, don't ask for a meeting

A cold MP with no reason to trust you will not "book a 30-min call." But they will say yes to **free pipeline** — because it's their oxygen and it costs them nothing.

So the cold offer is not "let me show you what we do." It's:

> **"I'll find you [N] companies showing executive-hiring signals in your niche this week — the decision-maker, why-now, and an opener you could use to win the mandate. Free. No call. Want me to send it?"**

This is the engine (`dealthreads-gtm-experiment-engine`) **pointed at the recruiter's client side** — the same signal-scored research cards Ryan runs on Oloxa, except the "accounts" are companies that need to hire a leader, i.e. **potential retained-search mandates worth ~$75k each.**

You're not *claiming* competence (you can't, no case studies). You're **demonstrating** it, on their actual market, before they risk anything. That's the only thing that reliably converts cold with no proof.

---

## Why this is their pipeline (the recruiter client-side signals)

A search firm's "customer" = a company that needs to hire an executive. The buying signals the free batch hunts for:
- **HIRING** — company posted a VP/C-suite/Director req in the firm's vertical → live mandate need.
- **FUNDING** — raised a round → scaling leadership → mandate.
- **DEPARTURE** — an exec just left (LinkedIn move / news) → backfill mandate.
- **EXPANSION** — new market/office/M&A → leadership build-out.
These map 1:1 to the signal taxonomy Ryan already built — so the free batch is genuinely buildable by the existing battlecard workflow, not vapor.

> The pitch writes itself: *"You're elite at finding the candidate. I find you the companies that need one — before your competitors call them."*

## The Value Equation (why this offer is hard to say no to)

Hormozi: **Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort)**. The free-pipeline wedge maxes all four:

| Lever | This offer |
|---|---|
| **Dream outcome** ↑ | More retained mandates (~$75k each) without cold-calling companies themselves |
| **Perceived likelihood** ↑↑ | They *see real opportunities* before paying — likelihood isn't argued, it's shown |
| **Time delay** ↓ | Free batch in days; they spot a usable mandate immediately |
| **Effort/sacrifice** ↓↓ | Zero. They don't even take a call to get it — just "yes, send it" |

The free wedge is engineered specifically to crush the two denominators (time + effort) to near-zero, which is what makes a *cold* yes possible.

## Why free-pipeline beats the alternatives
- **vs "free audit"** — an audit is *advice* (low perceived value, overdone, feels like a sales trap). A pipeline batch is *the actual outcome they want*, delivered. Show, don't tell.
- **vs "book a demo"** — a demo asks them to spend time on faith. The wedge spends *Ryan's* time first. Reciprocity + proof in one move.
- **vs leading with price/tiers** — pricing in a cold DM kills it. Price only comes up *after* the free batch has proven value and they've asked "how does this work ongoing?"

## Cost of Acquisition (the model you asked for)

> All conversion rates are **ESTIMATES**, flagged. Real numbers come from the first ~140 sends — which is exactly what the engine's weekly loop is built to learn. Treat this as the planning model, not a promise.

### What one free build costs
- **Ryan's time:** ~45–60 min/batch (the battlecard workflow does the heavy lifting; Ryan reviews + personalizes the openers + records nothing yet). Call it **~1 hr.**
- **Hard cost:** ~200–400k tokens/batch ≈ **low single-digit dollars** of API. AI Arc + LinkedIn already paid for.
- **The real constraint is TIME, not money.** ~5–8 free builds/week is the realistic ceiling solo → hence the scarcity cap.

### The funnel (cold LinkedIn, estimates flagged)
| Stage | Est. rate | From 100 connection requests |
|---|---|---|
| CR → accept (recruiters are LinkedIn-native) | ~35% | 35 |
| Accept → "yes, send the free batch" (low-friction give) | ~20% | 7 |
| Free batch → meeting (if the batch is genuinely good) | ~40% | ~3 |
| Meeting → paid pilot (cold, no case study) | ~25% | ~0.7 |

→ **~1 pilot per ~140 connection requests / ~8–10 free builds.**

### CAC vs LTV
- **CAC ≈ ~10 hours of Ryan's time + ~$10–30 hard cost** per first client. (Outreach itself rides the daily LinkedIn rhythm — not extra hours.)
- **LTV ≈ $23k** (Growth $3.5k × ~6-mo early retention + $2.5k setup); floor ~$9.5k at Starter.
- **CAC:LTV is wildly favorable** even if conversion runs 3× worse than estimated. **Money is not the constraint — Ryan's build-time is.**

### What the CAC model implies (the strategy, not just the number)
1. **Qualify hard before you build.** A free build on a wrong-ICP firm is the only real waste. Confirm: retained/specialized perm, 5–50 ppl, MP reachable, fee ≥ ~$25k. No fit → no free build.
2. **Cap free builds (~5–8/wk).** Protects the constraint *and* powers the scarcity line honestly.
3. **The free build IS the sales asset** — it's also the artifact you show the *next* prospect (anonymized) and the seed of your first case study. One build, three uses.
4. **CAC drops off a cliff after pilot #1.** Once you have one real result, you can sell on the case study and stop giving free builds to everyone → time-CAC collapses. The free wedge is a *cold-start bridge*, not forever.

## The cold outreach sequence (verbatim — give-first, in Ryan's voice)

> Rules: peer-to-peer MP-to-MP tone. No pitch in the connect. The "ask" is always to *give* (send the free batch), never to take a meeting — until after value lands. Sell **client-side BD**, never candidate sourcing. No AI-slop, no guarantees.

### 1. LinkedIn connection request (no pitch)
```
[Name] — I work with boutique search firms on the client-acquisition side (not candidates). Been studying how the market flipped this year — figured it was worth connecting with someone running [Firm].
```

### 2. LinkedIn DM (after accept — the give)
```
Appreciate the connect, [Name]. Quick one, no pitch.

You're elite at finding the candidate. The part I work on is the other side — finding the companies that need one before your competitors call them.

I can put together a free sample for [Firm]: ~7 companies in [vertical] showing executive-hiring signals right now — the decision-maker, why now, and an opener you could actually use. Yours whether or not we ever talk.

Want me to send it over? Only catch is I build these by hand so I do about 5 a week.
```

### 3. Follow-up 1 (no reply, ~3 days — add a proof crumb)

```
[Name] — no worries if the timing's off. One example of what I mean: [Company] just [raised $X / posted a VP role / lost their CRO]. That's a search mandate sitting there right now. The free batch is 7 of those, tailored to what you place. Want it?
```

### 4. Follow-up 2 (~5 days later — the give, restated, then rest)

```
Last one from me, [Name]. Happy to just send the batch over with no expectation — if it's useful, great; if not, no harm. Want me to drop it here?
```

## Guardrails / what NOT to do
- ❌ **Don't free-build for non-ICP firms.** The only real CAC waste. Qualify first.
- ❌ **Don't give away the ongoing engine** — one hand-built batch ≠ the weekly loop. The consistency, the approval workflow, and the time it takes are what they pay for. One sample creates desire; it doesn't satisfy the need.
- ❌ **Don't pitch candidate sourcing.** Client-side BD only — it's the gap; candidates are their strength.
- ❌ **Don't lead with price or "book a demo"** in cold. Give first; price after value + their "how does this work ongoing?"
- ❌ **Don't guarantee mandates/placements/revenue.** Guarantee the work. (Their conversion depends on their own sales calls — say so; it builds trust.)
- ❌ **Don't fake scarcity.** The ~5/week cap is real — use the true reason.
