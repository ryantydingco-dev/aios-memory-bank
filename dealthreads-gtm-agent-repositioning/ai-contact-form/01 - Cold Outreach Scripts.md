# Cold Outreach Scripts — Free Contact-Form Teardown

> Verbatim, copy-paste cold outreach to offer the **free contact-form teardown** to GTM leaders at the 13 verified targets (IdeaBrowser #7856 / `17`). The teardown IS the demo: we fill out the prospect's own form, enrich that lead, and show the gap — "here's the name+email your form captured vs. the full buyer profile my system built from it." The CTA in every cold touch is **"want the teardown?"** — never "book a call." No pricing in cold. Give-first. Operator voice.
>
> **How to use:** Section 1 = the reusable sequence (connection request → DM → email → 2 follow-ups → "yes, send it" delivery + book-a-call). Section 2 = the per-company **opener line** (already written, verified) you drop into the `[PROOF LINE]` slot. Section 3 = the drift_refugee timing overlay. Replace every `[bracket]`. Human reviews before anything sends.

---

## The rules (don't break these)

- **The ask is always "want the teardown?"** Not a meeting, not a demo, not "15 minutes." The teardown is free, costs them nothing, and demos the product by running it on them. The call only gets offered *after* the teardown lands.
- **No pricing in any cold touch.** Price comes up after value lands and they ask "how does this work / what's it cost." If they ask early, see the objection line in Section 4.
- **Async framing.** "Your rep gets the dossier before the callback" — minutes, not seconds. Never promise live in-chat enrichment (that's v2).
- **Real data only.** Every proof line below is built on what's verifiable about that company. Where a fact is uncertain it's marked. Honest gaps are a feature — they prove we don't bullshit. Do not embellish.
- **Operator voice.** Specific, practical, revenue-focused. Banned words: unlock, revolutionize, supercharge, leverage, transform, "AI-powered," "game-changer." Write like one GTM operator talking to another.
- **No guarantees.** Never promise revenue, pipeline, or meetings. We guarantee the work (the teardown), nothing we don't control.

# SECTION 1 — The reusable sequence (every target gets this shape)

The 6 beats:
1. **LinkedIn connection request** — name what they do, no pitch.
2. **LinkedIn DM** (on accept) — the give: free teardown of their own form.
3. **Cold email** (subject + body) — same give, for when LinkedIn doesn't connect or you have the email.
4. **Follow-up #1** (~3 days) — a concrete crumb of what their form is throwing away.
5. **Follow-up #2** (~5 days) — restate the give, then rest.
6. **"Yes, send it"** → deliver the teardown + soft book-a-call.

> Drop the company-specific `[PROOF LINE]` from Section 2 into the marked slot. Everything else is shared.

---

## 1. LinkedIn connection request (no pitch)

```
[Name] — I work with [B2B SaaS / fintech / security / logistics-software / etc.] GTM teams on what happens between a form fill and the rep's callback. Figured it was worth connecting with whoever owns that at [Company].
```

*Short. No ask. Establishes you work on their exact seam (form → callback), not "AI" in the abstract.*

---

## 2. LinkedIn DM (on accept — the give)

```
Appreciate the connect, [Name]. Quick one, no pitch.

When someone fills out [Company]'s [demo / contact / quote] form, your rep gets a name and an email — then spends ~20 min rebuilding who that company actually is before they call back. Company size, funding, the stack they run, who else needs to be on the call.

I can show you exactly what that looks like for [Company], for free. I fill out your own form, then build the buyer profile a rep *could* have had before the callback — and send you a short walkthrough of the gap. What your form caught vs. what was actually sittin' there.

No call, no deck. Want me to run it on [Company]'s form?
```

*Why it lands: it's their own form, their own gap, and the proof shows up before they spend a minute. The "yes" is to receive something free, not to sit through a pitch.*

---

## 3. Cold email (subject + body)

**Subject options (pick one — plain, lowercase, operator-style):**
```
what [Company]'s demo form throws away
the 20 minutes after every [Company] form fill
your form gives the rep a name + email. that's it.
```

**Body:**
```
[Name] —

Someone fills out [Company]'s [demo/contact/quote] form. Your rep gets a name and a work email — then burns ~20 minutes rebuilding the rest before the callback: company size, funding stage, the stack they already run, who else has to be in the room.

[PROOF LINE]

I'll show you the gap for free. I fill out your own form, then build the buyer profile your rep could've opened before dialing — and send you a 5-minute walkthrough of what your form caught vs. what was actually there.

No call required. Want me to run the teardown on [Company]?

[Ryan]
```

*The `[PROOF LINE]` (Section 2) is the company-specific proof that this isn't a mail-merge. It's the one line that makes them think "this person actually looked at us."*

---

## 4. Follow-up #1 (~3 days, no reply — show, don't ask again)

```
[Name] — no worries if it's not the right week.

To make it concrete: I took 30 seconds and put your own [demo/contact] form through it. Name + email is all it hands the rep. The teardown I'm offering fills in [headcount / funding / the security or ERP stack / the likely deal size / who else signs] — the stuff your reps go dig out by hand on every inbound.

Still free, still no call. Want it?
```

*This is the half-proof. You're demonstrating the gap on their actual form without giving away the full build. Creates the desire the teardown then satisfies.*

---

## 5. Follow-up #2 (~5 days later — the give, restated, then rest)

```
Last one from me, [Name]. Happy to just build the teardown and drop it here — if it's useful, great; if not, no harm done. Want me to send it over?
```

*Low-ego, give-first close. Leaves the door open without chasing.*

---

## 6. "Yes, send it" → delivery + book-a-call

**When they say yes, deliver the teardown, then this:**
```
Here you go, [Name] — [link to the teardown walkthrough / dossier].

Quick tour of what's in it: I filled out [Company]'s own [demo/contact] form, then built the buyer profile a rep could've had before the callback — [headcount, funding, the stack, the likely deal size, the second decision-maker]. The left side is what your form captured. The right side is what my system pulled from that same submission.

This is the exact thing we'd run live on your site — every inbound lead gets this profile pushed into [their CRM, e.g. HubSpot/Salesforce] before your rep calls back, instead of the rep rebuilding it by hand.

If it's worth 15 min to see how that'd run on [Company]'s real inbound, I'll walk you through it — [scheduling link or "what's a good day this week?"]. Either way the teardown's yours to keep.
```

*The meeting ask only appears HERE, after value landed — and it's framed as "see how the thing you just watched would run live," which is their natural next question. The teardown is theirs regardless, which keeps it a gift, not bait.*

# SECTION 4 — Handling the two most common cold replies

> Keep these short. The goal is always to route back to "let me just send the teardown."

### "What does it cost?" (price asked before value landed)
```
Depends on the setup, and honestly the number's a distraction until you've seen whether the thing's any good. Let me send the free teardown first — it's the exact product run on your own form. If it's worth it, the pricing conversation is easy. If it's not, you've lost nothing. Want it?
```
*Never quote $2,500 in a cold thread. The teardown sells the price; the price doesn't sell the teardown.*

### "Is this just [Clay / Apollo / ZoomInfo / our existing enrichment]?"
```
Fair question. Those are great if you've got an ops person to wire them up and run them. The thing I do is the done-for-you version — I install it on your form and push the finished buyer profile into [their CRM] before the rep calls back, so nobody on your team is stitching tools together or running it. Easiest way to see the difference is the teardown — want me to run it on [Company]?
```
*Honest: we're not claiming a better dataset; we're the managed install for teams with thin RevOps. That's the actual wedge.*

### "We don't really do chat / our buyers want a fast form"
```
Totally — I'm not here to force a chat widget on you. You keep the form your buyers like; the enrichment happens behind it, so the rep still opens a full profile before the callback. The teardown shows exactly that on your current form. Want me to send it?
```
*Defuses the chat-only objection flagged in the research. Sell the enrichment outcome, not the UI.*
