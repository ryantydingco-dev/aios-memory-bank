- 0-8: dead form
- 9-15: basic intake
- 16-21: useful form

## Page 2 — section 1: buyer context

# Section 1: buyer context

Question:

> Does your form tell sales who the person is and why they matter?

Score 1 point for each yes.

□ Captures job title or role  
□ Captures seniority or department  
□ Identifies whether they are buyer, evaluator, user, partner, or vendor  
□ Captures LinkedIn or enough data to find the person quickly  
□ Helps the rep know how to address them on the first reply/call  

Score: ___ / 5

## Why this matters

A VP Sales, founder, intern, consultant, and student can all submit the same form.

If they all hit the CRM looking identical, your reps have to guess priority.

That is not lead routing.

That is astrology with a CRM subscription.

## What good looks like

A useful buyer profile should tell the rep:

- who this person is
- whether they are likely a decision maker
- what role they play in the deal
- what kind of language to use in follow-up
- what question to ask first

## Quick fix if you scored low

Add one light qualifying question:

> What best describes your role in evaluating this?

Options:

- I am the founder/executive sponsor
- I own the team/process
- I am researching options
- I am a user/practitioner
- Other

Do not add 12 fields. That is how forms become tiny DMV offices.

## Page 3 — section 2: company context

# Section 2: company context

Question:

> Does your form tell sales whether the company is a real fit?

Score 1 point for each yes.

□ Captures company website/domain  
□ Captures company size or employee range  
□ Captures industry or segment  
□ Captures CRM/account match or existing customer/prospect status  
□ Enriches company data before sales follow-up  

Score: ___ / 5

## Why this matters

Company context changes the callback.

A 12-person vertical SaaS company and a 600-person enterprise do not need the same conversation.

A funded startup and a bootstrapped services firm may both be good leads, but the pain and urgency are different.

## What good looks like

Before callback, your rep should know:

- what the company does
- size range
- industry
- likely business model
- whether they match ICP
- any obvious growth or buying signals

## Quick fix if you scored low

At minimum, require business email and company website.

Then enrich behind the scenes.

Do not make the buyer type what the internet can tell you.

## Page 4 — section 3: intent and urgency

# Section 3: intent and urgency

Question:

> Does your form tell sales why this buyer is here now?

Score 1 point for each yes.

□ Captures their main problem or use case  
□ Captures timeline or urgency  
□ Captures current solution/workaround  
□ Captures budget range or buying stage, when appropriate  
□ Captures what triggered the search/demo request  

Score: ___ / 5

## Why this matters

Intent is the difference between:

> “We are browsing.”

and:

> “Our current process is breaking and we need to fix it this quarter.”

Most forms do not capture that.

They collect a message box and pray the buyer writes something useful.

Prayer is not a qualification strategy.

## What good looks like

Your rep should know:

- what problem made the buyer reach out
- whether they are researching or ready to act
- what they use today
- what happens if they do nothing
- what first question would move the conversation forward

## Quick fix if you scored low

Replace the vague “How can we help?” box with:

> What made you reach out today?

Options:

- We are evaluating options now
- Our current process/tool is not working
- We are planning a change this quarter
- I am researching for later
- Other

Then include an optional free-text box.

## Page 5 — section 4: CRM handoff

# Section 4: CRM handoff

Question:

> Does your CRM receive a useful record or just a receipt?

Score 1 point for each yes.

□ Form submission creates/updates the right contact record  
□ Company/account is created or matched correctly  
□ Source/page/campaign data is preserved  
□ Qualification fields are mapped into useful CRM fields  
□ Sales owner or next action is assigned automatically  

Score: ___ / 5

## Why this matters

A form submission is not the finish line.

The handoff matters.

If the CRM record is incomplete, assigned wrong, or missing context, the rep starts from scratch.

That is how “inbound” becomes “maybe someone will look at it later.”

## What good looks like

Your CRM should show:

- contact
- company/account
- source page
- campaign/source
- role
- intent
- fit score or priority
- rep owner
- recommended next action

## Quick fix if you scored low

Audit your last 10 form submissions.

For each one, ask:

- Was the company matched correctly?
- Did sales know why they reached out?
- Did anyone know what to do next?
- Did the right person get notified?

If the answer is mostly “kind of,” congratulations. You found the gremlin.

## Page 6 — section 5: rep follow-up readiness

# Section 5: rep follow-up readiness

Question:

> Could a rep follow up intelligently within five minutes?

Score 1 point for each yes.

□ Rep knows who the buyer is  
□ Rep knows what the company does  
□ Rep knows likely fit/priority  
□ Rep has a recommended first question  
□ Rep can personalize without opening five tabs  

Score: ___ / 5

## Why this matters

Speed-to-lead is good.

Speed-to-lead while clueless is just fast confusion.

The best handoff gives reps enough context to say something specific on the first touch.

Not creepy.

Not over-automated.

Just useful.

## What good looks like

A call-prep brief could say:

> Sarah is VP Ops at a 60-person logistics software company. She came from the pricing page and said they are replacing a manual intake process this quarter. Company appears to be founder-led and hiring sales roles. Suggested first question: “What is breaking in the current intake process that made this a priority now?”

That is a very different callback than:

> “Hey, saw you filled out our form.”

The second one is legally a sentence, but emotionally a wet napkin.

Your form is better than most. The next opportunity is turning useful intake into a complete buyer profile.

- data exists but is not packaged for rep follow-up

Next step:

### 22-25: Buyer-profile engine

Your form already acts like a front door for sales intelligence.

You may not need Dealthreads right now.
