# Dealthreads AI contact form — user persona system

Created: 2026-06-01

Brand note: the prompt uses Replede, but the current workspace direction is Dealthreads as the customer-facing brand. These personas are written for Dealthreads AI Contact Form / buyer-profile builder. If Replede becomes the final brand, swap the name only. The personas stay the same.

## Business context

Product: AI-driven contact form that pre-builds the buyer profile.

Core promise:

> Turn a thin demo/contact form submission into a full buyer profile in the CRM before the sales callback.

Market type: B2B managed SaaS/service hybrid.

Business model:

- Beta/install offer: setup fee or free/low-friction install depending on GTM test.
- Core service: ~$2,500/month for widget, enrichment, CRM sync, routing, and maintenance.
- Premium: ~$5,000/month with ICP campaigns and monthly intelligence reports.

Primary ICP:

- small to mid-market B2B companies
- high-value deals, roughly $8k+ or $3k+/mo retainers
- founder-led or small sales team
- real demo/contact-sales form
- no mature RevOps team
- manual research happens after inbound submits

Main buying trigger:

> “We’re getting inbound, but our follow-up is slower and dumber than it should be.”

## Research basis and assumptions

This persona system is based on:

- provided market research showing high pain around slow lead response, poor lead data, CRM inefficiency, and fragmented tools
- workspace positioning around founder-led B2B, vertical SaaS, freight/logistics software, and B2B services
- Dealthreads current offer docs around AI contact form, buyer profiles, free form teardowns, and managed install
- competitive context: Drift shifting/disappearing, Qualified moving enterprise, Intercom/Chili Piper/Clearbit handling pieces but not the managed mid-market wedge

Still unvalidated:

- exact buyer language from live interviews
- willingness to pay by segment
- whether RevOps or founder is the better first economic buyer
- which enrichment fields actually predict higher close rate
- whether buyers prefer “AI contact form,” “buyer-profile builder,” or another category phrase

Use these personas as working hypotheses. Update them after 10-15 teardown calls.

# 1. Primary persona

## Marcus Reed — the founder-led B2B operator

*“Every demo request should be treated like it might be real money. Right now they all hit my inbox looking exactly the same.”*

### Quick stats

- Age: 32-48
- Location: US/Canada, often secondary startup hubs or vertical-industry hubs
- Company: 8-45 employees
- Role: founder, CEO, co-founder, president, or operator-owner
- Revenue stage: $500k-$5M ARR/revenue, sometimes higher if niche vertical B2B
- Deal size: $8k-$75k ACV or project value
- Sales maturity: founder-led sales, maybe 1-3 reps, no real RevOps
- Tools: HubSpot, Pipedrive, Salesforce starter, Webflow/WordPress, Calendly, Apollo/Clay trial graveyard, maybe Intercom/Drift/HubSpot chat installed badly

### Background

Marcus runs a B2B company that is past the “can we sell this?” stage but not mature enough to have clean GTM operations. He has customers, a real offer, and a sales motion. He also has chaos.

Inbound comes from referrals, LinkedIn, paid traffic, partner pages, SEO, or industry content. The website has a “Request Demo,” “Contact Sales,” or “Talk to Us” form. The form technically works, but it gives the team only the basics: name, email, company, maybe message.

After that, someone has to research the lead manually.

Usually Marcus.

Or a rep who does it inconsistently.

Or nobody, which is how good leads quietly go cold.

### Goals and motivations

Primary goals:

1. Turn more inbound inquiries into qualified conversations.
2. Respond faster without calling blind.
3. Know which leads are worth immediate attention.
4. Create a cleaner handoff from website to CRM to sales follow-up.
5. Avoid hiring an SDR, RevOps person, or sales coordinator too early.

Secondary goals:

- make the company look more sophisticated without buying enterprise software
- improve founder time allocation
- stop losing context between marketing, CRM, and sales
- learn which segments and pages produce real buyers

Success metrics:

- less manual research per lead
- faster callback time
- more qualified meetings from inbound
- better CRM completeness
- fewer “who is this person?” sales moments
- higher close rate from inbound leads over time

Aspirations:

Marcus wants the company to feel like a real GTM machine, not a founder-powered improv show. He wants inbound to be handled like a grown-up company would handle it, but without building a whole RevOps department.

### Pain points and frustrations

Daily struggles:

- demo/contact requests arrive thin and underqualified
- reps treat high-fit buyers and random tire kickers the same
- researching every lead takes 10-20 minutes
- the first callback often starts with basic discovery that could have happened earlier
- CRM fields are incomplete or messy
- marketing cannot tell which inbound is actually good
- founder or sales lead gets pulled into lead triage

Emotional frustrations:

- “We worked hard to get someone to the site and then the form gives us nothing.”
- “I know we’re probably missing good opportunities, but I can’t prove it yet.”
- “I don’t want another tool. I want the lead to show up useful.”
- “We’re too small for enterprise RevOps, but too big for random inbox chaos.”

Resource constraints:

- limited budget for full-time sales ops
- no time for a long implementation
- low patience for tools that require team behavior change
- founder is already stretched
- engineering resources are protected for core product

### Behavioral patterns

Current workaround:

- check company website manually
- search LinkedIn for the person/company
- look for size/funding/industry manually
- skim CRM history
- ask the lead repetitive questions on first call
- qualify based on gut feel
- sometimes ignore low-context submissions until later

Decision-making process:

1. Sees the teardown or hears the “dead contact form” framing.
2. Thinks of recent inbound leads that went nowhere.
3. Wants to see what Dealthreads would have produced on one real form submission.
4. Asks implementation questions: “How hard is this to install?” “Does it work with HubSpot?” “Will this hallucinate?”
5. If the demo is concrete, decides quickly. Founder-led buyers can move in days, not quarters.

Timeline:

- free teardown: same day to 1 week
- pilot decision: 1-3 weeks
- paid install: 2-4 weeks if pain is real and integration feels safe

Information sources:

- LinkedIn founder/operator content
- peer referrals
- founder communities
- RevOps/HubSpot consultants
- niche podcasts/newsletters
- YouTube walkthroughs if practical and proof-based

Technology adoption:

Marcus will adopt tools if they are concrete, fast, and tied to money. He is skeptical of broad AI claims, but interested in workflow-specific AI that removes a real manual step.

He does not want a platform tour. He wants to see his own form improved.

### Objections and barriers

Likely objections:

- “We don’t get enough inbound yet.”
- “Our form works fine.”
- “Can’t HubSpot already do this?”
- “Will this annoy buyers?”
- “Is the data accurate?”
- “I don’t want AI making things up in our CRM.”
- “We’re too early for this.”
- “I don’t want another chatbot on my website.”
- “What happens if the enrichment is wrong?”

Budget concerns:

Marcus may compare $2,500/month to software tools, not headcount. The sale needs to anchor against founder/research time, missed opportunities, and the cost of hiring a coordinator/RevOps help.

Authority:

High. Marcus can approve the pilot personally.

Risk aversion:

Moderate. He will take a bet if:

- install feels reversible
- unknown fields are not faked
- CRM sync is controlled
- there is a manual beta/service layer
- Dealthreads shows a finished teardown first

### Communication preferences

Preferred channels:

- LinkedIn DM after useful comment/content
- Loom teardown
- email if sent with a concrete artifact
- direct founder-to-founder call

Preferred content formats:

- 2-minute teardown video
- before/after screenshot
- short LinkedIn posts
- one-page PDF
- sample buyer dossier
- simple calculator showing manual research cost

Tone that resonates:

- direct
- practical
- low-jargon
- founder/operator language
- “I found this leak on your site” rather than “we transform your GTM”

Messaging that lands:

- “Your form got the lead. Your rep still got homework.”
- “You do not need enterprise RevOps to stop calling leads blind.”
- “I’ll show what your form is not telling your rep.”
- “Unknown fields should say unknown. We don’t fake enrichment.”

Messaging to avoid:

- “revolutionary AI-powered lead engagement solution”
- “replace your sales team”
- “guaranteed more revenue”
- “fully autonomous inbound agent”

# 3. Anti-personas

## 1. The no-inbound tiny business

Characteristics:

- under 5 employees
- no meaningful website traffic
- no demo/contact-sales flow
- low ACV or transactional sales
- founder wants AI because it sounds cool

Why poor fit:

There is not enough inbound volume or deal value to justify the install. They need traffic, offer clarity, or basic sales process first.

How to identify:

- no clear CTA on site
- low-value service/product
- no CRM
- no one owns follow-up
- cannot name recent inbound opportunities

Avoid by asking:

- “How many demo/contact requests do you get per month?”
- “What is an average deal worth?”
- “Who follows up today?”

## 2. The enterprise committee maze

Characteristics:

- 500+ employees
- mature RevOps, procurement, legal/security review
- Salesforce heavily customized
- existing Drift/Qualified/6sense/Demandbase stack
- long sales cycle for even small pilots

Why poor fit:

Too slow for early GTM. They may be valuable later, but early Dealthreads needs fast learning, not six-month vendor reviews.

How to identify:

- asks for SOC 2/security docs immediately
- requires procurement portal
- multi-stakeholder evaluation before teardown
- has dedicated RevOps/platform team

Avoid by saying:

- “We’re focused on managed beta installs for founder-led and lean GTM teams first.”

## 3. The AI novelty buyer

Characteristics:

- wants “an AI chatbot” but cannot define business outcome
- asks about autonomous agents more than lead workflow
- no clear CRM process
- no buyer-profile use case
- gets excited but does not move

Why poor fit:

They consume time and create scope creep. They buy novelty, not workflow ROI.

How to identify:

- says “Can it do everything?”
- asks for custom AI features unrelated to inbound follow-up
- cannot describe current lead process
- has no success metric

Avoid by anchoring:

- “The first version only does one job: turn a form submit into a buyer profile before callback.”

## 4. The tool-hoarding RevOps tinkerer

Characteristics:

- already uses Clay, Clearbit, Apollo, custom Zapier, enrichment APIs
- likes building internally
- low urgency to buy managed service
- may copy the idea instead of buying

Why poor fit:

They are useful for research, but often not the buyer. They may see Dealthreads as something they can recreate.

How to identify:

- asks mostly implementation questions
- compares API vendors quickly
- says “we could probably build this”
- no budget owner involved

Handle carefully:

- sell managed speed, source trust, and outcome
- do not over-share the whole build stack before qualification

# 5. Implementation guide

## Product decisions

Use Marcus as the first design anchor.

Product should prioritize:

1. fast install
2. clear before/after profile
3. HubSpot-first CRM push
4. source/unknown transparency
5. minimal dashboard complexity
6. rep-readable call-prep summary

Do not overbuild:

- multi-tenant admin panels
- enterprise role permissions
- complex routing logic
- advanced analytics before beta proof
- autonomous follow-up agents

The first product experience should feel like:

> “I submitted the form. Two minutes later, the CRM has a useful buyer profile.”

## Copy decisions

Lead with Marcus language:

- “Stop calling inbound leads blind.”
- “Your form got the lead. Your rep still got homework.”
- “Turn every demo request into a buyer profile before callback.”
- “The 20-minute research gap is where good leads cool off.”

Use Nina language lower on the page:

- source-backed fields
- CRM mapping
- routing controls
- captured vs enriched vs inferred

Use Elena language for content/ads:

- shorter form, richer profile
- campaign quality
- better handoff to sales

Use Derek language in sales calls:

- rep prep time
- call quality
- prioritization
- lead response SLAs

## Design decisions

The page and demo should show the contrast visually:

Left side:

- name
- email
- company
- vague message

Right side:

- company profile
- buyer role
- fit score
- intent/budget/timeline
- relevant signals
- CRM next action
- recommended first question

Design principle:

> Make the buyer profile feel immediately more useful than the form submission.

Avoid abstract dashboards. Show the rep’s actual moment.

- Demand Gen / Growth
- company size 11-50 or 51-200

Best trigger signals:

- hiring first SDR/AE/Growth role

- recently funded or launched
- active LinkedIn founder content

## Sales conversation starters

For Marcus:

“Looked at your demo form. It captures the basics, but your rep still has to research company fit, buyer role, and likely urgency after the submit. Want me to show what a buyer profile could look like before the callback?”

For Nina:

“I’m not trying to replace your CRM. I’m looking at the gap before the record becomes useful: captured fields, enrichment, source confidence, and routing context. Want me to map what your current form sends into the CRM vs. what reps probably need?”

For Elena:

“Your form is probably optimized for conversion, which is good. The leak is what happens after conversion. I can show how to keep the form lightweight while still giving sales a richer profile.”

For Derek:

“Your reps can move faster when they know who is worth calling first and what to say first. I can show what a call-prep brief would look like from your current form.”

## Customer support approach

For early beta clients:

- weekly 20-minute check-in
- review completed profiles
- ask which fields reps used
- ask which fields were ignored
- track unknown/incorrect data
- refine conversation flow
- refine CRM field mapping

Key support questions:

1. Did the profile change how the rep followed up?
2. Which field was most useful?
3. Which field was missing or noisy?
4. Did the buyer experience feel natural?
5. Did any lead get prioritized differently because of the profile?
