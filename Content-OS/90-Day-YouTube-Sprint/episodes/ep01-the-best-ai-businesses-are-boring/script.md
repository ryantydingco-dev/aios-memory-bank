## WHERE THE IDEA CAME FROM  [DECK: slide 3]

So here's what kicked this off.

I spend a stupid amount of time on this tool called IdeaBrowser — it surfaces business ideas and demand data. And the pattern I kept noticing wasn't the flashy stuff. The ideas that actually had real search demand, real money, real desperate people... were boring as hell.

[OPINION — say which ones genuinely surprised you when you scrolled it]

And it clicked: the flashy AI stuff is crowded because it's fun to build. The boring stuff is wide open because nobody *wants* to build it. That gap is the opportunity.

---

## WHO HAS THIS PROBLEM & WHY IT COSTS THEM  [DECK: slide 4]

Here's who I'm talking about. Not tech startups. Real businesses:

- The plumbing company with a whiteboard for scheduling.
- The accountant re-typing numbers off PDFs into a spreadsheet, by hand, in 2026.
- The clinic where someone spends two hours a day chasing unpaid invoices over email.

[DECK: slide 5 — the cost]

And here's the thing — these aren't small annoyances. That's payroll. That's a human being paid $25 an hour to copy-paste. Multiply it across a year and these "boring" problems are quietly eating tens of thousands of dollars per business.

They're not gonna build an AI to fix it. They don't know how. They don't have time. **That's where you come in.**

---

## THE MANUAL WORKFLOW TODAY  [DECK: slide 6]

Let me show you what one of these actually looks like, because this is the whole game.

Take invoice chasing. Today the workflow is: invoice goes out → nobody pays → it sits → eventually someone remembers → they dig through email to find who owes what → they write an awkward "just following up!" email → they copy the next one → repeat forever.

[DECK: slide 7 — the leak]

Every one of those arrows is a place where time leaks and money gets forgotten. That mess — that ugly little chain of manual steps — *is* the product. If you can collapse that chain, you have something a business will pay for.

[OPINION — point at the deck, say which step you think is the most painful and why]

## THE TINY AI BUILD  [DECK: slide 8 — the scorecard]

Okay so how do I decide which boring problem is actually worth it? I score it. Five questions, one point each:

1. **Is the pain specific?** Vague pain = no sale.
2. **Is the workflow messy?** Messy = automatable = valuable.
3. **Does the buyer have money?** A business with revenue beats a broke side-hustler.
4. **Can I show proof in under 90 minutes?** If I can't build a quick demo, I can't sell it.
5. **Can someone pay me this month?** Short-term cash beats someday-maybe.

[DECK: slide 9 — score the invoice example live]

Let's run the invoice-chasing one right now. Specific pain? Yes — point. Messy workflow? Extremely — point. Buyer with money? Any business that invoices — point. Provable fast? I can mock up a follow-up dashboard today — point. Pay me this month? A $500 workflow audit, easy — point.

Five out of five. That's a green light. [OPINION — say whether you'd actually take this one on]

---

## THE BUSINESS VERDICT  [DECK: slide 10]

So what do you actually *do* with a green-light boring problem? Three ways to make money off the same insight:

- **Service:** "I'll map and automate this one workflow for you." Fastest cash.
- **Product/SaaS:** turn the fix into a tiny tool other businesses buy. Slower, scales.
- **Content:** make the video — like this one — and let the right person find you.

[OPINION — say which one you're personally chasing first and why]

My honest verdict on "boring AI businesses" as a whole: it's the least sexy and most reliable money in AI right now. I'd bet on boring every time.
