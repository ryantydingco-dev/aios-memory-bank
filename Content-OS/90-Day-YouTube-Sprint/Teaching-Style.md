# Teaching Style — The House Style for Every Video

Source: Omar El Attkoury, *"How To Become A Millionaire in 2026 (Start Teaching)."*
This is the standard for **all** Ryan's content — YouTube, Shorts, LinkedIn, X. Every script in this Content-OS must pass it.

---

## The one belief that changes everything

> **Teaching is not transferring information. It's creating the feeling that makes someone take the next step.**

If a viewer finishes overwhelmed, you failed — "an overwhelmed person never takes action." If they finish *feeling like it's possible, easy, and that they trust you*, you win. That feeling is the product.

---

## The 7 rules (every video obeys these)

### 1. ONE idea per video
Boil it to one. Resist covering everything you know. If you can't say the video in a single sentence, it's two videos.

### 2. Don't over-teach (≠ don't over-deliver)
Over-delivering = the viewer feels they got a ton of value. Over-teaching = you fire-hose info and they drown. You want the first, never the second. Cut the jargon, the weeds, the "you could do this, or this, or this."

### 3. Shift a belief — name it before you write
Your job: the viewer thinks about the thing *differently* after you than before. Before writing, answer: **"What do they currently believe wrong, and what should they believe instead?"** The whole video bends toward that shift. The three beliefs you're always installing: **it's possible · it's easy · I can trust this guy.**

### 4. Teach WHAT / WHEN / WHY-NOT — not HOW
This is the counterintuitive one. **Don't deliver step-by-step how-to.** Teach *what* matters, *what not* to do, *why now*, *when not* to bother. The moment you go full tutorial, you lose people — and you give away the thing they'd pay you for. You can *title* it "How to X." You just don't *deliver* the how. **The how lives behind the offer.**

### 5. Make it feel EASY
When you make it look easy, the viewer subconsciously concludes "it'll be easy if I work with him." Show the proof artifact as evidence — "look, it already exists, it's not scary." Easy = the strongest belief shift you can install.

### 6. Stamp your frameworks
Give your process a name. Named IP makes people feel smart for learning it and makes you the authority who owns it. (Dave Ramsey's Baby Steps, the BRRRR method, Omar's ALVIS.) Use Ryan's named method (below) in every relevant video.

### 7. The CTA is teaching, framed as service
The next step is part of the lesson, not a bolt-on ad. "Make videos that add value, not videos that are ads." Done right, people *thank you* for selling to them, because you made them feel hope. Put the invitation where it's natural, not just buried at the end.

## Ryan's signature method

**The AI Operator Install** - 4 steps:

1. **Leak** - find the revenue or operations leak inside the business.
2. **Map** - show the current workflow in one privacy-safe picture.
3. **Install** - build a small AI-assisted artifact that makes the fix feel easy and possible.
4. **Verdict** - make the honest business call: what changed, what is still unproven, and what gets built next.

> The video creates trust by showing the install. The future offer, if any, helps people apply the same process to their own business.

## Show, don't slide — the screen-share layer (Nate Herk)

Source: Nate Herk, *"I Turned Claude Fable Into The Ultimate Second Brain."*

Slides that just restate points aren't teaching — they're presenting. **The teaching happens when you do the thing on screen.** Nate opens *on the artifact*, tours the real system, plays real outputs, shows real numbers. That's what makes it land and what makes people trust you.

**The format: frame on slides, teach on screen, route the full how to the offer.**
- **Slides = the spine** (~5-6, not 15): hook, the ONE idea, named framework, verdict, CTA.
- **Screen-share = the teaching/proof:** actually do it live — pull up a real example, run the real check, show the AI doing the work, show before/after.
- **Cut between them:** slide sets up → "let me just show you" → screen-share → back to a slide for the takeaway.

**This IS Omar's model, done live.** Nate shows *enough* to make it feel real/possible/easy, but says out loud that the full step-by-step is in his course/community/repo. Screen-share = the "proof" beat. The complete how still lives behind the offer.

**Your critical adaptation:** Nate's audience wants his terminal. Yours doesn't. Screen-share the **result and the doing from the customer's POV** — the Google profile, the dashboard, the email the AI wrote, the before/after. **Never raw code.** Outcomes sell non-technical owners; code loses them.

**Steal these Nate devices:**
- **Open on the artifact** — show the end result in the first 10 seconds, then back into the story.
- **Gut-check questions** — "If you Googled your own business right now, would you show up above your competitor?" Makes the viewer self-diagnose = feeling.
- **One named framework as the spine** of the whole video (the AI Operator Install).
- **A real story/lesson, including a screwup** — Nate's accidental 150k-email blast taught "keys, not prompts." Vulnerable + concrete = memorable + trust.
- **Show real numbers/before-after on screen** — proof beats claims.

## The video structure (hybrid: slides frame, screen teaches)

1. **Cold open — on the artifact or the belief shift.** Either open ON your screen showing the end result ("watch what this did"), or open with the surprising claim/feeling. Never "Today I'll teach you…"
2. **[SLIDE] Name the ONE idea** in a sentence + the named framework.
3. **[SLIDE] Their wrong belief** — what they think/do wrong. Show you know their problem better than they do. Drop a gut-check question.
4. **[🖥️ SCREEN-SHARE] The demonstration** — *do the thing live.* This is the proof + the teaching + "feels easy." Real example, customer-POV result, AI doing the work, before/after. Show enough to make it real; don't turn it into a full tutorial.
5. **[SLIDE or over-screen] What / why / when-not** + framework recap. The verdict-level insight, not step-by-step.
6. **[SLIDE] Create the feeling to take the next step** — the offer as service. The full how = the free deliverable / audit / build. They thank you for it.

Keep it **raw**. Unedited, straight-to-camera + screen is a *feature* — honest, "I could do this too." (Both Omar and Nate shoot this way. It's also the cure for your over-engineering blind spot: ship the rep.)

## Before / After (so the difference is concrete)

**Over-teaching:** "Here are 17 ways AI can help a promotional products business: lead gen, CRM, email, quoting, invoices, vendor research, campaign ideas, customer service, reporting..."
→ Viewer is overwhelmed, screenshots it, does nothing, and forgets the channel.

**Belief-shift:** "This business does $3M and still leaks revenue because follow-up lives in memory. I would not start with a chatbot. I would start with one AI-assisted quote follow-up tracker."
→ Viewer feels *it's possible, it's easy, I trust this guy,* and wants to watch the next install.
